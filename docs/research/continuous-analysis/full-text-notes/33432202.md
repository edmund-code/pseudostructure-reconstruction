## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Data and coordinate. 10 male mice, scRNA-seq of hepatocytes (19,663 cells), each mouse sampled at ONE time point
  (ZT0 n=3, ZT6 n=2, ZT12 n=3, ZT18 n=2; Results para 1 and Methods "Hepatocytes isolation"). Each cell gets a
  layer-membership probability from a landmark-gene ratio (27 central + 28 portal landmark genes chosen to vary
  little across mice and time; Methods "Choice of landmark genes", "Reconstruction algorithm"). Layer means are
  computed per mouse, so each gene has 8 layers x 10 mice = 80 data points (Results, Fig. 1).
- Scale. The model is NOT a count model. Layer means (fraction of UMI) are log-transformed, y = log2(x + 1e-4) - B
  (Methods eq. i), and fitted with a Gaussian linear mixed model.
- Model (Methods eq. v-vii, Fig. 2b). y(x,t,i) = mu_i + mu(x) + a(x) cos(wt) + b(x) sin(wt) + noise, where mu(x), a(x),
  b(x) are Legendre polynomials of degree <= 2 in layer, w = 2pi/24h, and mu_i is a mouse random intercept. Nine fixed
  parameters at most. Fit per gene in Python StatsModels 0.9.0, Nelder-Mead, ML (not REML) so that models can be
  compared; a noise floor sigma0 = 0.15 log2 is added to sigma in the likelihood "to prevent overfitting".
- Classification. Sub-models are formed by zeroing parameters (intercept always kept; a and b paired). Five
  categories: F/N (flat or noisy), Z, R, Z+R (additive in log: shape invariant in time, magnitude rescaled), ZxR
  (any layer-dependent rhythmic parameter). Hard class = lowest BIC, but if models differ by < 1% in BIC the most
  complex is kept. Soft class probabilities = Schwarz BIC weights (Supplementary Table 2). F/N also absorbs noisy
  genes (Methods "Mixed-effect model ...").
- Gene filter before classification. Genes with high between-replicate variance (average relative replicate
  variance V_J >= 0.5) or max expression < 1e-5 are removed: 5,085 kept (Methods; the Results text says 5,058);
  4,733 removed for high variance alone. So genes that vary between mice are excluded, not modelled.
- Null / significance. No P values for the classes; classes come from BIC. Rhythmicity of bulk/proteomics data uses
  harmonic-regression F-tests; KEGG enrichment per category uses Enrichr (adjusted P < 0.1); time-window enrichment
  uses two-tailed hypergeometric tests (Fig. 6a, ED Fig. 7e).
- Replicates. Mice are the replicates through the random intercept, which "groups the dependent layer measurements
  ... and thereby properly adjusts the biological sample size for the rhythmicity analysis" (Methods). There is no
  mouse-specific random slope or shape term: layer-shape differences between mice go into the residual.
- Validation. smFISH for Bmal1, Per1 (R), Pck1, Elovl3, Arg1 (Z+R), Acly (ZxR) (Fig. 4, ED Fig. 4); agreement with
  Halpern 2017 zonation (ED Fig. 1f, centre of mass compared); agreement of phases with bulk data (ED Fig. 2e,
  circular r = 0.746); protein rhythms in bulk proteomics (ED Fig. 3). No simulation, no calibration study of the
  BIC classes.
- Results relevant to us. About 30% of profiles zonated and 20% rhythmic; 7% both; Z+R dominates, ZxR rare
  (Fig. 2d). Mid-lobular genes are rare (< 2%; Fig. 3a).
- Plots. t-SNE coloured by layer/time (Fig. 1a-e); per-gene layer profiles by time with per-mouse dots and SD bands
  (Fig. 1f-h, ED Fig. 2a-c); spatial SD vs temporal SD scatter (Fig. 2a); category counts (Fig. 2d); expression
  boxplots per category (Fig. 2e); central/mid/portal proportions (Fig. 3a); slope vs amplitude and delta-phase vs
  delta-amplitude scatters (Fig. 3c-d); polar amplitude-phase plots (Fig. 5b-d, ED Fig. 5b, 6a, 7c); KEGG table
  coloured by % central genes (Fig. 5a); time-window hypergeometric enrichment heatmap (Fig. 6a); zone-binned
  (central/mid/portal) heatmaps of Wnt targets over time (Fig. 6b). Web app czviz.epfl.ch.
- Software. Mixed models in Python StatsModels; preprocessing in R/Seurat 2.1; code and MATLAB profile files at
  github.com/naef-lab/Circadian-zonation (Data/Code availability). No package.
- Limitations the authors state: 8 layers is lower resolution than the 12-15 real layers; the variance filter
  costs sensitivity; RNA-seq vs smFISH quantitative differences; inter-animal variability.
- Limitations for OUR design: (1) Gaussian on log2 layer means, not NB counts. (2) The data unit is a per-mouse
  layer mean (80 points per gene), not cells; BIC weights from thousands of unit-level observations would be
  near 0/1 and would treat units as replicates. (3) Only a random intercept, so a position x condition interaction
  is judged against within-mouse residual noise, not mouse-to-mouse shape variation; with 2 vs 2 specimens this
  needs our relabeling calibration. (4) Spatial shapes are polynomials of degree <= 2: no sigmoid, step or
  transient-bump class. (5) Genes variable between mice were filtered out before classification.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
The best precedent for the structure of the analysis: nested models, five interpretable classes, BIC weights, one
intercept per animal, condition (time) varying between animals as AKI does for us. But it is Gaussian on log2 layer
means, its shapes are only quadratic, and it has no mouse-level shape variance. Use it on per-specimen binned
profiles and calibrate the interaction class by balanced specimen relabeling.
