## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model: expression of gene g at (x,y) = h_g(d(x,y)), with d the "isodepth" (a scalar function of tissue coordinates whose gradient field is shared by all genes: rank-one Jacobian, conservative field), and h_g piecewise linear in d with domain-specific intercepts alpha_g,p and slopes beta_g,p; domains are isodepth intervals with breakpoints shared across genes (Methods 4.1-4.2, eq. 6-8).
- Fitting: Step 1, a neural network d_theta(x,y) (two hidden layers of 20) composed with h, trained on the top 2P GLM-PCs under a Gaussian loss (30 restarts, Adam, 10,000 epochs). Step 2, breakpoints by the segmented-regression DP from Belayer. Step 3, per gene and domain Poisson regression (sklearn) with library size U_i as offset (Methods 4.3-4.4). For the colorectal tumour, mean H&E RGB values were added as features. Number of domains P by the Kneedle elbow of the likelihood (P = 7 chosen by prior knowledge for the olfactory bulb).
- Tests and calls (Methods 4.4-4.5): a Poisson log-likelihood-ratio test per gene and domain of beta = 0 (chi-square, Wilks), used to set the slope to zero (the text says "if the p-value is less than 0.1", which reads as a typo for "greater than"); no multiple-testing correction is described. "Discontinuity" and "intradomain variation" are then called by EFFECT-SIZE thresholds (magnitude above a percentile cut-off of all genes' estimated magnitudes, stated as the "tenth percentile"), not by tests. Genes need >= 500-1000 total UMIs; slopes need >= T non-zero spots in the domain.
- Cell-type attribution: cell-type-specific piecewise fits; intradomain variation "attributable" to cell type c if its slope exceeds (1-gamma) of the agnostic slope, gamma = 0.5.
- Replicates: none; one slice at a time; no calibration study of the P values.
- Validation: Slide-seqV2 cerebellum (domain coherence, F-measure vs RCTD; marker-gene AUPRC ~0.31 vs 0.07-0.25 for HotSpot, trendsceek, SpatialDE, SPARK-X, C-SIDE, SpaGCN; genes ranked by the s.d. of domain means); DLPFC ARI vs Belayer/SpaGCN/STAGATE; MERFISH cortex vs ENVI; Visium colorectal tumour; Xenium breast; Stereo-seq olfactory bulb and E9.5 embryo.
- Pattern classes: in the colorectal tumour (2 domains of interest), the three binary features intratumoral slope, boundary discontinuity, intrastromal slope give 7 patterns (Fig. 4d); over-representation of Hallmark sets per pattern by one-sided Fisher's exact test in Enrichr (Fig. 4e legend), which the text calls "GSEA"; patterns grouped into Types I-III. The text reports 1,572 SVGs, the Fig. 4 legend 986 (an inconsistency in the paper).
- Plot types: isodepth maps with contours and gradient streamlines (Figs 2a, 4c, 5b, 6b); domain maps; cell-type proportion vs isodepth (Figs 2j, 5d, 6d); pooled log-CPM vs isodepth with fitted piecewise lines (Figs 3, 4f-k); pattern-class counts and enrichment bars (Fig. 4d,e). No slope-vs-jump scatter is shown.
- Software: Python, github.com/raphael-group/GASTON; tutorials gaston.readthedocs.io.
- Limitations (authors): one shared gradient field for all genes; conservative field assumption; piecewise linear only; cannot fully resolve causes of intradomain variation. For us: isodepth is learned from tissue x/y (excluded for our reconstruction); Poisson, no overdispersion; breakpoints learned, not fixed at labels; no replicate model; jump calls are thresholds, not tests.

## Fit to our design
Medium as a model template, low as a tool. The piecewise-linear "slope within a segment vs jump at a boundary" decomposition maps directly onto S1/S2/S3 on our pseudospace. We must add what GASTON lacks: NB, fixed knots at the label transitions (or free knots with uncertainty), a real test for the jump, specimen terms, and calibration.
