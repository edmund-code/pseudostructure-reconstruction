## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: gene-by-gene version of the Agniel & Hejblum variance-component score test. Working linear model on normalized expression (default log2 CPM) with covariates X and variables of interest Phi; for grouped/longitudinal data, an individual-level random effect of Phi is added (Supp. 3.2). H0: beta = 0 (and Sigma_xi = 0 with random effects). Heteroscedasticity: per-gene variance modelled as a smooth function of mean by local linear regression on log squared residuals after a probit-type rescaling of mean expression (Supp. 3.3). Statistic Q = q'q; asymptotically a mixture of chi-square(1).
- Null / calibration: asymptotic test (valid once the CLT applies; type I error and FDR controlled from about 16-50 samples depending on scenario, Fig. 1), or a permutation test with Phipson-Smyth P values for small n; observations of one individual are exchangeable under H0 (Supp. 3.7). Benjamini-Hochberg across genes.
- Replicates: samples/individuals are the units; repeated measures enter through a random effect.
- Validation: 1,000 simulated data sets per scenario at n = 4-200: NB, a strongly nonlinear model, SEQC resampling (no true DE), and NB with parameters fitted to the TB data. dearseq (asymptotic once n is large; permutation at all n) controlled type I error and FDR. edgeR lost FDR control above n = 50 in the NB scenario; DESeq2 failed in three scenarios, worsening as n grew; limma-voom was fine when linear and n was large enough. Real data: Singhania TB blood RNA-seq (54 samples): 272 DE genes vs 373 (edgeR), 471 (DESeq2), 402 (limma-voom); genes private to the other methods had worse leave-one-out Brier scores (Figs 4-7).
- Plots: type I error/FDR and power/TDR curves against n (Figs 1-2); Venn diagrams; Brier-score boxplots and scatter (Figs 3-7).
- Software: R/Bioconductor dearseq (https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/dearseq.html); analysis code at https://github.com/borishejblum/dearseq.
- Limitations (authors): the asymptotic test needs a large n; permutation precision trades off with run time; DESeq2/edgeR are not always inflated. For us: it is a gene-level association test of expression with a covariate across independent samples; with 2 vs 2 specimens the permutation space is tiny, and units within a specimen are not independent samples.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Not a positional-curve method. It matters as calibration evidence: parametric NB tests can be anti-conservative, so our specimen-level relabeling control is the right safeguard. Its permutation design (exchange observations within an individual) does not transfer to positions estimated from the same counts.
