## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: genewise linear models on log-expression (limma), one contrast beta_g of interest. Genewise moderated t converted to z. CAMERA = two-sample comparison of z for genes in the set versus genes NOT in the set (the complement), with the set's variance inflated by VIF = 1 + (m - 1) * mean inter-gene correlation. Two versions: parametric (correlation-adjusted t-test) and rank (Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney with a correlation-adjusted variance).
- Correlation estimate: the mean pairwise RESIDUAL correlation across replicate RNA samples, after the design effects are removed (QR residuals, d = n - p residual df); computed without forming the correlation matrix; treated as having chi-square precision on d df, so the p-value uses a t distribution on d df.
- Null (stated in parametric terms, a first per the authors): directional, mean beta in the set equals mean beta outside; non-directional, mean |beta| equal. Competitive: it can be true when all set genes are DE.
- Calibration/validation: simulation, 10,000 genes, set of 100 with correlation 0.05, two groups of four arrays (residual df 6): existing gene-permutation tests have type I error 0.24-0.28 at nominal 0.01; CAMERA 0.0087 (Table 1, Fig 1 histograms). Holds size under a "clumpy" genome-wide correlation where every gene sits in a 200-gene pathway. Power for small consistent shifts (Table 3). Real data: residual inter-gene correlations of MSigDB C2 sets are mostly positive (up to 0.71) even in sorted cells from inbred mice (Fig 2). Breast cancer (94 arrays): 74 sets at FDR 0.05, recovering basal-like signatures (Table 4); compared with GSEA.
- Replicates: RNA samples are the replicates; the correlation needs residual df from replicate samples.
- Plot types: p-value histograms under the null (Fig 1); cumulative VIF and correlation distributions across gene sets (Fig 2). No barcode plot in this paper.
- Software: limma (Bioconductor) function camera; MSigDB R objects at bioinf.wehi.edu.au.
- Limitations stated: estimating the correlation costs some power; CAMERA gives less significance to sets with positive correlation (authors argue such correlation is non-specific co-regulation).
- Not in this paper: cameraPR, the default inter.gene.cor = 0.01, fry, barcodeplot, and any statement about matrix contrasts. Those come from later limma documentation.
- Limitations for OUR design: CAMERA's correlation is the residual correlation across replicate samples under the design. Our gene-level positional statistics are computed from units within specimens, so the analogous quantity is the correlation of genes' residuals across units after the positional fit (not raw co-expression, which contains the positional signal). With 2 specimens per group, specimen-level residual df is near zero.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
CAMERA's key lesson applies directly: a set's mean statistic must be compared with the complement using a VIF built from the residual inter-gene correlation; even 0.05 inflated type I error 25-fold at alpha 0.01 for a 100-gene set. Our cameraPR with a fixed 0.01 is therefore the weak point of the competitive screen. Estimate the correlation per set from unit-level residuals of the positional NB fits (per specimen), and keep the balanced specimen relabeling as the calibration check.
