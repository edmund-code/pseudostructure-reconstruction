## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: counts per pixel, gene, sample are Poisson with mean = total UMI x lambda; lambda is a mixture over cell types of cell-type-specific rates, times a gene platform random effect and a gene-specific overdispersion random effect (both normal on the log scale, i.e. Poisson-lognormal, not NB; Results "C-SIDE learns...", Methods "C-SIDE model"). Log cell-type rate = intercept + design matrix x coefficients. The design can be an indicator (two regions), several regions, a continuous covariate (distance to a feature, density of plaque or of another cell type), or "nonparametric" thin-plate spline basis functions from mgcv (Methods "Parameterization of the design matrix"). All covariates are rescaled to [0,1]. Cell-type proportions are fixed first (RCTD, needs an annotated scRNA-seq reference); coefficients are then fitted by ML with a trust-region optimiser, separately per sample.
- Null / calibration / significance: asymptotic normality with Fisher-information standard errors; two-tailed z-test per coefficient; Benjamini-Hochberg within each cell type; FDR 0.01 plus a 1.5 fold-change cutoff (FDR 0.1, no fold cutoff in nonparametric mode). Nonparametric mode tests each spline coefficient separately with multiplicity correction (Methods "Hypothesis testing"). Calibration shown only by simulation of a two-region, single-sample design (Fig. 2d: FPR vs significance level on the identity line, 15 genes x 100 replicates).
- Replicates: "Statistical inference on multiple samples/replicates" (Methods; Supplementary Notes "Multiple replicates", eqs 29-39). Each sample's coefficient is assumed normal around a population-level effect A with between-sample SD tau; tau^2 is estimated by an unbiased method of moments, the sample variance of the per-sample estimates minus the mean squared standard error, truncated at 0 ("similar to the widely used DerSimonian-Laird method"); A is the feasible GLS (inverse-variance weighted) mean with variance 1/sum(1/(tau^2 + s_g^2)); then the same two-sided normal z-test. A nested version handles several samples with several replicates each (eqs 40-46). With G = 2 or 3 samples, the tau^2 estimate is very noisy and is often truncated to 0, in which case the pool reduces to a fixed-effect meta-analysis; no t or small-sample correction is used. Nonparametric multi-sample inference needs a common coordinate system and a common spline basis; each coefficient is tested across samples. Function names: run.CSIDE.replicates, CSIDE.population.inference.
- Validation: simulated Purkinje/Bergmann mixtures from cerebellum snRNA-seq (bias vs Bulk, Single, Decompose; FPR; unbiased estimates); Slide-seqV2 cerebellum (3 replicates, one from a prior study) with HCR validation of 4 genes (R2 = 0.89, Fig. 3d); testes Slide-seq (tubule stages); J20 Alzheimer hippocampus (4 sections from one 8.8-month female mouse; plaque density covariate); MERFISH hypothalamus (linear and quadratic midline distance); Visium lymph node (B-cell region); KP tumour Slide-seq (nonparametric patterns clustered into 7 clusters; Hallmark sets by binomial test or a permutation test on mean C-SIDE score).
- Plot types: volcano plots per cell type (Figs 4g, 5d, 6f); binned raw averages with model-prediction lines and +/-1.96 s.d. bars along a covariate (Fig. 4k midline distance; Fig. 5f DC proportion); Z-score dot matrix gene x cell type (Fig. 3c); C-SIDE vs HCR scatter (Fig. 3d); dendrogram of fitted smooth patterns (Fig. 6c); moving average of fitted expression vs distance from tumour centre (Fig. 6d); R2 vs overdispersion scatter (Fig. 6b).
- Software: R package spacexr (github.com/dmcable/spacexr), functions run.CSIDE*, CSIDE.population.inference.
- Limitations (authors): power limited by low counts, few pixels, rare cell types; needs an annotated single-cell reference; more complex models need more data. Limitations for us: the population model is a normal random-effects meta-analysis whose between-sample variance is estimated by moments and then treated as known in a z-test; with 2 specimens per group that variance is barely estimable (and is truncated at 0 when the per-sample spread is below the standard errors), and the z-test has no small-sample (t) correction. Neither the paper nor the supplement shows calibration of the multi-sample step with few samples (Supp. Fig. 12b only shows the distribution of estimated tau across genes for three cerebellum replicates). Its "replicates" were often sections from one animal (Alzheimer: 4 sections, one mouse). It pools samples to one population effect; it does not itself test a group difference (e.g. human vs mouse) unless the group is put in the design. The mixture/deconvolution machinery is irrelevant to our segmented units.

## Fit to our design
Medium-low. The useful piece is the two-stage logic (per-specimen coefficients, then a random-effects pool) and the use of a spline basis or a continuous covariate in a log-linear count model, which we already have. The deconvolution machinery is not needed (our units are segmented tubules). Its population inference is a normal-theory meta-analysis without small-sample calibration, so it does not solve our 2 vs 2 problem.
