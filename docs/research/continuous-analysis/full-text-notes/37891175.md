## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic (Supp. Note 1): input = longitudinal profiles from many individuals, at least 3 time points each, irregular in number and timing. Step 1, "conserved-dynamics seed": features min-max scaled; keep features with many unique values and high SD; for each feature, bootstrap-like resampling of equal numbers of time points from each pair of patients and Spearman correlation across time; average over pairs and iterations; keep top L (default 50) features, or a user list. Step 2, multiple trajectory alignment: progressive merging along a random guide tree; each merge is a DTW alignment that keeps both series' order; merged profiles are confidence-weighted averages (confidence = mean correlation of aligned profiles); 100 random guide trees as an ensemble. Pseudotime positions on each consensus from cumulative PCA distances (PCs to 90% variance), scaled to [0,1]. Step 3: a new sample is z-scored with the consensus normalisation and placed at the most Spearman-correlated consensus state; final pseudotime = mean over the 100 consensus trajectories; the SD across them is reported as an uncertainty score.
- Robustness score (Supp. Note 2): agreement between free placement and a monotone (non-decreasing within individual) placement found by a shortest path, using cellAlign-style interpolation.
- Null / calibration: no null for the alignment. Gene associations with pseudotime by Pearson correlation or polynomial regression across all samples (samples treated as independent), FDR-corrected; pathway "enrichment" = KS test of members' correlation coefficients vs non-members (Methods "Pathway enrichment").
- Replicates: individuals are the units being aligned (each must be a time series); downstream gene tests pool samples.
- Validation: simulations (gamma-distributed expression; 10-100 subjects, 3-10 time points) where TimeAx beat diffusion maps and chronological time for recovering true pseudotime and dynamic genes (Supp. Note 3, Fig. S1E-G). Real: influenza challenge RNA-seq (17 adults, 13-15 time points; validated in two cohorts), urothelial bladder cancer recurrences (18 patients, 4-6 samples over up to 15 years; validated in held-out, microarray and TCGA cohorts), AMD OCT imaging features (157 training patients, 1,641 test). Finds a "stromal pro-invasion point" in bladder cancer linked to survival (Fig. 3).
- Plots: seed-feature heatmap and line plots per patient (Fig. 1B, schematic); disease pseudotime vs chronological time per patient (Fig. 2B, E, H); scatter of -log10 q for gene association with pseudotime vs chronological time (Fig. 2C, F); features along pseudotime with a change point (Fig. 3B); boxplots by stage/subtype; survival curves.
- Software: R package TimeAx, https://github.com/shenorrLabTRDF/TimeAx; paper code at https://github.com/shenorrLabTRDF/TimeAxPaperCode.
- Limitations stated: one-dimensional consensus only; seed selection limited to increasing or decreasing features; needs at least 3 time points per individual. Limitations for OUR design: it needs each individual's samples to be ORDERED in time (DTW keeps order); our tubule cross-sections within a specimen are unordered, so the alignment step does not apply.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Conceptually close (a consensus axis built from several individuals) but not applicable, because TimeAx aligns ordered time series and our units are unordered cross-sections. Its ensemble-of-consensus uncertainty score is a cheap idea for unit-position uncertainty; its gene and pathway statistics pool samples and are not calibrated.
