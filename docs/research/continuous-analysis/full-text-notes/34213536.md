## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: GP regression per gene with an NB likelihood, log link (mean = exp(f(x))), dispersion fitted by maximum likelihood; optional ZINB with a Michaelis-Menten dropout probability (Supp section 1); Gaussian and Poisson likelihoods as comparators. RBF kernel by default; linear and periodic kernels compared by BIC. Sparse variational inference with inducing points chosen by M-DPP (default M = 5% of N) or k-means. A per-location multiplicative scale factor (from an NB regression of counts on total counts) handles library-size confounding (section 2.4). Branching: the DEtime/BGP crossing kernel, with the branching point x_b's posterior computed on a grid as normalised likelihood, other hyperparameters fixed at ML (section 2.5); a log Bayes factor for branching vs none (Supp eq 3).
- Null / calibration: one-sample LRT of GP vs constant model (constant = infinite lengthscale); two-sample LRT with three GPs (each sample, and one shared GP "where the datasets are treated as replicates"). P-values from chi-square with 1 df; q-value FDR. For spatial data also a permutation null that shuffles spot coordinates (section 2.3). Supplement: chi-square p-values were conservative on the olfactory bulb data (Fig S12a); the permuted null gave more calls (1202 vs 1096 SV genes). No null-data calibration study.
- Replicates: no sample hierarchy. Replicates are pooled as extra observations (simulations had two replicates per time point; the two-sample test's null pools samples as replicates).
- Validation: 4 simulated NB time courses (600 genes, 11 time points, 2 replicates; ROC by LLR, Fig 2: NB best at high dispersion); 10 cyclic dyntoy datasets from the tradeSeq benchmark: tradeSeq better with Slingshot pseudotime, GPcounts better with true time (Fig 3; authors attribute the gap to pseudotime artefacts); pancreatic alpha cells vs DESeq2 (Figs 6-7); mouse olfactory bulb ST (262 spots, 2-D): 1096 SV genes vs SpatialDE 67 (345 after fixing its LLR) and SPARK 772 (Fig 8); haematopoiesis branching times (Fig 9).
- Plots: GP fits with 5-95% predictive bands of counts, smoothed by Savitzky-Golay (mean) and LOWESS (percentiles) (Fig 1, 5, 9); posterior histogram of the branching time (Fig 9, S5-S8); ROC and precision-recall curves; spatial expression maps; Venn diagrams; LLR-vs-LLR scatter.
- Software: Python, GPflow; https://github.com/ManchesterBioinference/GPcounts (zenodo 5027066).
- Limitations stated: local optima and numerical instability (safe mode, off by default); cubic scaling without sparse approximation; pseudotime-induced artefacts; ZINB dropout parameter hard to estimate.
- Limitations for OUR design: no specimen level; the bands are predictive bands for counts, not credible bands for the mean curve; no derivative output; the spatial test is per spot with spot-shuffling permutation (units exchangeable); branching-time posterior conditions on fixed hyperparameters.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
A per-specimen NB smoother with a library-size scale factor, and an NB version of the DEtime/BGP divergence point. It does not give specimen-level inference or derivative bands, and its spatial test treats spots as exchangeable. Use it, if at all, for per-specimen divergence-point posteriors (IR vs control), with our own specimen-level calibration on top.
