## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: mutual information I(g; x) between expression level g and position x (Eqs 4-6), estimated directly from P(g|x) across embryos. Positional error sigma_x(x) from Bayesian decoding P(x|{g}) with a Gaussian small-noise approximation: 1/sigma_x^2 = sum_ij (d g_i/dx) C^-1_ij (d g_j/dx) (Eq 11, multi-gene with the covariance matrix C(x)); single-gene version sigma_x = sigma_g / |d g/dx| (Eq 12, Fig 4A). With Gaussian errors, I = <log2(L / (sigma_x sqrt(2 pi e)))>.
- Numbers (journal version): single genes Hb 2.26 +/- 0.04, Kr 1.95 +/- 0.07, Gt 1.84 +/- 0.05, Kni 1.75 +/- 0.05 bits (same as arXiv v1); "nearly two bits", "twice as much as would be expected" from sharp on/off domains (abstract). Four genes: sigma_x/L about 1%, nearly constant along the axis (Fig 4C); I = 4.14 +/- 0.05 bits from decoding and 4.1 +/- 0.23 bits directly (arXiv v1 gave 4.57 and 4.97). Unique identity for about 58 nuclei would need about 5.9 bits. Observed information is I/Imax = 0.984 +/- 0.003 of the capacity given the measured errors.
- Discrete comparator (journal version only, section "More Than One Bit per Gene?", Fig 5): each gene is thresholded on/off at the thresholds that maximize information; the 4-bit binary words carry at most 2.92 +/- 0.03 bits, against about 4.1 bits from continuous levels; binary words define some regions within a few percent of L and others very broad. Conclusion: ignoring intermediate levels loses a substantial fraction of positional information.
- Null / calibration / significance: no hypothesis tests. Uncertainty by bootstrap; MI estimates corrected for finite-sample bias by extrapolating in bin number and sample size (Strong et al., Slonim et al.); Gaussian approximation calibrated on single genes before use for multiple genes (Materials and Methods).
- Replicates: 24 embryos per gene in a tight age window (38-48 min of nuclear cycle 14); the "noise" in P(g|x) is embryo-to-embryo (and cell-to-cell) variability at the same, independently measured position x. Central 80% of the axis only; dorsal profiles only.
- Validation: agreement of decoding-based and direct information estimates; positional error compared with the measured reproducibility of later pattern elements (Eve and Runt stripes, cephalic furrow, Fig 2).
- Plot types: Fig 1 expression versus x with conditional P(x|g) slices and entropy reduction; Fig 2 positional SD of pattern elements versus position; Fig 4 sigma_x(x) along the axis for single genes and combinations; Fig 5 binary on/off domain map with per-gene information.
- Software: none; equations only.
- Limitations stated: Gaussian small-noise approximation; measurement error lowers the estimate (so values are lower bounds); relative (centre-of-mass) position may carry more information than absolute position (preliminary).
- Limitations for OUR design: (1) x here is measured independently of expression; our PT coordinate is inferred from expression, so bits are inflated for genes that built the curve. (2) The noise is estimated across 24 replicate embryos at fixed x; we have 2 specimens per group, so noise at fixed x would be mostly unit-to-unit noise within a specimen. (3) MI needs bias correction and many samples per position; multi-gene MI needed a Gaussian model. (4) Unit density along PT is not uniform; P(x) enters the information.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
The decoding identity sigma_x = noise / slope (multi-gene with covariance) is directly usable for a "where along PT is this pathway informative" curve. The best-matched "continuous vs discrete" number is the paper's own: information from continuous levels versus from an optimally thresholded code, plus I(label; x) from the same units, not the bare log2 3 bound. Two cautions decide feasibility: x must be independent of the genes scored (held-out genes or count splitting), and the noise at fixed x needs replicate structure we lack beyond units within 2 specimens.
