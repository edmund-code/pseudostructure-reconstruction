## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: for each gene, a kernel estimate of its CDF ACROSS SAMPLES (Gaussian kernel, bandwidth sd/4, for microarray; Poisson kernel for RNA-seq counts) gives a per-sample expression-level statistic; within each sample these are ranked and symmetrized (|p/2 - rank|); a KS-like random walk over the ranked genes (weight tau = 1) compares set members with non-members (Eq 3). Score either the maximum deviation (bimodal null, Eq 4) or the difference ES+ - ES- (approximately normal, Eq 5). The paper calls the per-sample score "analogous to a competitive gene set test" and "unsupervised" (no phenotype used).
- Important property stated for Eq 5: "For pathways containing genes strongly acting in both directions, the deviations will cancel each other out and show little or no enrichment"; the authors advise Eq 4 when sets are not split into up and down.
- Null / significance: GSVA itself gives no p-values; scores go into ordinary models (t-test, limma, Cox); FDR by sample-label permutation or BH (Methods).
- Replicates: scores are per sample; any sample-level model follows. Discussion: the kernel density step needs "a sufficient number of observations", more than n = 10 per their power analysis.
- Validation: simulations (linear additive model with probe and sample effects; power and type I error versus sample size, Fig 2; AUC for calling DE sets, Fig 3; survival prediction, Fig 4); leukemia ALL vs MLL (ARI of clustering on top-5 sets, Fig 5); TCGA ovarian survival, n = 588 (Fig 6, Table 1); RNA-seq vs microarray agreement on HapMap LCLs (Fig 7). Comparators: PLAGE, combined z-score, ssGSEA.
- Description of the comparators (Results, "Review of other methods"): PLAGE standardizes each gene over samples and takes the first right-singular vector of the set's SVD; combined z-score standardizes genes and combines z-scores per sample (formula not given, cites Lee et al. 2008); "Both, PLAGE and the combined z-score are parametric and assume that gene expression profiles are jointly normally distributed. The combined z-score additionally assumes that genes act independently within each gene set." ssGSEA: difference of ECDFs of ranks inside versus outside, normalized by the range over all sets and samples.
- Plot types: method schematic (Fig 1), power/type I curves, AUC and concordance boxplots, volcano + ARI boxplots, RNA-seq/microarray correlation distributions and score scatter by sex. No score heatmap in the main text.
- Software: R/Bioconductor package GSVA (R, C; GPL).
- Limitations stated: not optimal for every data set; needs n > 10; overlapping gene sets make multiple-testing correction problematic.
- Limitations for OUR design: the per-gene CDF is estimated across all samples included, so scores of one specimen's bins depend on which other specimens/species are in the matrix; with probe-panel level differences between species, pooling species would mostly score the panel offset. Eq 5 cancels bidirectional sets just as our median does.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Of limited use: GSVA is a sample-relative, rank-based score designed for large heterogeneous cohorts (n > 10, often hundreds), and its default score cancels bidirectional sets. If used at all, compute it per specimen on that specimen's bins only, use the max-deviation score (Eq 4) for unsplit KEGG/Reactome sets, and fit curves to the scores, never pooling species.
