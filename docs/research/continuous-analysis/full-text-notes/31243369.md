## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Data: bulk RNA-seq time series of 7 organs (brain/cerebrum, cerebellum, heart, kidney, liver, ovary, testis) in
  7 species (human, rhesus, mouse, rat, rabbit, opossum and chicken, i.e. six mammals and a bird); 1,893 libraries;
  9-23 stages per species; median 2-4 replicates per stage (Methods; human 1-4, median 2; mouse 4 per stage, 2 for
  gonads). Human kidney sampled up to "school" age (7-9 y).
- Model / statistic:
  - Developmentally dynamic genes (DDGs): maSigPro per organ, degree-3 polynomial in log time (days post-conception),
    DDG if goodness of fit R^2 >= 0.3 and max RPKM >= 1 (Methods "DDGs"). 79-91% of protein-coding genes are DDGs.
  - Stage correspondences: Spearman correlations between all stage medians of mouse and each other species over 1:1
    DDGs pooled across somatic organs, then dynamic time warping (R dtw, step pattern symmetricP05; asymmetric with
    open begin for rhesus and chicken) (Methods "Stage correspondences"; ED Fig. 3). Organ-specific correspondences
    checked against the global one with loess 98% intervals (ED Fig. 6): they agree except early heart in opossum and
    early ovary in human and rabbit. Authors say they were underpowered to detect short shifts.
  - Mfuzz soft clustering (c-means, 6-8 clusters per organ, membership >= 0.7) was used per species and organ ONLY to
    label genes "early" (decreasing) or "late" (increasing) for the constraint/pleiotropy analyses (Methods
    "Relationships between evolution and development").
  - Trajectory changes ("Comparing developmental trajectories"): GPClust (Gaussian-process clustering of time series,
    Hensman et al.) on vst counts, median across replicates at matched stages, noise variance k2.variance.fix = 0.7,
    jointly for human, mouse, rat, rabbit and opossum (rhesus and chicken excluded). Ancestral cluster probabilities
    are inferred along the tree as inverse-branch-length weighted averages of child nodes; a node is called changed
    if the probability that its two children are in the same cluster is below 1%; changes are then mapped to a branch
    using the outgroup.
- Null / calibration: no permutation. DDGs from maSigPro regression; trajectory changes from a 1% threshold on a
  cluster-sharing probability; organ/species differences by binomial tests; many Wilcoxon tests.
- Replicates: used in maSigPro (authors note DDG power depends on replicate agreement, Methods) and DESeq2 between
  adjacent stages; collapsed to medians for stage correspondences and for the trajectory-change clustering.
- Results relevant to the review: brain has the fewest trajectory changes (11% of DDGs), liver and testis the most
  (23%, 27%); trajectory changes are organ-specific in 93-96% of cases. The trajectory-change figure (Fig. 4b) lists
  genes tested in brain (3,980), cerebellum (3,064), heart (1,871), liver (2,284) and testis (3,191); kidney and
  ovary are not in it.
- Plot types: PCA of samples (Fig. 1b); DTW alignment path on a stage-by-stage correlation matrix (ED Fig. 3b);
  global vs organ-specific correspondence lines with loess bands (ED Fig. 6); DE gene counts between adjacent stages
  (Fig. 2b, ED Figs. 4-5); pie charts of trajectory changes per branch (Fig. 4b).
- Software: R; maSigPro, dtw, Mfuzz, GPClust, DESeq2, edgeR, WebGestalt. App: evodevoapp.kaessmannlab.org.
- Limitations stated: species differ in replicate agreement and series length, which changes DDG counts; primate
  replicates span developmental periods; underpowered for short organ-specific shifts; bulk organ data cannot
  separate regulation from cell composition. For us: bulk organs over developmental time, not a spatial axis.

## Review check
- d-comparing-trajectories.md:47 (row 14), "Seven organs, kidney included, across 7 species" - VERDICT: correct (2019).
- d:47, "Stage correspondences between species" - VERDICT: correct; misses that this is done by DTW on stage-by-stage
  Spearman correlation matrices with mouse as reference, plus an organ-specific check against the global map. That is
  a direct precedent for idea 4 (global warp first, then local deviations).
- d:47, "then maSigPro to find dynamic genes" - VERDICT: correct (degree-3 polynomial in log time, R^2 >= 0.3).
- d:47, "then joint soft clustering (Mfuzz) of human and model-species orthologue profiles" - VERDICT: wrong. The
  joint cross-species clustering used GPClust (Gaussian-process clustering). Mfuzz was run per species and organ only
  to label genes early or late.
  Replacement: "then joint Gaussian-process clustering (GPClust) of orthologue profiles (median of replicates at
  matched stages). 2019: ancestral cluster probabilities on the phylogeny; a change is called when the chance that
  two nodes share a cluster is below 1%. 2020: pairwise human-versus-species clustering; a gene differs when the
  BH-adjusted chance that both orthologues share a cluster is <= 5%. Mfuzz was only used within one species."
- d:47, "A gene whose orthologues fall in different clusters has a 'trajectory change'" - VERDICT: correct in spirit;
  in 2019 the call is phylogenetic (per branch), not pairwise.
- d:47, Plots "% trajectory changes per organ" - VERDICT: correct (ED Fig. 10a; text gives 11% brain, 23% liver, 27%
  testis). Note kidney is not among the organs in the 2019 trajectory-change figure (Fig. 4b).
- d:47, Replicates "Yes (replicate libraries per stage)" - VERDICT: misses an important point. Replicates exist and
  feed the DDG step, but the trajectory comparison uses the median across replicates, so replicate variation does not
  enter the "trajectory change" call.
  Replacement: "Replicate libraries per stage (median 2-4) feed the dynamic-gene step; the cross-species comparison
  uses the median of the replicates, so it ignores replicate variation."
- d:111-117 (idea 5), attributed to Cardoso-Moreira 2020 (and Sepp): see 33113372 note; Cardoso-Moreira uses no
  permutation.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Useful as a precedent for (i) a global DTW stage map checked organ by organ, and (ii) calling cross-species
"trajectory changes" by joint clustering of orthologue curves. For us the call would have to use centred curves
(probe panels) and add a specimen-level null, because the paper's call ignores replicates.
