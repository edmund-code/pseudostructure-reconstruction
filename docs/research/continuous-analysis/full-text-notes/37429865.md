## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: per gene, a Gaussian model on log-transformed normalised counts (logcounts; binomial deviance residuals as an alternative), y ~ N(X beta, C(theta) + tau2 I), with an exponential covariance (spatial variance sigma2, gene-specific length scale l) and a nugget tau2. A nearest-neighbour GP approximation (BRISC, 10 neighbours) makes it linear in the number of spots. X is intercept-only by default, or includes spatial-domain indicators (to find SVGs within domains). Effect size propSV = sigma2/(sigma2+tau2) (Methods "nnSVG model and parameters"). It is NOT a count model.
- Null / calibration / significance: LR test of sigma2 = 0 against a linear model without spatial correlation, asymptotic chi-square with 2 df, Benjamini-Hochberg. Calibration: null simulations by permuting coordinates (DLPFC, OB) give 1.5% and 1.0% false positives at p<0.05; real-data p-value histograms are roughly uniform with a spike near 1 (conservative), larger without low-expression filtering (Results "Evaluating the p-value distributions"; Supp. Figs S18-S19). Some LR statistics are slightly negative (Discussion).
- Replicates: one tissue section at a time. Multi-sample use = averaging per-sample ranks (described in the package vignette); a joint multi-sample model is named as future work (Results "Applying nnSVG to datasets with multiple samples"; Discussion). Rank stability across the 12 DLPFC sections: Spearman >0.8 within donors 1 and 3, lower for donor 2.
- Validation: Visium DLPFC (one section 151673, 3639 spots; 133 of 134 filtered layer markers significant among 2198 SVGs; SPARK-X called 3394 of 3396 genes significant); ST mouse olfactory bulb; seqFISH embryo (351 genes, all called significant by nnSVG and SPARK-X); Slide-seqV2 hippocampus with CA3 domain covariate; simulations varying length scale and strength (TPR/FPR); coordinate-shuffle ablation; runtime scaling. Baselines: HVGs, binomial deviance residuals, Moran's I. HVGs perform very well in several datasets (Discussion).
- Plot types: spatial expression maps; histogram of estimated length scales with known genes marked (Fig. 1b); rank-order dot chart of known SVGs per method (Fig. 1c); LR statistic vs rank with significance cutoff (Figs 1d, 2d); propSV vs mean logcounts coloured by LR (Fig. 1e); rank-vs-rank scatter against baselines (Fig. 1f); runtime boxplots (Fig. 3).
- Software: R/Bioconductor nnSVG (bioconductor.org/packages/nnSVG; github.com/lmweber/nnSVG), using BRISC and BiocParallel; analyses at github.com/lmweber/nnSVG-analyses.
- Limitations (authors): hours of runtime for >=10,000 spots; negative LR statistics; p-values for low-expressed genes not well calibrated; no gene grouping; single sample; propSV does not separate technical from biological variance. Limitations for us: Gaussian on logcounts (our units are counts with an offset); a stationary GP length scale describes the typical range of spatial correlation over the whole section, not where or how sharply a single transition happens; the coordinates are assumed 2-D (the authors say it may generalise).

## Fit to our design
Low. It is a single-section, Gaussian-on-logcounts SVG ranker. Our NB positional test on a 1-D coordinate already covers its purpose. The one transferable idea is a gene-specific scale of variation, but a located sigmoid width is the better-defined sharpness index for a 1-D axis.
