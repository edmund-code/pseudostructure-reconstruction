# Part D: comparing continuous trajectories between conditions or species, plus reviews and benchmarks

Literature review, 2026-10-14. Every PMID below was checked with `lit.py summary` (title, year and journal match).
Details come from abstracts and, where open access, from the full text. Rows whose details rest on the abstract
alone say so. "For us" means the project as described in RULES.md: units are tubule cross-sections, there is one
continuous PT axis (S1 -> S3), replicates are specimens (2 control mice, 2 IR mice, 1 human donor), and the probe
panel differs by species.

Types of difference these methods detect. I use the same names throughout:
- **level**: a constant offset (Lamian calls it a "mean shift"; Droin calls it "additive");
- **shape**: the curve itself changes (Lamian "trend difference"; Droin "interaction");
- **timing or warp**: the same programme runs earlier, later, faster or slower along the axis (cellAlign, G2G
  warps, Rayon's "allochrony");
- **divergence or mismatch**: the curves agree up to a point and then separate, or one condition has a state the
  other lacks (DEtime/BGP "perturbation or branching time"; G2G insertions and deletions);
- **density**: units are distributed differently along the axis (condiments progressionTest, Lamian XCD, Milo).

## (b) Concrete ideas to adopt (method, then the figure it produces)

1. **Five-class gene and programme classification for condition x position, after Droin 2021 and Lamian.**
   - Model: per gene, nested NB GAMMs with a specimen random intercept: flat; position only; condition only
     (level); position + condition (same shape, shifted level); position x condition (shape change).
   - Classes are assigned by BIC weights.
   - Calibration: use the balanced specimen relabeling already in use.
   - Human versus mouse: report only the interaction class, because the level term is confounded with the probe panel.
   - Figures:
     - Lamian-style heatmap row, sorted by peak position: control fitted | IR fitted | mean shift | trend difference.
     - A stacked bar per pathway showing class probabilities.

2. **"Where along PT does IR act": a difference curve with a specimen-based band, plus a divergence point.**
   - Model: an HGAM GS model (global smooth plus specimen factor-smooths, NB family). It gives Δ(x) = f_IR(x) - f_ctrl(x),
     and the band comes from specimen spread (not unit count).
   - Add a DEtime/BGP-style posterior for x*, the first position where the curves diverge.
   - Figures:
     - An **effect-location map**: a heatmap of Δ(x) for responsive genes, ordered by where |Δ| peaks, with the
       S1/S2/S3 boundaries ticked.
     - A density of divergence points along the axis. If IR acts at S3 this shows directly; the injured-S3 and Atf3
       genes are the positive controls.

3. **A level-free gradient coefficient per gene, after Weiss 2026 and Yakubovsky 2026.**
   - Statistic: a positional slope (or S1-vs-S3 log bias) with a specimen random intercept. It is immune to
     probe-panel offsets.
   - Figures:
     - Human-versus-mouse slope scatter, with correlation and quadrant counts (conserved S1, conserved S3, reversed).
     - Control-versus-IR slope scatter.
     - "Gradient lost / kept" bars split by S1-high versus S3-high genes.
     - NES-versus-NES scatter of pathway rankings in each species or condition.
   - It is cheap, and each point can be replicated per specimen.

4. **Global warp first, then gene-specific residual shifts, after Rayon 2020 with cellAlign/G2G.**
   - Step 1: fit one monotone human-to-mouse axis warp from all genes (DTW on scaled smoothed curves, or a monotone
     spline). Check it against the S1/S2/S3 boundaries.
   - Step 2: for each gene, compute the residual centre-of-mass shift after the warp and the G2G alignment similarity.
   - Figures:
     - A cellAlign pseudotime-mapping plot of the warp.
     - A scatter of residual Δ centre of mass against alignment similarity.
     - G2G aggregate-alignment paths for each pathway programme on the pairwise dissimilarity matrix.
   - This prevents a uniform stretch from being reported as many shifted genes.

5. **Joint clustering with conservation tiers, after Cardoso-Moreira 2020 and Sepp 2024.**
   - Method: soft-cluster human and mouse curves (or control and IR curves) together, then call each orthologue pair
     preserved, intermediate or diverged from cluster membership plus a permutation P.
   - Figures:
     - An alluvial (cluster-switch) diagram.
     - Clusters ordered by centre of mass.
     - Fraction diverged per pathway.

6. **Unit density along the axis before any expression comparison, after condiments, Lamian XCD and Milo.**
   - Plot per-specimen ridge plots of unit position along PT, control versus IR.
   - Test with a specimen-level test, such as a KS statistic computed per specimen and then compared across specimens.
   - Show an imbalance-score strip along the axis.
   - This separates "IR removes S3 units" from "IR changes S3 expression".

## (c) Gaps: what nobody seems to have done

- **Alignment that accounts for replicates.** cellAlign, G2G, TrAGEDy and CAPITAL each align one pooled reference to
  one pooled query. None asks whether a mismatch or warp is larger than mouse-to-mouse variation. A per-specimen
  alignment with a between-specimen null is missing.
- **Calibrated tests for very small designs.** Lamian, TDEseq and HGAM/GAMM handle replicates in principle, but none
  is calibrated for 2 versus 2 animals plus 1 donor:
  - Lamian's unconstrained covariance needs S > V+K+2;
  - permutation over 4 mice has only a handful of distinct balanced relabelings.

  Nobody provides exact or small-sample guidance for between-condition curve tests.
- **Separating platform level from biological level.** Alignment methods scale the data, which removes level;
  regression methods keep level, which carries the panel offset. The working fix in the literature is to use shape
  or slope only (Weiss β1, Yakubovsky bias, Droin interaction class). No method models a species-specific probe or
  platform offset explicitly.
- **AKI along the continuous anatomical PT axis.** Kidney AKI studies are regional or cluster-based (Dixon, Melo
  Ferreira), or they build an injury-state pseudotime (Kirita) that mixes anatomical position with injury state.
  Liver has the continuous version (Weiss 2026 zonation loss by zone; Ben-Moshe 2022 regeneration front). A
  two-axis kidney model (anatomical position x injury) appears not to exist.
- **Pathway-level differential trajectories with uncertainty.** G2G aggregate alignment and NES-on-slope correlation
  exist, but there is no competitive set test on **difference curves** Δ(x) with specimen replication. No method
  compares programme curves (median of member genes) between conditions with a calibrated null.
- **No independent benchmark of between-condition trajectory methods.** The comparisons were made by each method's
  own authors. The time-course benchmark (Spies 2019) warns that continuous models do not automatically beat pairwise
  tests.
- **Slicing bias.** Heitz 2025 names slicing bias for spatial trajectory inference, but no comparison method
  propagates it.
