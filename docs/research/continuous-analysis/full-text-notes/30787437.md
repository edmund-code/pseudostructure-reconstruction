## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic
  - Mainly an atlas paper (sci-RNA-seq3, ~2 million nuclei, 61 embryos E9.5-E13.5). Monocle 3 is introduced here (Methods, "Trajectory inference with Monocle 3").
  - Monocle 3 pipeline: UMAP (reduceDimension) -> kNN graph (k=20) in UMAP space + Louvain -> PAGA-style test of links between Louvain communities to split cells into disconnected partitions (partitionCells) -> principal graph per partition via a SimplePPT-based method on k-means "landmark" cells (ncenter), with branch pruning and optional loop closure (learnGraph) -> pseudotime = geodesic distance along a cell graph built from projections onto principal-graph edges, from user-chosen root node(s) (Methods, "Learning the principal graph", "Computing pseudotimes").
  - Gene test in Monocle 3: Moran's I spatial autocorrelation on a kNN graph (k=25, UMAP space) from which edges are removed between cells whose nearest principal-graph nodes are not adjacent; the function is named principalGraphTest() and uses modified spdep routines (Methods, "Identifying genes with complex trajectory-dependent expression"). Genes kept at FDR < 0.05 and Moran's I > 0.01 (myogenesis: 2,908 genes, main text "Reconstructing skeletal myogenesis"); FDR 1% and I > 0.01 in limb (1,783 genes, Supp. Note 1).
  - Older Monocle 2 tests are also used: differentialGeneTest (LRT, natural spline in pseudotime vs reduced model) for AER (Methods, "AER and limb mesenchyme pseudo-time analysis"), and a branch test with full model ~path * sm.ns(Pseudotime, df=3) vs reduced ~sm.ns(Pseudotime, df=3) for the Myf5 vs Myod paths (ED Fig. 12a legend).
  - AER gene dynamics (Methods; ED Fig. 6d): cells grouped along pseudotime (k-means on pseudotime, k=10, then groups of 50-100 cells), group profiles aggregated, natural spline fit with a genes-detected covariate; NB GLM spline per ED 6d legend. Each fitted curve min-max scaled; genes with the maximum in the first 20% of pseudotime called "repressed", in the last 20% "activated", otherwise "transient". This is an argmax-position classification.
  - Gene modules: hierarchical clustering cut with cutree (8 limb modules, ED Fig. 7m; 14 myogenesis modules, ED Fig. 12c); branch-DE genes clustered by Ward's method (ED Fig. 12a). No k-means clustering of genes is described.
- Null / calibration / significance: Moran's I test p-values (spdep-based), FDR; LRT p-values with multiple-testing adjustment. Cells are the observations.
- Replicates / multiple samples: 61 embryos are pooled. Embryos are used for pseudo-bulk ordering (Monocle 2 pseudotime of embryo pseudobulks, Fig. 1c) and to drop subclusters where >50% of cells come from one embryo (Methods). No embryo term in any gene test.
- Validation: WISH for novel markers (Tox2, Fndc3a, Adamts3, Snap91, Cpa2); consistency of subtrajectories with collection stage; cross-atlas matching (NNLS) to MCA and BCA. No simulation or calibration of the Moran's I test.
- Plots: 3-D UMAP with principal graph (Figs 4-6, ED 9-11); AER kinetics plot (Fig. 3h); heatmap of activated/repressed/transient genes (ED 6d); module scores on UMAP (ED 7m, 12c); branch-DE heatmap (ED 12a).
- Software: R Monocle 3 (alpha at the time; tutorial URL in Code Availability), Monocle 2 v2.6.0, scanpy v1.0, UMAP v0.3.2 via reticulate.
- Limitations (authors): sparse data, preliminary annotations, ~7% of input cells profiled, coverage < 1x per embryo. Limitations for us: cell-level tests with no specimen term; Moran's I measures autocorrelation, not shape or position; the activated/repressed/transient call depends on where the maximum of a scaled curve lands (the argmax artefact our synthesis wants to remove).

## Review check
- a-gene-dynamics.md:31 - "Monocle 3 runs a Moran's I test of graph autocorrelation on a kNN graph (`graph_test`)." - VERDICT: correct in substance, misses a detail. The paper names the function principalGraphTest(); the kNN graph is pruned to edges between cells on adjacent principal-graph nodes, so the test is trajectory-aware. `graph_test` is the later monocle3 name and is not in this paper.
  - Replacement: "Monocle 3 (Cao 2019) tests each gene with Moran's I on a cell kNN graph that keeps only edges between cells on adjacent principal-graph nodes (principalGraphTest; later `graph_test`). Genes pass at FDR < 0.05 and I > 0.01."

(The k-means claim may come from the other Monocle papers in the row; not checked here.)
  - Replacement: "Cao 2019: gene modules by hierarchical clustering (cutree), shown as module scores on the UMAP; an AER heatmap split into activated / repressed / transient genes; a Ward-clustered heatmap of path-dependent genes."
- a-gene-dynamics.md:31 (Replicates "No") - VERDICT: correct. Embryos are pooled; they are used only for pseudobulk ordering and to drop embryo-dominated subclusters.

Cao 2019 contains the plain published version of the three-class scheme our synthesis (Recommendation 1 and the heatmap point) wants to replace: genes called activated, repressed or transient from where the maximum of a min-max-scaled spline falls (first 20%, last 20%, otherwise; Methods "AER and limb mesenchyme pseudo-time analysis", ED Fig. 6d). It is also an early precedent for aggregating cells into pseudotime-contiguous groups of 50-100 before fitting NB splines, which resembles our aggregated units.
  - Replacement (add): "Cao 2019 calls genes activated, repressed or transient from where the maximum of the scaled fitted curve lies (first 20%, last 20%, otherwise). This is the argmax-based baseline that a model-ladder classification should beat. It also aggregates cells into groups of 50-100 along pseudotime before fitting NB splines."

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Little to adopt as a test: Moran's I and the LRTs treat cells as replicates. Two useful things: the argmax-position class rule (ED 6d) is the explicit baseline for Recommendation 1, and the path x spline interaction LRT (ED 12a) is the simplest "shape differs between groups" contrast, which we would run with specimen terms. Monocle 3's principal graph (SimplePPT family) is a relative of our elastic principal curve, but on UMAP coordinates.
