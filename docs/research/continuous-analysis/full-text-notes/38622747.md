## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: a linear additive mixed model (LAMM) on TRANSFORMED expression, not counts: "log-normalized gene expression level of raw counts" (Results, Statistical modeling); the Discussion says TDEseq was designed to model log-normalized or VST data "rather than the count nature of raw gene expression data". Mean = intercept part W0 + covariates W (e.g. read depth) + spline part S beta with beta >= 0. Growth/recession: quadratic I-spline basis, W0 = 1. Peak/trough: cubic C-spline basis (integrated I-splines, i.e. convex/concave) with W0 = [1, t]. Gaussian errors; a per-individual random effect with covariance proportional to a block-diagonal all-ones matrix, i.e. a random intercept per individual (compound symmetry); no random slopes. Knots default to the number of time points (Methods, knot choice; Fig S17). Fitted by cone projection (coneproj) after an eigen-transform; Mixed TDEseq iterates REML-type updates of sigma^2 and the variance ratio (Supp Text, Algorithm 1). Linear TDEseq drops the random effect.
- Null / calibration: per pattern, B_p = (SSR0 - SSR1)/SSR0, whose null is a mixture of Beta distributions (standard cone-projection result); mixture weights estimated by simulating y ~ MVN(0, I) and projecting (Supp Text). The four pattern p-values are combined by the Cauchy combination rule; combined p below 5.55e-17 set to 0. In real data, FDR is computed from an empirical null built by permuting the time-point variable 5 times (Methods, "Permutation strategy"); the level of permutation (cells or samples) is not stated. Pattern assignment: AIC for Linear TDEseq, the B-statistic for Mixed TDEseq.
- Replicates: yes, a random intercept per individual. In every design used, each individual belongs to one time point.
- Validation: Splatter simulations (4-6 time points, 3 samples per time point, 50-300 cells per sample, UMI levels, effect sizes, batch effects 0/0.04/0.12; 10 replicates of 10,000 genes). Mixed TDEseq and DESeq2 calibrated at baseline (Fig 1B); Mixed TDEseq NOT calibrated with large batch effects (Fig 1E; fixed by scMerge) or with high UMI counts (Fig S3B, "TDEseq generated well-calibrated p-values in low UMI counts but not in high UMI counts"). Pattern accuracy at 5% FDR: growth 99.0%, recession 100%, peak 80.4%, trough 43.6% (Fig 1D). SymSim "smudged" data: with many intertwined cells, pseudotime methods (tradeSeq, ImpulseDE2) beat time-point TDEseq; with pseudotime input, Linear TDEseq beat Mixed TDEseq and ImpulseDE2 but not tradeSeq (Fig S12E). Real data: HCT116 drug time course (one sample per time point; Linear only), mouse fetal liver hepatoblasts (7 stages, 13 samples, 1-2 per stage), LUAD epithelium (4 stages, 2-11 samples per stage), COVID NK cells (5 stages, 3-8 patients per stage); also run with Slingshot pseudotime on the liver data (Fig S14H, comparable to tradeSeq/ImpulseDE2).
- Plots: schematic of the four pattern shapes (Fig 1A), QQ plots, power-vs-FDR curves, pattern-accuracy bars, heatmaps of pattern-specific genes from log-transformed z-scored expression (Fig 2D, 3D, 4D, 5E; S12F, S14I), Venn diagrams with GO counts, UMAP gene plots, GO bubble plots. No fitted-curve-per-pattern panels in the main or supplementary figure legends.
- Software: R package TDEseq, https://github.com/fanyue322/TDEseq and https://sqsun.github.io/software.html (zenodo 10869078).
- Limitations stated: multi-modal patterns poorly detected; Mixed TDEseq calls weak peaks/troughs growth/recession; LAMM inference hard above ~6,000 cells (down-sample); designed for time courses where cells are not heavily intertwined between stages; batch correction can over-correct.
- Limitations for OUR design: Gaussian model on log-normalised data, not NB counts with an exposure offset. Random intercept only, so specimen-to-specimen shape differences are not modelled. Its individuals each sit at one time point, whereas each of our specimens spans the whole axis. Mixed TDEseq lost calibration at high UMI counts, which aggregated tubule units have. "Peak" is a concave C-spline fit plus a linear term, so it also fits monotone-saturating curves. No between-condition test.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Borrow the idea only: monotone (I-spline) vs convex/concave (C-spline) constrained fits per gene, with a combined p-value across shapes. Re-implement it on NB counts with an exposure offset and specimen terms (e.g. scam), because TDEseq is Gaussian on log data, uses a random intercept only, and lost calibration at high counts. Treat its "peak" class with care: a concave fit is not necessarily a mid-axis bump.
