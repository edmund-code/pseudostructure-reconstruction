# Part C: spatial zonation and spatial gradient methods

Scope: tissues analysed along one continuous anatomical axis (liver lobule, villus, intestinal length,
kidney cortico-medullary axis and proximal tubule, cortical depth, hair follicle, thymus), methods that
separate smooth gradients from sharp boundaries, spatially variable gene (SVG) tests, and common
coordinate frameworks. Every PMID below was checked with `lit.py summary` on 2026-10-14 (title, year
and journal match). Details marked **[abstract only]** come from the abstract because I could not
open the full text. Everything else was checked against the open full text (Europe PMC) or the PMC page.

## Cross-cutting: how the field summarises and shows zonation

- **Coordinates:** discrete layers or zones are the norm: 9 layers (#1), 8 gates (#2, #3), percentile-binned landmark score (#5), 30 segments (#9). Analysis then runs on bins. Continuous coordinates appear as DPT pseudo-space (#4), normalised depth (#11), distance to a landmark (#6, #19), isodepth (#17, #18), and a landmark-based CCF (#15). Most continuous-axis papers still **bin for statistics** and use the continuous coordinate for display.
- **Per-gene summaries:** max-normalised profile; **centre of mass**; argmax layer; **end-ratio bias** (log2 PP/PC); mid-zone score (max in middle / max elsewhere); Kruskal-Wallis across bins; per-domain slope plus boundary jump (#17, #18); FSV or length scale (#22); sigmoid t0 and k (#21).
- **Pathway placement:** enrichment of zonated genes per zone (hypergeometric, #1); **gene-set mean profile ± s.e.m.** (#1, #2, #5); GSEA on a ranked zonation score (#5, #17); category-specific KEGG enrichment after model selection (#3); boundary-agnostic modules (SOM #4, NMF #9); **enzyme-order test** (#1). Nobody we found runs a *competitive* test of a pathway's **position or shape** against background genes with correlation adjustment.
- **Figures:** COM-sorted max-normalised heatmaps; line profiles ± s.e.m.; mean-vs-COM scatter; ortholog bias scatter (species vs species); binned-axis heatmaps of cell-type abundance; cluster density along the axis (violins); dendrograms of ordered bins; pattern-class bar charts.
- **Group comparisons on one axis:** per-gene COM or bias scatter plus rank-sum (#5); cosine similarity plus bin × group ANOVA interaction (#15); mixed model with replicate intercept and BIC categories (#3); a "flattened gradient" judged by eye (#12).

## (b) Concrete ideas to adopt

1. **Pattern classes by nested NB models plus BIC, with specimen terms** (Droin #3 × GASTON #17 × switchde #21). Per gene, fit a nested ladder on pseudospace with a per-specimen offset: flat → linear → smooth spline (graded) → **sigmoid** (switch; t0 and width = 4.39/k) → **piecewise linear with jumps at the S1/S2 and S2/S3 transitions** (boundary). Choose by BIC and keep the posterior model weights. *Figure:* a bar chart of class counts, a small-multiples panel of exemplar curves per class, and a **2-D scatter of within-segment slope vs boundary jump** (GASTON-style), coloured by class. This replaces the stand-alone logistic-edge call with a call that has an alternative model.
2. **Fixed-bin shape-vs-level comparison across groups** (OrganAxis #15; Yakubovsky #5). Bin pseudospace with the same cut-offs for every specimen. For each gene and pathway program, report the **cosine similarity** of binned profiles (shape conservation) and an **NB bin × group interaction** with specimen terms (shape difference), for human vs mouse and AKI vs control. *Figure:* a sorted bar (cosine) with overlaid dots (interaction −log10 q) per pathway, plus a **human-vs-mouse ortholog scatter of COM and of S3/S1 bias** (log2 late/early) with discordant genes labelled.
3. **Position-based pathway analysis** (Halpern #1; Yakubovsky #5). (a) Run a competitive test on **member-gene COM** (or argmax+COM score) against background genes. This asks whether the pathway is *located* earlier or later than expected, not just whether it is positional. (b) For ordered metabolic pathways, run Halpern's **enzyme-order test**: do consecutive enzymes peak at consecutive positions, compared with random pairs? *Figure:* COM vs reaction-step plot per pathway with a permutation band, and pathway member COMs as a strip plot on the pseudospace axis.
4. **Discrete-domain check on the continuous axis** (Zwick #9; Belayer #18). Cluster ordered pseudospace bins (adjacency-constrained hierarchical clustering) and run a shared-breakpoint DP. Ask whether the transcriptome prefers discrete domains, how many there are, and where they sit relative to the S1/S2/S3 label transitions. *Figure:* a dendrogram of ordered bins with label-transition ticks, plus NMF/SOM module curves along pseudospace that do not depend on boundaries.
5. **Per-specimen curve features pooled by random effects** (C-SIDE #24; per-sample COM in #5). Compute COM, slope, jump and sigmoid width per specimen, then pool with between-specimen variance. With so few specimens, show every specimen's value. *Figure:* a forest-style dot plot per gene or pathway (one dot per specimen, a pooled estimate, an interval), grouped by species or condition. Pair it with a **DESpace-style NB test on S1/S2/S3 labels with specimen terms** (#23) as the strong discrete comparator.
6. **Label densities along the axis** (MERFISH normalised depth #11). For each specimen, plot S1/S2/S3 label kernel densities along pseudospace. This shows directly where discrete labels overlap and how wide each transition is. *Figure:* stacked density ridges, one row per specimen.

## (c) Gaps: what nobody seems to have done

- **Replicate-aware gradient-vs-boundary inference.** GASTON, Belayer, SPATA2 and switchde all fit one sample or one pooled trajectory. Droin has a random intercept but no shape random effects. C-SIDE pools coefficients but does not classify shapes. I found no method that classifies "sharp vs graded" with **specimens as replicates**, or that tests whether boundary *position* or *width* differs between groups.
- **Repeated physical units as replicates of one axis.** Liver and villus papers pool cells from many lobules or villi into one profile. The imaging (#14) shows **PT-to-PT variation in transition length**, but no transcriptomic method estimates unit-level variation in where transitions occur.
- **Coordinate uncertainty.** Every method treats the coordinate (layers, isodepth, DPT) as fixed when testing genes. None propagates uncertainty in a *reconstructed* coordinate into boundary-width or slope estimates, which matters for calling "sharp" on a pseudospace.
- **Pathway position and shape tests.** Pathways are placed by enrichment within zones, GSEA on per-gene zonation scores, or mean profiles. We found no competitive, correlation-adjusted test of a pathway's **location (COM) or sharpness** relative to background, and no cross-species test of pathway **shifts along the axis** with species-specific probe panels.
- **Continuous PT zonation in situ.** Kidney work found here is discrete (S1/S2/S3 clusters, #13), cortico-medullary across segments (#12), or protein and imaging based (#14). A PubMed search for "proximal tubule zonation single cell" returned no relevant hits. A genome-wide continuous S1→S3 atlas, with human vs mouse and injury on the same axis, appears to be new.

## Notes and uncertainties

- Moor 2018 (#8), Harnik 2024 (#8) and Ben-Moshe 2022 (#7) are summarised from abstracts only: the full texts were paywalled and bioRxiv returned HTTP 429. I make no claims about their exact per-gene statistics or figure types. Schuh 2018 (#14) is also abstract only.
- Hinze 2021 (#12) method details (novoSpaRc, 100 positions, sliding average of 150, sort by regression P, n=2 knockouts, no formal slope test) come from a WebFetch summary of the PMC page, not from a direct quote.
- The Belayer repository URL was not verified. Software for Zwick 2024 was not checked.
- The SVG tests (#22) and spatial pseudotime (#20) build on tissue x/y. They are listed for completeness and for their per-gene summaries. Under our rule of no tissue x/y in reconstruction, they are not candidates for building the coordinate.
