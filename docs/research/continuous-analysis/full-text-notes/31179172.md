## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: a tutorial, not a new method. A hierarchical GAM (HGAM) lets a smooth function of a covariate
  vary between levels of a grouping factor, with the group curves pooled toward a common shape by penalties
  ("What are Hierarchical GAMs?"). Three choices give five models (Fig. 4): G = one global smoother (plus a
  random intercept per group in their examples); GS = global smoother plus group-level smoothers sharing one
  smoothing parameter, coded with the factor-smooth interaction basis bs="fs" (each group also gets its own
  intercept through the penalised null space, footnote 3); GI = global plus group smoothers each with its own
  smoothing parameter (by= argument, plus an explicit bs="re" intercept); S = GS without the global term;
  I = GI without the global term. They recommend m=1 (first-derivative penalty) for group-level smoothers and
  m=2 for the global one, to reduce concurvity between global and group terms.
- Null / calibration / significance: there is no test of a between-condition difference. Model structures are
  compared with AIC (Table 1) and out-of-sample deviance (Tables 3-4). The authors do NOT recommend generalized
  likelihood-ratio tests between HGAM structures: "There is not sufficient theory on how accurate parametric p-values
  are for comparing these models" ("Comparing different HGAM specifications"). They also show AIC is unreliable for
  choosing between models with and without a global smoother (the global term is concurve with the group terms) and
  "strongly recommend not selecting models based purely on AIC". To test whether a global smooth is needed they
  suggest select=TRUE and summary.gam (footnote 5). mgcv intervals are Bayesian credible intervals, conditional on
  the estimated smoothing parameters unless unconditional=TRUE ("A brief foray into the land of Bayes").
- Replicates / multiple samples: yes in the sense of grouped curves: groups are 12 plants (CO2), 6 simulated bird
  species, 8 zooplankton taxa, 4 lakes. Group-level smoothers are random-effect-like terms ("Model GS is a close
  analogue to a GLMM with varying slopes"). The authors say the unit of analysis is still individual observations,
  not whole functions ("Beyond HGAMs: functional regression"). No design has groups nested in treatment
  conditions, and no condition-difference curve is estimated.
- Validation: worked examples (CO2 data; simulated bird_move data generated from a GS model; Lake Mendota
  zooplankton, Daphnia across 4 lakes, Gamma family). One small simulation: 4 groups of cosine curves of different
  frequency, 3 noise levels, 25 replicates each, comparing model S and I (Fig. 18): with a shared penalty, model S
  over-smooths high-frequency groups at moderate noise and smooths everything toward straight lines at
  signal-to-noise 0.125. Timing simulation for gam/bam/gamm/gamm4 (Fig. 19). Global smoother recovery under te()
  vs t2() group terms (Fig. 20): te() over-smooths the global term. They also note mgcv "still occasionally finds
  solutions with simulated data where the global term is over-smoothed".
- Plot types: gratia draw() of the global smoother and of group-specific deviation smoothers (Figs. 6, 10, 13);
  per-group predicted curves +/-2 s.e. over the data (Figs. 7, 11, 15, 17); observed-vs-predicted (Figs. 9, 12);
  QQ and residual diagnostics (Figs. 14, 16); AIC and out-of-sample deviance tables. No "difference smooth" between
  two conditions is shown.
- Software: R mgcv (version 1.8-26), gratia for plots; brms, jagam/JAGS, bamlss and refund (pffr) mentioned. Code:
  github.com/eric-pedersen/mixed-effect-gams.
- Families: examples use Gaussian (log uptake), Poisson (bird counts) and Gamma (zooplankton). The text states that
  negative binomial is one of mgcv's extended families, available in gam()/bam() but NOT in gamm() or gamm4()
  ("Alternative formulations").
- Limitations stated: concurvity of global and group terms inflates uncertainty of the global smooth and can flatten
  it; side-constraint dropping in tensor-product GS models changes the global smooth; equal-smoothness assumption
  biases group curves; how to model a distribution of penalties across groups "has not been well studied"; the
  "global function" is an average whose meaning depends on the sampling of groups (footnote 11).
- Limitations for OUR design: with 2 control + 2 IR specimens, a factor-smooth specimen term has its smoothing
  (variance) parameter estimated from 4 curves; the credible band on any IR-minus-control difference is model-based
  and conditional on that estimate, and the paper offers no calibration for so few groups. Its advice against
  GLRT p-values for these comparisons applies directly.

## Review check
- d-comparing-trajectories.md:31 (row 8), taxonomy "G (global smooth only), GS (global plus group smooths of shared
  wiggliness, the "fs" factor-smooth basis), GI (global plus group smooths each with its own wiggliness), S and I
  (group smooths only)" - VERDICT: correct ("What are Hierarchical GAMs?", Fig. 4).
- d:31, "It is fitted in mgcv, which allows NB families" - VERDICT: correct, with a caveat the review misses: NB works
  with gam()/bam() but not with gamm()/gamm4(), and the paper has no NB example (Gaussian, Poisson, Gamma only).
- d:31, Plots "Global curve with group curves around it; difference smooths" - VERDICT: partly unsupported. The paper
  plots the global smooth and each group's deviation smooth (Figs. 10, 13) and per-group fitted curves; it shows no
  difference curve between conditions.
  Replacement: "Global smooth plus each group's deviation smooth (gratia draw); per-group fitted curves with +/-2 s.e.;
  AIC and out-of-sample deviance tables."
- d:31, Replicates "Yes: this is exactly the 'replicates as random curves' model" - VERDICT: correct in substance (GS
  treats group curves as penalised random deviations; "close analogue to a GLMM with varying slopes"), but misses that
  the paper's groups are 4-12 levels with no treatment factor above them, and that it gives no tests for comparing
  conditions.
- d:31, For us "condition-level smooths plus specimen-level random-wiggle smooths ... yields a difference curve Δ(x)
  with a band that reflects specimen-to-specimen spread, not unit count" - VERDICT: unsupported by the full text.
  The paper never fits condition-level smooths or a difference curve, and says nothing about how well the band is
  calibrated when there are few groups.
  Replacement: "Fit condition-level smooths (s(x, by = condition)) plus specimen factor-smooths (bs = "fs", m = 1),
  NB family, in gam(). The difference curve Δ(x) and its band come from the model. The band is a Bayesian interval,
  conditional on smoothing parameters estimated from only 2 specimens per arm. Pedersen et al. give no calibration
  for so few groups, so check the band with our balanced specimen relabeling."
- d:82 (idea 2), "Model: an HGAM GS model (global smooth plus specimen factor-smooths, NB family). It gives Δ(x) =
  f_IR(x) - f_ctrl(x), and the band comes from specimen spread (not unit count)." - VERDICT: wrong as a model
  description, and unsupported on the band. A GS model has one global smooth and no condition term, so it cannot
  give f_IR - f_ctrl. Also, the paper warns that global and group terms are concurve, which inflates uncertainty and
  can over-smooth the global curve, and that p-values for comparing HGAM structures lack theory.
  Replacement: "Model: NB GAMM in mgcv gam(): one smooth per condition plus specimen factor-smooths (bs = "fs",
  m = 1). Δ(x) = f_IR(x) - f_ctrl(x) is the difference of the two condition smooths. Its band is model-based and
  depends on a specimen-variance estimate from 4 mice, so calibrate it with balanced specimen relabeling before
  reading it as specimen-level uncertainty. Do not use likelihood-ratio p-values between HGAM structures (the
  authors advise against it)."
- d:130-135 (gap), "Lamian, TDEseq and HGAM/GAMM handle replicates in principle, but none is calibrated for 2 versus
  2 animals plus 1 donor" - VERDICT: correct for HGAM. The paper gives no small-sample calibration and advises against
  GLRT p-values; it selects structures by AIC, out-of-sample deviance and subject knowledge.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
A good vocabulary and fitting recipe (fs basis, m=1 group terms, NB in gam(), REML, select=TRUE) for per-specimen
curves pooled toward a condition curve. It is a tutorial with no inferential machinery for condition contrasts and
no calibration for 2-4 groups; any Δ(x) band from it must be calibrated by our specimen relabeling.
