## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: dynamic time warping of two multi-gene time series. Each time point is a vector of k genes'
  expression (log2 ratios); DTW finds the order-preserving, global (Needleman-Wunsch-like) many-to-many mapping of time
  points that minimises a time-weighted sum of Euclidean distances (simple warp, `genewarp`). An interpolative variant
  (`genewarpi`) maps sample points to linear interpolations in the other series, after Kruskal & Liberman. Horizontal or
  vertical path segments ("compexps") are local compressions/expansions.
- One warp for all genes: "it does this by mapping the time points themselves and therefore operates as if all the
  genes in a cell move relatively in lock step" (Discussion). Gene-group-specific timing ("time course
  superpositions") is raised as an open problem.
- Null / significance: none usable. A shuffled-time-point null placed the real alignment score at the 25.5th percentile,
  and the authors conclude "use of shuffled series does not provide a good basis for evaluating scores statistically"
  (Results "Statistics of optimal alignment scores"). Quality is judged by path shape (slope near 1).
- Stability: 250 random half-splits of genes; the area between the two warping paths measures instability. Large gene
  sets with high variance (990 genes) give the most stable warps; small clusters (11-27 genes) give unstable paths
  (Table 4; Results). The MET cluster (11 genes) "too small to assure a stable alignment" (Discussion).
- Replicates: none; three yeast cell-cycle series (alpha factor, cdc15, elutriation; Spellman 1998) aligned pairwise.
- Validation: half-series self-alignments (alpha, cdc15 align; elutriation, with < 1 period, correctly fails as a
  negative control); a simulated 28-min arrest (4 repeated time points with noise, 100 instances) recovered as a
  horizontal segment in all 100 paths; comparison with Ward hierarchical clustering of time points (warping keeps series
  apart and uses order).
- Plots: the warping path on the dynamic-programming matrix (path graph); input series in real time; aligned series on
  a common "trajectory average" time frame (Figs 2, 5); superimposed path graphs (Fig. 6).
- Software: Win32 DOS executables genewarp, genewarpi, grphwarp, grphwarpi (C++), from the Church lab site.
- Limitations stated: no statistics for alignment scores; small gene sets unstable; data from different platforms or
  normalisations bias alignments; one warp for all genes; suggestions include local and multiple alignments, gaps,
  HMMs, and "concentration warping" for non-temporal axes.
- Limitations for our design: no replicates, no test, Euclidean distance on levels (a probe-panel level offset between
  species would enter the distance unless genes are centred per species).

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Historical precedent for DTW on expression series and for "concentration warping" of a non-temporal axis (the idea
transfers to an anatomical axis). Its two findings are the useful part for us: global warps need many genes to be
stable, and alignment scores have no easy null. Any human-to-mouse PT warp we fit should be checked for stability by
gene splits and per specimen.
