## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Type: expert-recommendation review summarising independent benchmarks; no new method or data.
- Differential expression (section "Differential gene expression analysis"): two views, sample-level pseudobulk (edgeR, DESeq2, limma) and cell-level mixed models (MAST). Bulk methods perform favourably; single-cell methods underestimate variance. Pseudoreplication inflates FDR; "Within-sample correlation should be accounted for by aggregating cell-type-specific counts within an individual before DGE analysis"; "pseudobulk methods with sum aggregation and mixed models such as MAST with random effect setting were found to be superior to naive methods, such as the popular Wilcoxon rank-sum test". PCA on pseudobulk samples to find sources of variation and build the design; failing to model several sources of biological variability inflates FDR.
- Trajectories (section "From discrete states to continuous processes"): method performance depends on topology (Slingshot for simple, PAGA and RaceID/StemID for complex), use dynguidelines; "When the expected topology is unknown, trajectories and downstream hypotheses should be confirmed by multiple trajectory inference methods using different underlying assumptions"; inferred trajectories "might not necessarily have biological meaning". No discussion of between-condition trajectory comparison or trajectory DE tests.
- Marker tests: Wilcoxon is best for cluster markers, but P values are inflated because the same data define the clusters (double dipping) (section "Mapping cell clusters to cell identities").
- Composition: no independent benchmark; univariate tests ignore compositionality; scCODA etc.; Milo and DA-seq for kNN neighbourhoods, with the caveat that they lose information when condition and confounders are correlated (section "Deciphering changes in cell composition").
- Spatial: no consensus on how to call spatially variable genes (SPARK, SpatialDE, BayesSpace, spaGCN, sepal listed) (section "Identification of spatial patterns").
- Gene set enrichment: results depend more on gene-set choice than on the statistical method.
- Null / replicates: recommends individual-level aggregation or random effects; no small-sample guidance.
- Plots: workflow schematics only (Figs 1-6).
- Software: frameworks listed (Scanpy, Seurat, Bioconductor, Squidpy, Giotto) plus the sc-best-practices.org book.
- Limitations stated: recommendations lag behind method development; several areas lack benchmarks. For OUR design: nothing on continuous-axis between-condition statistics or on 2-vs-2 designs.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
General support for specimen-level aggregation and against cell- or unit-level Wilcoxon tests; also flags double dipping when testing on labels built from the same data. It offers nothing specific on continuous-axis comparisons between conditions or on very small designs, so cite it only for the replicate principle.
