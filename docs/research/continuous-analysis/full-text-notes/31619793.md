## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Data. 10x scRNA-seq of human (7 lines; 2 lines over a 0 d to 4 month time course), chimpanzee (3 iPSC lines
  listed in Methods) and macaque (1 line) cerebral organoids; reads mapped to a human-chimpanzee-macaque consensus
  genome that masks all discordant sites, to remove species mapping bias (Methods "consensus genome"). Plus
  scATAC-seq (human, chimp, macaque) and adult prefrontal snRNA-seq.
- Pseudotime. Pseudocells (seed cells plus 10 nearest neighbours, averaged; Methods "RSS and pseudocells"); cortical
  pseudotime = rank on diffusion component 1 (destiny), computed separately per species (Methods "Pseudotime
  estimation ... different species").
- Alignment (Methods "Dynamic time warping (dtw)-based alignment"). Each pseudotime course is cut into 50 equal
  blocks; block averages are compared by Pearson correlation distance (1 - r) over highly variable genes; standard
  DTW recursion with "fixed-end", "fixed-start" and "end-to-end" modes. Human vs chimp cortical courses used
  fixed-start (Fig 3f,g): the later chimpanzee pseudotime points "fail to map to a human pseudocell counterpart".
- Truncated cross-species alignment (Methods "Truncated dtw-based alignment"; ED Fig 11c). Upper-layer neurons are
  removed. Step 1 (trimming): support-vector regression on RSS predicts each human pseudocell's chimp/macaque
  pseudotime; a constrained B-spline (R cobs) maps human pseudotime to chimp/macaque pseudotime; chimp/macaque cells
  beyond the point matching the end of the human course are dropped. Step 2: end-to-end DTW with the human course as
  template. This is a partial (extent-matched) alignment, then a nonlinear warp. No single time-scaling factor is
  estimated anywhere.
- "Slower pace" evidence (main text para on chimpanzee; Fig 3f-j; ED Fig 8, 9). Qualitative, combining: late chimp
  states with no human counterpart in the DTW; human cells project to earlier stages of a fetal reference (Fig 3h);
  neuron-maturation gene scores rise higher in chimp along unaligned pseudotime (Fig 3i); more astrocytes per radial
  glia in chimp at 2 and 4 months (Fig 3j); earlier upper/deep-layer divergence in chimp and earlier still in macaque
  (ED Fig 8e, 9d). Preprint abstract: "human neuronal development proceeds at a delayed pace relative to the other
  two primates" (journal abstract on PubMed: "occurs at a slower pace").
- Conservation test (Methods "Conserved developmental trajectories"). Per species, an F-test of a natural-spline
  (df 6) fit on pseudotime vs none finds dynamic genes; for genes dynamic in all three species, Pearson r between
  species over 50 interpolated points on the aligned pseudotime; threshold from 100 pseudotime permutations. Result:
  76.6% of dynamic genes conserved (main text).
- Differential expression (Methods "Identification, clustering ..."). Per gene, natural-spline (df 6) regression
  on the aligned pseudotime: null = one curve for both species; alternative = species-specific slopes and intercept;
  F-test, BH P < 0.01. Robustness: resample human pseudocells from 1 to 7 lines, matched to the chimp distribution
  in 10 pseudotime bins, 100 times each; "robust DE" = detected in >= 80% of resamples. False-positive estimate:
  the same test between two groups of human lines (ED Fig 11e). Human-specificity by macaque outgroup using
  cumulative divergence over 50 bins. 98 human-specific DE genes, 96 in seven pseudotemporal clusters (Fig 4c; Ward
  clustering of normalised DE spectra). Most human-specific changes are gains (Fig 4g).
- Null / replicate unit. P values treat pseudocells as observations (pseudoreplication); cell lines enter only
  through the resampling robustness filter and the human-vs-human false-positive check.
- Plot types. DTW alignment path on the block distance matrix (Fig 3f, ED Fig 11c); embedding coloured by aligned
  pseudotime (Fig 3g); gene profiles before and after alignment (ED Fig 11d); per-cluster mean DE along pseudotime
  with 50%/95% bands (Fig 4c, 4e); per-gene three-species curves (Fig 4d); gain/loss bars (Fig 4g).
- Software. R (Seurat, destiny, igraph, princurve, cobs, e1071, splines); DTW implemented as described in Methods;
  no package released for the alignment; data browser at bioinf.eva.mpg.de/shiny/sample-apps/scApeX.
- Limitations the authors state. iPSC lines differ in organoid composition and in upper/deep-layer timing; snRNA-seq
  DE needed a detection-rate test because Wilcoxon was unreliable with unequal coverage.
- Limitations for OUR design. "Pace" is read against real culture time (days to months); our axis is anatomical,
  so only the alignment machinery transfers, not the heterochrony interpretation. Correlation distance across genes
  per block removes block-level scale but not gene-specific constant offsets such as probe-panel efficiency; gene-wise
  centring within species would be needed before a cross-panel DTW. The F-test uses pseudocells as replicates.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Relevant as a method template for human vs mouse along one axis: per-species pseudotime, trim to the shared extent,
DTW on block correlation distances, then a natural-spline F-test of shared versus species-specific curves, with a
same-species split (human lines vs human lines) to estimate false positives. That split is close to our balanced
specimen relabeling. Its pace (heterochrony) reading does not transfer to an anatomical axis, and its consensus-genome
fix for species measurement bias has no direct equivalent for species-specific probe panels.
