## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Data: re-analysis of the 2019 resource (1,443 libraries: human 297, mouse 316, rat 350, rabbit 315, rhesus 165 in
  Methods; 154 rhesus in Results text). Seven organs including kidney. Median 2 replicates for human and rhesus,
  typically 4 per stage for mouse, rat and rabbit (2 for gonads).
- Model / statistic:
  - WGCNA co-expression modules over human organs and stages (Fig. 1B).
  - Mfuzz c-means soft clustering of human DDGs per organ (standardised profiles; 6-8 clusters) to find which temporal
    profiles disease genes are enriched in (Fig. 2D; Methods). This is within human only.
  - Trajectory differences ("Organ developmental trajectories", Methods): for each organ, GPClust (Gaussian-process
    clustering of time series) on the combined human + one other species data (vst counts, median across replicates,
    matched stages from the 2019 DTW correspondences); k2.variance.fix = 0.7; GPClust infers the number of clusters.
    Each gene gets a vector of cluster-membership probabilities; the probability that a pair of 1:1 orthologues is in
    the same cluster gives a P value; Benjamini-Hochberg FDR 5% calls "same" vs "different". Pairwise comparisons
    (human vs mouse, rat, rabbit, rhesus) instead of the 2019 phylogenetic approach, which doubles or triples the genes
    tested (5,253-8,666 per organ for human-mouse).
- Null / calibration: no permutation. Calls come from GPClust membership probabilities plus BH. Checks: trio
  consistency (genes similar in mouse-rat and mouse-human are similar in human-rat in ~96% of calls, matching a 5% FDR;
  mouse-rat similar but mouse-human different implies human-rat different in only 65-82%, so the call is conservative;
  Fig. S6A), external PsychENCODE human-vs-rhesus comparison (p = 1e-5, Wilcoxon; Fig. S6B), robustness to the
  orthologue set (Fig. S6C) and to k2.variance.fix (Fig. S6D). The number and kind of differences depend strongly on
  k2.variance.fix: 1 finds only opposing trajectories, 0.5 finds many subtle ones; even lower values add clusters
  "strongly biased toward having genes from only one of the species".
- Replicates: collapsed to the median before clustering; replicate variability does not enter the call.
- Key numbers: human vs mouse, 51% of tested genes (6,816 of 13,471) differ in at least one organ; human vs rat 45%
  (5,459/12,155); human vs rabbit 38% (4,568/11,731) (Results "Organ Developmental Trajectory Differences Are
  Common"). Disease genes are depleted for differences; >200 brain/heart/liver disease genes still differ.
- Plot types: Fig. 5 - for each organ, a line diagram with one line per significantly different orthologue pair,
  joining the human cluster (top) to the mouse cluster (bottom), coloured by human cluster, with the cluster mean
  profiles drawn below. Fig. 5 is titled "...Human and Mouse in Brain, Heart, and Kidney", yet its legend ends "The
  same analysis for cerebellum, kidney, and testis is shown in Figure S5" (the legend is inconsistent; kidney is in
  Fig. 5 or Fig. S5 either way). Fig. 6A: % genes with different trajectories per organ and species (rhesus only brain,
  heart, liver). Fig. 6B: example disease genes; Fig. 6C: disease vs non-disease fractions.
- Software: R; WGCNA, DESeq2, Mfuzz, GPClust (github.com/SheffieldML/GPclust), WebGestalt. "This study did not generate
  any unique datasets or code."
- Limitations stated: bulk organs cannot separate regulation, cell composition or cell-type identity as causes;
  only 1:1 orthologues; phenotypic meaning of subtle trajectory differences unclear.
- For OUR design: the paper does not say whether profiles were centred or scaled before GPClust (Mfuzz input is
  standardised; GPClust input is described only as vst counts, median of replicates). With species-specific probe
  panels, we would have to centre curves first or level offsets would drive cluster switches.

- d:47, For us "call a gene 'diverged' when its orthologues switch cluster ... Report the fraction diverged per

  label change.
  Replacement: "...call a gene diverged when the probability that its orthologues share a cluster is low (BH FDR).
  Report how the calls change with the number of clusters, as the paper does with the GP noise setting. Centre
  curves first, because our panels differ in level."
- d:111-117 (idea 5), "call each orthologue pair preserved, intermediate or diverged from cluster membership plus a
  permutation P" - VERDICT: unsupported for Cardoso-Moreira. The paper has two calls (same / different), from GPClust
  membership probabilities with BH correction; there is no permutation and no three-tier scheme. (The tiers may come
  from Sepp 2024, which this note does not check.)
  Replacement: "Method: cluster human and mouse curves together (Gaussian-process or soft clustering). For each
  orthologue pair, compute the probability that both fall in the same cluster from the membership probabilities.
  Call 'different' at BH FDR 5% (Cardoso-Moreira 2020). Three tiers with a P value follow Sepp 2024. With one donor,
  add a specimen-level check: do the two control mice agree with each other?"

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
A cheap, interpretable human-vs-mouse "trajectory different?" call per gene with a good figure (the cluster-switch
line diagram). It ignores replicates and depends on a clustering-resolution parameter, so for us it is exploratory:
centre curves, report sensitivity to cluster number, and use mouse-vs-mouse agreement as the floor.
