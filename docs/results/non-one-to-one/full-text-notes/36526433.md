## What the paper does (with sections/figures)
- **How homology enters.**
  - The input is a heterogeneous graph with cell and gene nodes. A cell-gene edge means non-zero expression, a
    gene-gene edge means homology, and a cell-cell edge is a within-species KNN with k = 5 (Methods; Fig. 1A).
  - Homologs come from Ensembl Compara (BioMart). One-to-many and many-to-many pairs are allowed. Domainoid was used as
    an alternative (Methods, Orthology inference).
  - The paper states that the gene-gene weights are the same for every pair: "Note that the values of Wgg are
    assigned in the same way for one-to-one gene homologies and non-one-to-one homologies". Wgg is also tied to the
    gene self-loop weight. There is no orthology confidence or sequence-similarity weight.
  - Cell-node input features are 1:1 only: "one-to-one homologous genes are taken as the common input features". These
    are top-50 DEGs per group that have 1:1 homologs, giving 240-400 genes for distant pairs and 400-900 otherwise.
  - Non-1:1 genes enter only as gene nodes (the union of HVGs, DEGs and their homologs, 5,415-7,050 node genes)
    linked by homology edges (Methods, Gene selection).
- **Model and outputs.** A relational GCN with two recurrent layers, then a graph-attention classifier. It is trained
  only on reference labels (cross-entropy plus multilabel loss, class weights, label smoothing). The checkpoint is
  chosen by AMI against Leiden clusters of the query cells (Methods). Outputs are:
  - per-query-cell type probabilities;
  - aligned cell and gene embeddings;
  - per-cell attention over genes;
  - "gene modules", from Leiden at resolution 0.8 on a KNN graph of gene embeddings from both species;
  - module-to-module weights, the summed cosine similarity divided by the larger module size (Methods, Gene module
    extraction; Fig. 4E-F, 5D-E).

  There is **no paralog-specific output**. Homologs are only bridges for message passing.
- **Null / significance / calibration.**
  - Gene modules have no null and no stability or resampling assessment. They are judged by GO enrichment
    (BH-adjusted p < 0.1; Tables S2/S3), and by eye on UMAPs.
  - Cell-typing comparisons use Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across dataset pairs. CAME beats others with p < 1e-16
    (zebrafish-excluded) and p < 1e-54 (zebrafish-associated) (Fig. 2A,B). The pairs share datasets, so they are not
    independent; the paper does not address this.
  - The homology source makes no difference: Ensembl Compara vs Domainoid gives paired t p = 0.701 and rank-sum
    p = 0.941 (Discussion; Fig. S17).
- **Replicate handling.** There is none at the donor or specimen level. The human pancreas reference has eight
  batches, and the paper presents it as a feature that CAME "can ignore the batch effects of reference data", with no
  batch labels given (Results; Fig. 3B, S11). The unit of benchmarking is a dataset pair.
- **Validation.** The benchmark covers 54 datasets, 7 species and 5 tissues, giving 649 reference-query pairs: 139
  zebrafish-excluded and 510 zebrafish-associated (Results; Table S1).
  - **Kidney is included**: mouse kidney (Adam, Karaiskos, Park, Tabula Muris 10x), human kidney (Wu, Young, Hochane)
    and zebrafish kidney (Table S1).
  - Human-to-mouse brain: about 98% accuracy, similar to Seurat and SciBet; macro-F1 0.55 vs 0.44 and 0.46 (Fig. 4A).
  - Interneuron subtypes: 98.3% human-to-mouse and 95.5% mouse-to-human (Fig. S13).
  - Human-to-macaque spermatogenesis: 95.0%, with qualitative trajectory and module alignment (Fig. 5).
  - Retina, zebrafish to chick, mouse and human: accuracy is 27.1%, 14.7% and 26.0% above the next best method
    (Fig. 6A).
- **Close species and 1:1-only vs non-1:1, the key ablation (CAME vs CAME\* using only 1:1 homologs).**
  - Zebrafish-associated pairs: removing non-1:1 lowers accuracy by 1.5-8.7%, 6.26% on average, p = 7.8e-23
    (Results; Fig. S3D, S4B).
  - **Zebrafish-excluded pairs show no difference: p = 0.67 (Fig. S3D).** Fig. S4A gives accuracy p = 0.68,
    macro-F1 p = 0.89 and weighted-F1 p = 0.14.
  - The zebrafish-excluded group holds the human-mouse pairs, including the kidney pairs, the human-macaque pairs and
    the reptile pairs. Its kidney points lie on the diagonal (Fig. S4A).
  - Non-1:1 homologs make up 60-75% of the informative genes (HIGs) in zebrafish pairs but only 15-40% in other pairs
    (Results; Fig. S3C).
  - Fig. S3B lists 16,622 human-mouse homologous genes (69.63%).
  - The Discussion places the benefit in distant species with "a large number of gene duplications". One chick vs
    zebrafish example notes that CD74 and its two zebrafish homologs cd74a and cd74b are "more conserved than other
    DEGs" (Results, retina). This is anecdotal.
- **Limitations stated.** CAME needs a correctly annotated reference and cannot run unsupervised. Ortholog resources
  disagree with each other (Discussion).
- **Limitations for our design.**
  - It is a reference-to-query classifier, and human-mouse PT segments already transfer trivially.
  - Cell-gene edges are binary non-zero indicators. On summed tubule cross-sections almost every expressed gene is
    non-zero, so these edges would carry little information. This is our inference; it was not tested.
  - Species-specific panels are allowed, because the gene sets differ and only 1:1 DEGs are needed as cell features.
    Probe-ambiguous paralogs and continuous coordinates are not addressed.
  - Gene modules have no null, so nothing is comparable to our matched-random-gene nulls.

## Fit to our question
CAME's own ablation is the most direct evidence in these three papers: adding non-1:1 homologs gives no measurable gain
for non-zebrafish pairs (p = 0.67), human-mouse kidney included. The gain appears only where non-1:1 genes are 60-75%
of the informative genes (zebrafish). CAME has no paralog-level output or test, and its gene modules are untested Leiden
clusters. It would add nothing to our orthogroup, family-sum and substitution tests. Instead, it supports keeping a 1:1
shared space for human vs mouse.
