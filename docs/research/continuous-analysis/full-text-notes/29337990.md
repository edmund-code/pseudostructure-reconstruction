## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model: Dirichlet-process mixture; each cluster h is a GP with its own mean function, squared-exponential length-scale, signal variance and marginal (noise) variance, with conjugate-style priors (inverse gamma, default shape 12, rate 2). Concentration alpha is fixed at 1 ("favors a relatively small number of clusters, where the expected number of clusters scales as alpha log P"). Gaussian residuals.
- Inference: Gibbs sampling (Neal's Algorithm 8, 4 empty clusters per iteration), two burn-in phases, kernel hyper-parameters updated by type-II maximum likelihood (L-BFGS) every s-th iteration, thinning s = 3, default 1,000 iterations. fDPGP: a faster per-gene marginal-likelihood version when all genes share time points (used for the real data).
- Outputs: MAP partition; posterior similarity matrix (PSM) = share of Gibbs samples in which two genes co-cluster; per-gene probability of inclusion in its assigned cluster; cluster mean with 95% band.
- Input: trajectories standardised to zero mean and unit variance per gene. H. salinarum: "Mean expression across replicates was standardized"; A549: triplicates, DE by DESeq2 per time point, genes DE at two consecutive time points (1,216 transcripts), standardised.
- Null / calibration: none (clustering). Validation by ARI on 620 simulated GP data sets (Gaussian errors) and 500 with t errors (df 2): DPGP beat GIMM, k-means and Mclust but was generally beaten by BHC and SplineCluster with Gaussian errors; with t errors it beat BHC, SplineCluster, Mclust and hierarchical clustering but was beaten by GIMM and k-means; "no algorithm outperformed all others". Dropping genes with low inclusion probability improved ARI. Held-out time points fell within the 95% bands at >= 90% in most settings. The posterior "was not strongly peaked at the true partition" in nearly all simulations. Biological validation by recapitulating RosR biology and by TF/histone features predicting cluster membership.
- Stated assumptions: stationary cluster trajectories; exchangeable trajectories; each gene in exactly one cluster; same time points for all genes; Gaussian residuals.
- Plots: per-cluster member trajectories with posterior cluster mean +/- 2 SD (Figs 2-3); PSM heat maps ordered by Ward linkage (Fig. 2M,N); elastic-net coefficient heat maps (Figs 4-5).
- Software: Python, github.com/PrincetonUniversity/DP_GP_cluster.
- Limitations for us: Gaussian on standardised profiles (no counts, no specimens); alpha fixed, so the number of clusters is still a prior choice; hard single-cluster membership in the model (the PSM gives the soft view).

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
Useful only as an exploratory, level-free grouping of fitted (standardised) PT curves, with the PSM as a stability display; a pathway-restricted PSM is a direct check for sub-programs before taking a median curve. It adds no specimen-level inference; agreement across specimens would have to be shown by clustering each specimen's curves and comparing PSMs.
