## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic
  - Inputs: log1p-normalized cell x gene matrices for a reference R and a query Q, plus one pseudotime per cell in
    each system (Overview of the G2G framework; Fig. 2 legend).
  - Interpolation: each system's pseudotime is min-max scaled to [0,1]; m equispaced points are set (m chosen by
    optBinning, 13-15 in the examples); at each point a Gaussian (weighted mean and s.d.) is estimated from all
    cells with Gaussian-kernel weights (window_size 0.1, taken from CellAlign); 50 values are sampled from it
    (Methods, "Preprocessing ... by distributional interpolation"; "Window_size").
  - Match cost: an MML (Wallace-Freeman) compression statistic, per datum, between hypothesis A (one Gaussian
    explains both time points' data; made symmetric by averaging the two choices) and hypothesis Phi (two separate
    Gaussians). It reacts to differences in both mean and variance (Methods, "Casting Cost_match under MML";
    "Computing the Shannon information"). Priors: uniform mu over a range of 15, log-sigma over a range of 3.
  - Alignment: Gotoh's affine-gap DP extended with DTW warp states. Five states: M (1-1 match), V (1-to-many
    expansion warp), W (many-to-1 compression warp), I (insertion), D (deletion) (Fig. 1b; Methods, "A new DP
    algorithm"). State-transition costs are -log Pr from a symmetric five-state machine with 3 free parameters,
    set by grid search on simulated dataset 1: Pr(M|M)=0.99, Pr(I|I)=0.1, Pr(M|I)=0.7 ("Five-state machine").
  - Outputs: one five-state string per gene; "alignment similarity" = % of M/V/W in the string; pairwise
    normalized Levenshtein distances between strings, agglomerative clustering (average linkage) with the
    threshold chosen by a manual trade-off on the mean silhouette ("Clustering alignment patterns";
    "Hierarchical clustering of alignment strings requires a tradeoff"); an aggregate (cell-level) alignment over
    all genes or any subset, built by walking back through the genes' optimal-cost landscape matrices and taking
    the most frequent state at each cell ("Aggregate alignment").
  - A mismatch (I/D) means either an unobserved state OR a significantly different expression distribution at
    that time point (Main, 2nd paragraph). So a pure level difference produces mismatches.
- Null / calibration / significance
  - No P value or null for an alignment, a warp or a mismatch. Alignment similarity is descriptive.
  - Pathway ORA: top mismatched genes (alignment similarity <= 40%), and optionally gene clusters, are tested
    with Enrichr (GSEApy) hypergeometric test, BH FDR < 0.05, against MSigDB Hallmark 2020 and KEGG 2021 Human
    ("Pathway over-representation analysis").
  - Only in the TNF validation: an empirical null of MML distances from random pairs of 50-cell subsets of the same
    cell type (137,100 pairs over 1,371 TFs), ECDF P values, BH; then a Mann-Whitney U test on distances before vs
    after TNF ("Estimating an empirical null distribution of MML distances"). Cells are the units.
- Replicates / multiple samples handled? No. Cells from many donors are pooled into one reference and one query
  (IPF case: 3,157 healthy cells from 28 individuals vs 890 IPF cells from 31 individuals; Methods, "Dataset for
  healthy versus IPF").
- Validation
  - Experiment 1: GP-simulated 3,500 gene pairs (matching, early/mid/late divergence, early/mid/late convergence;
    300 cells each; 15 points). G2G accuracy 98.2-100% per pattern vs TrAGEDy 5.4-100%; clustering at threshold
    0.22 gives 15 clusters with 0.1% mis-clustering (Fig. 3f,g). The five-state parameters were tuned on this same
    simulated dataset ("Five-state machine"; Experiment 1).
  - Experiment 2: mouse pancreas beta lineage (1,845 cells, 769 genes) split at random into R and Q, with
    simulated deletions or shifts at the trajectory start. Flat expression stretches produced warps instead of
    mismatches (Extended Data Fig. 3d).
  - Experiment 3 (negative control): two DynGen trajectories with no shared process; G2G aggregate = 100% mismatch,
    TrAGEDy falsely called matched segments (Fig. 3h).
  - Real data: PAM vs LPS dendritic cells (aggregate alignment over 99 "core antiviral" genes, Fig. 4b; 89
    "peaked inflammatory" genes clustered into 7 clusters, Fig. 4d, ED Fig. 4); healthy AT2->AT1 vs IPF AT2->ABC
    (994 HVGs; ORA of top mismatched genes -> EMT; aggregate alignment over all EMT genes, Fig. 5d); in vitro
    ATO vs in vivo T cells (1,371 TFs; ORA -> TNF via NF-kB; aggregate alignment over the 196 TNF-pathway genes,
    ED Fig. 10a); small TNF-addition experiment (116 treated T cells).
  - Benchmark caveat: CellAlign and TrAGEDy were run by the G2G authors with Euclidean distance, although TrAGEDy
    recommends Spearman ("Benchmarking against CellAlign and TrAGEDy").
- Plot types: five-state alignment strings (Fig. 1d, Fig. 2); pairwise time-point matrix coloured by the number
  of genes matched at each R/Q pair, with the average path (Figs 4b, 5b-d, 6b-d); stacked cell-composition bars per
  pseudotime point with match lines and boxed mismatches; interpolated expression plots with 50 sampled points per
  time point; Levenshtein clustermaps and silhouette-vs-threshold diagnostics (ED Figs 2, 4, 5, 9); alignment
  similarity vs log2 fold change scatter (Fig. 4f); z-scored pathway heatmaps along pseudotime (Figs 5d, 6c,d).
- Software: Python package Genes2Genes, https://github.com/Teichlab/Genes2Genes; analysis notebooks
  https://github.com/Teichlab/G2G_notebooks (v0.1.0 used; v0.2.0 faster).
- Limitations the authors state: depends on pseudotime quality; kernel interpolation could be adaptive; gaps in
  cell density break the smooth-trajectory assumption; only two linear trajectories; results depend on which
  genes are aligned (Discussion).
- Limitations for OUR design:
  - No replicate model and no null for alignments; with 2 vs 2 specimens nothing says whether a mismatch exceeds
    mouse-to-mouse variation.
  - The cost compares absolute log1p distributions (mean and variance). A species-specific probe-panel level
    offset will be called a mismatch, so human-vs-mouse use needs per-gene standardization (or ranks) first.
  - Flat stretches yield warps (ED Fig. 3d), so a "warp" in a flat region is not a timing shift.
  - Aggregate alignment is a majority vote over genes; a minority of strongly mismatched members disappears.
  - Gaussian-on-log1p per unit; our units are aggregated counts, which fits better than single cells, but there
    is no count model or offset.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Useful as a descriptive "where do conditions match or diverge" picture for IR vs control within mouse (same panel).
For human vs mouse it needs per-gene standardization within species, because the MML cost scores level offsets as
mismatches. It has no replicate model or alignment null, so any use needs our own specimen-level null (for example
mouse-to-mouse and IR-to-IR alignments as the reference distribution). Five-state defaults were tuned on the
authors' own simulation, so accuracy figures are optimistic.
