## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Data: 1,422 single epidermal cells (Fluidigm C1, STRT UMI protocol) from dorsal skin of n = 19 female C57BL/6 mice in second telogen (Results "Single-Cell Transcriptome Analysis"; Methods "Mice").
- Clustering: affinity propagation (AP) on Pearson correlation of log2 data, 2,500 CV-selected features; 13 first-level groups, then second-level subclustering of IB, OB, uHF and IFE-basal cells to 25 (sub)populations (Methods (2), (8b)-(8e)).
- Axes: both the differentiation pseudotime (536 IFE cells) and the proximal-distal "pseudospace" (486 basal IFE + HF cells) are built the same way, from expression only: t-SNE, minimum spanning tree, diameter path, PQ-tree ordering (Monocle-1 style, Trapnell 2014) (Methods (5a)). The pseudospace is an expression-derived ordering of cells, not a tissue coordinate; it is validated against known markers and IHC/FISH localisation (Fig. 4B, 4E).
- Per-gene test: a cubic smoothing spline with 5 effective df per gene (VGAM, via rpy2), compared with a position-independent restricted model by an approximate likelihood-ratio test; Bonferroni alpha = 0.001 (Methods (5c)). Results: 1,627 pseudotime-dependent genes, 547 pseudospace-dependent genes (Results; Methods (8h), (8j)).
- Gene grouping: spline-smoothed profiles AP-clustered into 8 groups per axis; genes ordered by peak or by onset (onset = where expression first exceeds 50% of the peak) (Methods (5c)). Group-level MSigDB "Investigate Gene Sets" overlap (FDR q < 0.05) for function (Methods (7)).
- Two-axis "pseudospacetime" model: every cell placed on both axes by maximal Pearson correlation to the spline model, using genes unique to one axis (1,409 time, 329 space). Each axis is cut into 15 equal bins; bin membership is used as predictors in a Bayesian negative binomial regression (Stan; Zeisel 2015 model) with a library-size "Baseline" predictor, plus 5 extra predictors (SG, SG opening, immune) (Methods (8k)-(8l)).
- Model comparison: the same NB model with first- or second-level cluster labels as predictors; posterior simulation counts "explained / under- / over-explained molecules" (Methods (4c), (8m); Fig. 5D-E). Result: pseudospacetime had "an equally high 'explanatory performance'" as the first- and second-level clustering; baseline about 50% of molecules, differentiation adds about 25%, and with the spatial signatures "more than 95%" are explained (Results "The Differentiation and Spatial Signatures Explain Most Epidermal Heterogeneity"). Negative control: shuffled predictor matrix.
- Null / calibration: per-gene LRT P values with Bonferroni over genes; cells are the units. No specimen-level test. Cluster robustness by 25% cell resampling (100x) against permuted labels; ordering robustness by 100 t-SNE re-runs, 25% cell drop-out and shuffled labels (Methods (2c), (5b)).
- Replicates: 19 mice pooled at the cell level. The only replicate analysis checks that each cluster contains cells from enough mice: "cells of at least eight different mice formed each cluster", with a resampling p-value per population against the expected number of mice (Results; Methods (8g) table). Replicates do not enter the spline tests or the regression.
- Validation: IHC and RNAscope FISH on sections from the same mice for 24 of 25 populations (uHF III placed by exclusion) (Methods "Systematic Staining").
- Plot types: t-SNE maps (Fig. 1D, 2A-D); MST ordering in t-SNE (Fig. 3A, 4A); expression vs pseudotime/pseudospace with spline (Fig. 3B, 4B); "rolling wave" heatmaps of spline-smoothed genes clustered into 8 groups and ordered by peak (Fig. 3C, 4C), and of the 30 top TFs with P values (Fig. 3D, 4D); **kernel density of each population's position along the spatial axis** (Fig. 4E); a 2-D pseudospacetime matrix with per-bin gene counts (Fig. 5A-B); explained-molecule bars (Fig. 5D-E); bar plots of differentiation-group genes per population (Fig. 3E).
- Software: Python (NumPy, SciPy, pandas, matplotlib, scikit-learn for AP and t-SNE, NetworkX), R VGAM for splines and LRT, Stan for the NB model; scripts at https://github.com/kasperlab; data GEO GSE67602 (Methods (1); Key Resources).
- Limitations (authors): AP parameters chosen partly by "subjective measures"; FISH less sensitive than scRNA-seq; the continuous spatial profile "does not exclude the existence of compartmental borders" (Discussion). Limitations for OUR design: cell-level tests with no specimen replication; the axis is fixed when testing genes; the continuous-vs-cluster comparison is dataset-level (explained molecules), not per gene, and the continuous model itself is binned (15 bins).

## Review check
- c-spatial-gradients.md:25 (row #10), "Heterogeneity decomposes along two axes: a differentiation axis (pseudotime) and a proximal-distal spatial axis of the follicle. The authors model each axis's share of heterogeneity." - VERDICT: correct (Fig. 5E: baseline ~50%, differentiation +25%, with spatial >95%).
- c:25, "Spatial signatures are confirmed by FISH and IHC." - VERDICT: correct but imprecise. FISH/IHC confirm the location of the 25 populations; the spatial ordering itself is checked with known markers (Fig. 4B) and population densities (Fig. 4E).
- c:25, Plot types "t-SNE; axis-ordered heatmaps; 2-D 'spatial x differentiation' maps." - VERDICT: correct but misses an important point: Fig. 4E plots kernel densities of discrete populations along the reconstructed spatial axis, which is the "label densities along the axis" idea the review credits only to the MERFISH paper (#11, idea 6).
- c:25, Replicates "≥8 mice per cluster" - VERDICT: correct as stated ("cells of at least eight different mice formed each cluster"), but misses that cells from 19 mice are pooled and that no test uses mice as replicates.
- c:25, Software "Affinity propagation (custom)" - VERDICT: wrong / incomplete. Replacement: "Python (scikit-learn AP and t-SNE, NetworkX), R VGAM splines, Stan NB regression; github.com/kasperlab."
- c:25, What it adds "Template for two-axis framing: PT position x injury state in AKI" - VERDICT: correct but misses an important point. Joost is also a direct precedent for our core comparison: they fit the same NB model once with binned continuous axes and once with cluster labels, and found the continuous model explained the data "equally" well (not better). The per-gene test is a 5-df smoothing spline LRT, Bonferroni, cells as units.
- Proposed replacement text for the "What it does" and "What it adds" cells:
  "Epidermis and hair follicle scRNA-seq (1,422 cells, 19 mice). Two expression-derived axes, each built by an MST through t-SNE: differentiation pseudotime and a proximal-distal 'pseudospace' of basal cells. Per gene, a 5-df smoothing spline is tested against a flat model by LRT (Bonferroni 0.001; 547 spatial genes). Genes are clustered into 8 groups and shown as 'rolling wave' heatmaps ordered by peak or onset. Each axis is cut into 15 bins and used in an NB regression; it explains the data as well as the cluster labels do (baseline ~50%, +25% differentiation, >95% with space)." / "What it adds: an early precedent for an expression-derived spatial axis, for KDE plots of discrete populations along it (Fig. 4E), and for comparing a continuous-axis model with cluster labels. Their comparison was dataset-wide and found a tie, not a win, and it used cells, not mice, as units. A two-axis (position x injury) framing for AKI follows the same logic."

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
Closer than the review says: like ours, the spatial axis is reconstructed from expression alone, and the paper asks "clusters or continuous axes?" with one NB model. Their answer (a tie in explained molecules, pooled cells, no specimen replication) is a useful, honest benchmark for our "continuous beats discrete" claim: we should expect to show it with a calibrated per-gene or per-pathway comparison, not by default. The KDE-of-labels-along-axis figure (Fig. 4E) is a direct template.
