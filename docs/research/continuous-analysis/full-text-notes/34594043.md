## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: build a kNN graph in PCA space; sample index cells with a refined scheme; each neighbourhood = an index cell plus its graph neighbours (overlapping). Count cells per neighbourhood per experimental sample (N x S matrix). Fit an NB GLM per neighbourhood with edgeR's quasi-likelihood framework, TMM normalisation for different cell numbers per sample, and a QL F-test for a chosen contrast; dispersion is shared across neighbourhoods (Methods C-D). Multiple testing by a "spatial FDR": weighted BH with weights = reciprocal of neighbourhood connectivity (distance to the k-th nearest neighbour), adapted from Cydar (Methods E).
- Null / calibration: parametric QL F-test; the replicate unit is the experimental sample, so biological replicates are required ("Biological replicates are required to estimate the negative binomial dispersion parameter for each neighbourhood", Discussion). Batch and other nuisance covariates enter the GLM.
- Replicates: yes, samples are the unit. Benchmarks used 3 simulated replicates per condition, described as "a minimal number of replicates required to estimate a variance parameter" (Results, benchmark section). Continuous covariates (age as a linear predictor in thymus) are supported.
- Validation: semi-synthetic benchmarks (discrete clusters, linear and branching dyntoy trajectories, mouse gastrulation kNN graph with simulated condition labels) against Louvain-cluster DA, Cydar, DAseq and MELD; Milo had high TPR with FDR control and was the only method to keep FDR control under uncorrected batch effects when batch was in the GLM (Fig. 2E; Extended Data Fig. 4). MELD underestimated fold changes (Extended Data Fig. 3). Real data: ageing mouse thymus (2,327 cells, 5 ages x 5 replicates; 208/363 DA neighbourhoods at 10% FDR) and human cirrhotic liver (5 healthy, 5 cirrhotic; 1,351/2,676 neighbourhoods DA at 10% FDR), with neighbourhoods of opposite DA direction inside single published subclusters (Fig. 5D).
- Plots: an abstracted neighbourhood graph (nodes = neighbourhoods placed at index-cell UMAP positions, size = cells, edges = shared cells, coloured by log fold change) (Methods "Visualization of DA neighbourhoods"); per-cluster DA summaries (Fig. 4D, 5D; the beeswarm form is not verifiable from this text because main legends are missing); grouping of concordant neighbourhoods for marker DE (Fig. 4E-F, 5E-F).
- Software: R package miloR (https://github.com/MarioniLab/miloR; Bioconductor >= 3.13); analysis code at https://github.com/MarioniLab/milo_analysis_2020.
- Limitations stated: needs biological replicates; must avoid confounding technical factors (including number of cells acquired per condition) with condition; a state may span several neighbourhoods; cluster-level DA may be preferable for large obvious differences. Limitations for OUR design: Milo works on an expression kNN graph, not on a 1-D anatomical coordinate; with 2 vs 2 specimens the per-neighbourhood dispersion is weakly estimated (shared-dispersion borrowing helps); TMM-normalised counts measure relative, compositional abundance; for tubule cross-sections, the number of units per axis window also depends on sectioning geometry, not only biology.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Good template for the unit-density check: per-specimen counts in overlapping windows, an NB GLM with specimens as replicates, and a spatial FDR over overlapping windows. Use the axis windows instead of an expression kNN graph. With 2 vs 2 mice, dispersion must be shared across windows and calibration checked by specimen relabeling; window counts also reflect sectioning geometry.
