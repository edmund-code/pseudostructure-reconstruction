## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: NMF (scikit-learn) run R times (200 in simulation, 500 on real data) with different seeds on the 2,000 most over-dispersed genes, each variance-scaled but not log-transformed or mean-centred; no per-cell count normalization. Components from all runs are l2-normalized, outliers (high mean distance to the nearest rho = 30% of runs) filtered with a threshold tau chosen from a histogram, k-means clustered into K groups, and the median of each cluster is the consensus program. Usages are refit by non-negative least squares with programs fixed and normalized to sum to 1 per cell; programs are then refit in TPM on all genes (Methods). Gene-program association = OLS of z-scored TPM on the usage matrix (P values not used). Gene-set enrichment = one-sided Mann-Whitney U on the z-score coefficients (negatives floored to 0).
- Identity vs activity: not an algorithmic output. The authors label a program "activity" when it is co-used with many uncorrelated programs and spread across published clusters, "identity" otherwise (Results, organoid and cortex sections).
- K selection: stability (silhouette) vs Frobenius error plot plus a scree plot, then judgement on interpretability; results stable within about +/-4 of the chosen K (Fig. 5).
- Null / calibration: none for programs; simulations measure accuracy.
- Replicates: not modelled. Supplementary note: per-replicate aggregated usage is inspected descriptively; on a pancreas data set with donor batch effects, cNMF learned hybrid "cell type x donor group" programs (Fig. 5-figure supplement 2), so the authors recommend batch correction before cNMF.
- Validation: Splatter-based simulation (15,000 cells, 13 types, one activity program in 30% of cells of 4 types, 6% doublets, 20 replicates x 3 noise levels): cNMF best at recovering the activity program and its genes (61% sensitivity at 5% FDR vs 56% for an oracle hard clustering); PCA components are mixtures of programs. Real data: 52,600 brain organoid cells (31 programs incl. G1/S, G2/M, hypoxia); 15,011 visual cortex neurons (20 programs incl. early and two late depolarization programs, and new "neurosecretory" and "synaptogenesis" programs), replicated in Tasic et al.; pancreas (10,939 cells) in the response to reviewers.
- Plots: usage heatmaps (programs x cells grouped by identity program); tSNE coloured by usage; GO-enrichment tables; gene z-score heatmaps; proportion of cells per type using an activity program; K-selection diagnostics; robustness-to-K line plots; per-replicate usage heatmaps.
- Software: Python, https://github.com/dylkot/cNMF; simulator https://github.com/dylkot/scsim; Code Ocean capsule.
- Limitations (authors): linear mixture only (no repression); not count-based; usages not sparse; K needs judgement; batch effects create hybrid programs; Discussion notes that "a cell's position along a morphological gradient" blurs the identity/activity distinction. For us: PT position is exactly such a gradient, so the identity/activity split does not map onto positional programs; specimens and species would split programs unless corrected.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
A robust way to get data-driven programs with per-unit usages, which could then be fitted along PT per specimen. But it models a linear mixture of discrete-ish programs, not smooth positional change, and specimen or species differences would surface as separate programs. Low priority for us relative to curve-based summaries.
