# Part A. Single-gene dynamics along a continuous axis: describing, classifying, ordering, visualising

Literature review, 2026-10-14. I checked every PMID with `lit.py summary`; title, year and journal match.
Method details come from abstracts or open full text (`lit.py grep`). Entries without a PMID give the
URL or DOI I opened.

"Replicates?" asks whether the method models **samples/specimens** as the replicate unit. "Cells as
independent observations" counts as **no**.

## (b) Concrete ideas we could adopt (method → the figure it produces)

1. **Shape classes with probabilities, as a model-selection problem across specimens.**
   *Sources:* Droin 2021, ImpulseDE2, TDEseq, Ouija.
   - For each gene, fit a small family of NB models on the PT coordinate. Every model has specimen offsets and the same exposure offset.
   - The family: (i) flat; (ii) monotone sigmoid, switchde-style, with t0 and k; (iii) impulse or bump, ImpulseDE2's two-sigmoid or Ouija's Gaussian; (iv) shape-constrained monotone, convex or concave (`scam`); (v) a free spline.
   - Assign classes with AIC/BIC weights, so every gene gets P(flat), P(monotone up or down), P(peak), P(trough) and P(complex).
   - This extends our logistic edge fits. Transient genes become their own class instead of failing a sigmoid fit. Each call carries a probability instead of a hard threshold.
   - *Figure:* a "pattern atlas". One panel per class, with thin per-specimen curves and the class median. Next to it, a stacked bar of class composition per species and condition. Then an **alluvial plot** of class membership from mouse to human, or from control to AKI, for orthologous genes.

2. **A PT "switch timeline" with positional intervals.**
   *Sources:* GeneSwitches, Ouija, Swain, Simpson.
   - For monotone genes, estimate the half-maximum position t0 and the 10–90% width. For transient genes, estimate the peak position and the full width at half maximum.
   - Get intervals by refitting per specimen and by posterior simulation of the NB-GAM coefficients. Refitting the elastic curve on subsamples (PseudotimeDE) would also capture coordinate uncertainty.
   - Report P(A before B) for chosen marker pairs, as Ouija does.
   - *Figure:* a horizontal PT axis with S1/S2/S3 drawn as background bands. Up-switching genes sit above the axis and down-switching genes below. Each point is t0, the bar is its 90% interval, and colour shows steepness. Draw mouse and human mirrored on the same axis.

3. **Derivative maps and a "change-density" track.**
   *Sources:* Simpson 2018, SiZer, Trendy, scLANE.
   - Compute d log μ / d position for each gene and program, with **simultaneous** bands from posterior simulation.
   - Mark positions where the band excludes zero.
   - Sum across genes into a track: the number of genes significantly rising or falling at each position, which plays the role of Trendy's breakpoint density.
   - *Figures:* (i) each gene curve stacked over its derivative panel, with significant stretches shaded; (ii) the genome-wide change-density track with the annotated S1/S2 and S2/S3 boundaries overlaid. This tests directly whether change is confined to the boundaries (discrete is enough) or spread along PT (continuous adds information). (iii) SiZer maps for showcase genes, showing that an "edge" holds across smoothing scales.

4. **Split each contrast into mean shift vs trend difference, and locate where curves diverge.**
   *Sources:* Lamian, scFates, tradeSeq/condiments, Heinonen nsgp, DEtime.
   - For AKI vs control and human vs mouse, split each gene's difference curve into a constant offset and a shape part. Report meanSig, trendSig and bothSig.
   - With 2 vs 2 mice, use the effect sizes descriptively and keep our balanced specimen-relabeling control as the calibration. Do not quote Lamian-style permutation p-values.
   - Then find **where** the curves differ: the intervals where the simultaneous band on the difference curve excludes zero.
   - *Figures:* the Lamian six-heatmap layout (raw per group, fitted per group, trend difference, mean shift). A "divergence cascade": one horizontal segment per gene covering where AKI differs from control, sorted by start position. This shows whether the injury signature starts in S3 and spreads, or is uniform.

5. **Model pathway programs hierarchically instead of taking a median of centred curves.**
   *Sources:* Hensman 2013, DPGP, moanin, Mfuzz.
   - For a curated pathway, fit a pathway curve, gene deviations from it, and specimen deviations from each gene, as an NB-GAM with nested factor smooths or a hierarchical GP.
   - Report the program curve with a credible band, plus a coherence measure: the share of member-gene shape variance explained by the pathway curve.
   - For data-driven programs, cluster [0,1]-rescaled fitted curves (moanin), use soft membership (Mfuzz), or use DPGP to get a co-clustering matrix.
   - *Figures:* program ribbons with the member-gene curves faintly behind them. A DPGP co-clustering heatmap restricted to one pathway's members, which shows sub-programs.

6. **Heatmap conventions that suit continuous data.**
   *Sources:* Ben-Moshe/Halpern COM sorting, CellRank 2 peak sorting, condiments/Lamian side-by-side images, TrendCatcher TimeHeatmap.
   - Split the gene heatmap into blocks by shape class from idea 1. Within the blocks: decreasing genes sorted by t0, transient genes by peak position, increasing genes by t0. Use COM when a gene's class is uncertain.
   - This gives a clean cascade without the argmax artefact, where monotone genes pile up at the two ends.
   - Add side annotations for steepness (10–90% width) and class probability.
   - Draw human and mouse heatmaps mirrored with the **same row order**, each scaled to [0,1].
   - For pathways, draw a TimeHeatmap: pathways × PT windows, coloured by the sign and size of the significant derivative.
   - Add a mean-expression vs COM scatter (Ben-Moshe) as an overview.

## (c) Gaps: what nobody seems to have done

- **Onset/peak positions with specimen-level uncertainty when there are very few specimens.** Two groups of methods exist, and neither fits.
  - switchde, GeneSwitches and Ouija treat cells as replicates.
  - Lamian and scLANE's GLMM give samples random effects but need many samples: Lamian needs S − (V+1) ≥ K+1, and scLANE needs random slopes per subject.
  - Nothing targets 2 vs 2 specimens with thousands of aggregated units each. Specimen fixed effects plus relabeling controls remain our own construction.
- **A one-dimensional *anatomical* axis built from repeated structures.** The only comparable precedents are the liver zonation papers (layers, with mice as replicates). The pseudotime tools assume single cells, branching, or real time.
  - Trendy and GPcounts say they handle "spatial" data. Trendy's means a spatial ordering of conditions; GPcounts' means 2-D tissue coordinates.
  - None treats the units as aggregates of segmented tubules with an exposure offset.
- **Testing continuous vs discrete gene by gene.** I found no method that asks of each gene whether a step function at the *known* S1/S2/S3 boundaries explains it as well as a smooth or free-position sigmoid curve. Close relatives:
  - Ouija's metastable states and scFates' linearity-deviation test.
  - Trendy's and scLANE's estimated breakpoints, which could be compared against the annotated boundaries.
- **Comparing shape classes and onset positions across species measured on different probe panels.** Comparison methods (condiments, Lamian, maSigPro group terms) assume the same measurement in both groups. Level-free summaries such as onset, peak, COM, width and shape class are the natural cross-species currency, but nobody has formalised a test for them.
- **Pathway-level dynamics with uncertainty.** TrendCatcher's TimeHeatmap rests on enrichment in time windows. The hierarchical-GP machinery (Hensman 2013) exists but, as far as I found, has not been used to give a pathway curve with a credible band and a gene-coherence measure.
- **Propagating coordinate uncertainty into onset and peak intervals.** PseudotimeDE propagates it into p-values only. I found no method that carries it into switch- or peak-position intervals.
