KIDNEY PANOPTIC SEGMENTATION — MODEL SUMMARY (v4 checkpoint)
================================================================================
Written 2026-07-30.
Checkpoint:  runs/v4_panoptic/best_pq.pt   (epoch 35 of 54 run)
Config:      configs/default.yaml  <- configs/data.yaml
Manifest:    data/manifest_v4.csv

A note on names: the CHECKPOINT is called v4 because it is the fourth training round
of this system. The code was developed in a `model_v2/` subtree (the second-generation
rewrite) and was promoted to the repository root once the first-generation HoVer-Net
model was retired; the two numbers were never the same and the v2 name is now gone.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT IT DOES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Input:   an H&E mouse-kidney whole-slide image at ~0.44 um/px (20x).
Output:  every tubule and glomerulus on the slide as a SEPARATE, individually
         outlined object, with a class label, exported as QuPath-loadable GeoJSON
         plus a slide-wide instance map.

"Panoptic instance segmentation" means two things at once:
  - SEMANTIC: which class is this pixel?
  - INSTANCE: which specific object is this pixel part of?

The second is the hard one and the reason this is not just a pixel classifier. Two
tubules that touch are one connected blob of "tubule" pixels. Splitting that blob
into two counted objects is the entire problem.

Classes (5, index 0 is background):
    background
    tubule_proximal      (annotation label "Tubules")
    tubule_distal        (annotation label "Tubules2")
    tubule_collecting    (annotation label "Tubules3")
    glomerulus           (annotation label "Glomeruli")

Vessel / Blood Cells / Nerve are IGNORED — see section 8.

Class labels are an AUXILIARY task, not the deliverable. The stated goal is
segmentation quality. The classes exist because the extra supervision helps the
shared decoder and because they make the output readable in QuPath. This is why
checkpoint selection uses a CLASS-AGNOSTIC score (section 6).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. ARCHITECTURE — three parts
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  WSI ──> [ 512 px patch ]
             │
             ├──> FROZEN ENCODER (OpenMidnight ViT-g/14 with registers)
             │       518 px input -> 37x37 tokens, 1536-dim
             │       intermediate blocks [9, 19, 29, 39] pulled out as a pyramid
             │       0 trainable parameters
             │
             ├──> TRAINABLE RGB STEM (strides 1, 2, 4, 8, 16)
             │       channels [32, 64, 128, 192, 256]
             │
             v
        FPN FUSION + DECODER  (7.31 M trainable parameters)
             ViT tokens enter at stride 16; decoder climbs back to STRIDE 1
             decoder channels [192, 128, 96, 64], GroupNorm, dropout 0.10
             │
             ├──> SEMANTIC HEAD    5 channels  (per-pixel class)
             ├──> BOUNDARY HEAD    4 channels  (see below)
             └──> CENTER HEAD      1 channel   (glomerulus only)

2a. Why a frozen foundation encoder
    OpenMidnight is a pathology-pretrained DINOv2 ViT-g/14. It already knows what
    H&E tissue looks like. Freezing it means the 6-slide training set only has to
    fit 7.31 M decoder parameters instead of ~1.1 B, which is the difference
    between "trainable" and "hopeless" at this data scale.

2b. Why the RGB stem exists (ADR 0002)
    A ViT at patch size 14 sees nothing finer than 14 px. A tubule wall is 3-5 px.
    The stem is a parallel convolutional path over the RAW image that carries
    full-resolution detail into the decoder as skip connections. Without it the
    output is a blurry stride-8 blob map. Output stride is 1 — predictions are at
    the same resolution as the input, not upsampled from a coarse grid.

2c. The boundary head is the instance mechanism (ADR 0001)
    4 channels per pixel:
        0  background
        1  interior              <- the seed
        2  boundary-to-background  (the object's outer contour)
        3  boundary-to-INSTANCE    (the wall where two objects touch)

    Channel 3 is the important one. The wall between two touching tubules is the
    single thing that must fire to prevent them being merged into one object, so
    it gets its own output rather than being a slightly upweighted subset of a
    channel dominated by ordinary outer contours.

    This REPLACES the HoVer-Net centroid-offset (HV) representation used by the
    older system in the repo root. HV predicts, for each pixel, a vector to its
    object's centre; instances then fall out of the discontinuities in that field.
    It works, but it is non-local — the correct value at a pixel depends on where
    the object's far side is — which breaks when tiles disagree, and it cannot be
    averaged across tiles or orientations without inventing values. The boundary
    formulation is purely local. That single property is what makes both seamless
    tiling (section 5) and test-time augmentation (section 5b) possible.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. SUPERVISION — the three-way ignore mask (ADR 0003)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The annotations are PARTIAL. Nobody drew every tubule on a 32000x18000 slide.
Treating unannotated pixels as background would teach the model that real tubules
are background. Every pixel therefore gets one of three labels:

    TRUSTED FOREGROUND  annotated instances
    TRUSTED BACKGROUND  a 5 px ring around each instance, PLUS all off-tissue glass
    IGNORED             tissue with no annotation — contributes no gradient at all

Including glass as trusted background is what stops the model hallucinating
objects on blank slide. It was measured: the earlier strategy that left glass
ignored produced 42.6 instances covering 25.5% of a CONSTANT grey image. With
glass supervised, the same probe returns 0.

All losses are masked and normalised over valid pixels only. A consequence worth
remembering: validation loss is NOT comparable across runs that use different
ignore strategies, because each normalises over a different denominator.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW AN INSTANCE IS PRODUCED (decode)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Marker-controlled watershed, run on the predicted probability maps:

  1. FOREGROUND    (1 - p_background) >= 0.50
  2. SEEDS         connected components of p_interior >= 0.40, area >= 24 px
  3. FLOOD         watershed from those seeds, using boundary probability as the
                   elevation, so the cut lands on image evidence
  4. CLASSIFY      majority vote of the semantic map over each instance's core
                   (boundary band excluded, so wall pixels do not vote)
  5. FILTER        drop instances below a per-class minimum area

One seed becomes exactly one object. This is why merging is a SEEDING failure,
not a flooding failure: if two touching tubules produce a single connected
interior region, no amount of ridge height can split them afterwards.

Per-class minimum area (px^2 at 0.44 um/px):
    default            950
    tubule_proximal   1950
    tubule_distal     1000
    tubule_collecting  950
    glomerulus        2300

These were RECALIBRATED and the previous values were wrong by ~10x. They had been
set from the 5th percentile of instance areas measured INSIDE 512 px training
patches — a distribution dominated by objects the patch border had cut in half.
Measured on whole polygons instead, the 5th percentiles are 3885 / 2026 / 1948 /
4564. The floors are set at half of those: a floor at p5 would discard 5% of real
objects by construction, while half still removes watershed slivers, which sit an
order of magnitude lower.

Two mechanisms exist but are OFF by default, both for measured reasons:
  - semantic_split: splits a blob spanning two tubule classes. Costs 1.6 PQ and
    raises the split rate to buy one merge. Implemented and tested; not enabled.
  - merge_fragments: re-merges adjacent fragments. Merging is the dominant error,
    so a mechanism that merges more is the wrong tool.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHOLE-SLIDE INFERENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5a. Seamless tiling
    Prediction tiles are 512 px with 128 px overlap, combined with a Gaussian
    window so a tile contributes least where it is least reliable. Tiles sit on a
    single slide-global grid, and each decode window is run with a halo of one
    FULL tile — so every pixel receives exactly the contributions it would have
    received from one slide-wide pass. Instances crossing window borders are
    stitched by IoU >= 0.50.

    The halo roughly doubles the forward passes on a 2048 window. It buys an
    exactly seam-free map, which the older root-directory system does not have:
    there, disjoint tile cores create a hard discontinuity in the HV field at
    every seam, producing a measured 3-5x excess of straight cuts on seam lines.

5b. Test-time augmentation (--tta d4)
    The image is predicted 8 times — 4 rotations x 2 mirrors — and the probability
    maps are averaged back in the original orientation.

    Sound ONLY because every predicted field is a per-channel SCALAR map: rotating
    the input rotates each map and nothing else. An HV field could not be averaged
    this way; its vector components would need rotating too, and averaging two
    disagreeing offset fields invents values neither pass predicted.

    Cost is linear: a full slide goes from ~8 min to ~45 min. It is worth it —
    see section 7, where TTA is a larger improvement than the retrain was.

5c. Tissue gate
    A saturation-based tissue mask skips whole tiles of glass. Deliberately
    DISABLED for the constant-input canary, so that test measures the model rather
    than the gate.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. TRAINING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Data:     6475 patches of 512 px, stride 256, from 7 slide-regions
          Nx_4wk_001 1717 | Nx_24h_1 1132 | Nx_4wk_100 1049 | Nx_4wk_050 1009
          Nx_24h_100 495 | Nx_24h_50 453 | Ctrl_1A2 449 | Ctrl_1A2_val 171
Objects:  tubule_proximal 7981 | tubule_distal 2395 | tubule_collecting 1752 |
          glomerulus 1088   (instance-in-patch counts, objects recur across patches)

Optimiser: AdamW, lr 3e-4 cosine to 1e-6, 2 warmup epochs, weight decay 0.03,
           batch 8, AMP, grad clip 1.0, 60 epochs planned.

Augmentation (heavy, because 7 slides overfits fast): full rotation, flips,
anisotropic scale 0.80-1.25, aspect 0.90-1.11, shear 6 deg, translation 128 px,
HED stain jitter (multiplicative alpha 0.10 + additive beta 0.004 OD), HSV,
brightness/contrast, blur, Gaussian noise, JPEG recompression, cutout, elastic
deformation, and copy-paste of rare classes.

  A caution recorded here because it was got wrong once: the HED additive term is
  in OPTICAL-DENSITY units and hed2rgb is EXPONENTIAL. Reasoning about its size in
  OD space badly understates its effect in RGB. Measured mean |dRGB| on real
  patches: alpha-only 3.00, beta as shipped 3.25, beta without the 0.05 scaling
  37.07 (destructive). Measure in RGB, not OD.

CHECKPOINT SELECTION (ADR 0004): best validation PQ, never validation loss.
Ties within 0.005 PQ break toward lower merge rate.

  The PQ used for selection is CLASS-AGNOSTIC — it scores the instance maps alone,
  so a correctly outlined tubule scores the same whether it was called proximal or
  distal. That matches the actual goal. A class-aware score would let a model that
  segments perfectly but confuses two tubule segments lose to one that segments
  worse.

  Validation decodes with min_area scaled to 0.25. Validation runs on 512 px
  patches where border truncation makes many objects undersized; applying the
  slide-scale floor there would delete those predictions while their ground truth
  still counted as a miss. The shipped decode uses the full values.

VALIDATION SET — the biggest measurement change in this round.
  Nx_4wk_050        fully held-out slide
  Ctrl_1A2_val      interleaved 2048 px blocks of Ctrl_1A2

  Ctrl_1A2 contributes to BOTH splits via complementary block sets. A patch is kept
  only if it lies ENTIRELY inside one block, so patches straddling the interface are
  dropped by both — that containment rule is the only thing preventing a validation
  patch from sharing pixels with a training patch at stride 256. It is asserted
  directly in tests/test_roi_split.py, because that leakage would silently RAISE
  validation PQ and look like success.

  Blocks are INTERLEAVED, not one contiguous band. A band split was tried first and
  was wrong: kidney anatomy is spatially organised, the medulla sits at one end of
  the tissue, and the held-out band came out 85% collecting duct — it would have
  measured the medulla and nothing else.

  Honest limitation: adjacent blocks share staining and section artefacts, so
  Ctrl_1A2_val is NOT an independent test of cross-slide generalisation.
  Nx_4wk_050 is the slide that tests that.

  Effect: validation went from 109 objects to 1298. Differences that were
  previously unresolvable now are.

    READ THE "FAIR COMPARISON" ROW, NOT 0.806. v4's scoring no longer contains the
    other_structure objects, because those annotations are now ignored. Comparing
    to v3's all-class number would credit v4 for deleting the class it was worst
    at. Rescored on the same 98-object subset, v3 reads 0.822 — so the retrain
    gain is 0.822 -> 0.837, and roughly half the apparent improvement was
    bookkeeping.

    TTA is worth more than the retrain was: +0.043 PQ, driven by false positives
    collapsing from 7 to 1 and the split rate from 4.1% to 1.0%. Averaging over 8
    orientations kills predictions that only survive in one orientation.

    TTA's merge rate rising 4.1% -> 6.1% is 4 objects versus 6 at n=98. Not a real
    effect.

    Caveat on all of 7b: 98 objects. The 1298-object set in 7a is the one that
    resolves small differences, and it tells the same story — merging 38% -> 12%.

    nephrectomy plus one control). Their proximal-tubule density is 1.4-1.8x the
    controls and mean object area is ~20% smaller, and IR_2A4's collecting-duct
    density is 4x lower than every other slide. That may be real biology, or it may
    be that decode thresholds calibrated on control and nephrectomy morphology do
    not transfer to injured tubules. It has not been checked against ground truth.
    Treat cross-condition comparisons as unvalidated until it is.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY REMOVED, AND WHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Vessel / Blood Cells / Nerve. Previously folded into one "other_structure" class.
It collapsed onto its smallest member: predicted areas capped at p95 = 1847 px
while the annotations run to 26022 px, meaning it learned blood cells and never
predicted a vessel — while emitting 3162 objects on Ctrl_1A2, 14% of the slide
output, that are not per-object interpretable. They are now IGNORED, so those
pixels contribute no gradient in either direction and the decoder capacity goes to
the tubules and glomeruli that are the actual deliverable.

Consequence: v4 does not detect vessels, blood cells or nerves at all. If those
are wanted later they need their own annotation effort, not a folded class.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. KNOWN LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- MERGING IS STILL THE DOMINANT ERROR. 4.1% of ground-truth objects on the held-out
  regions, 12.4% on the patch-level validation set. Down from 7.1% / 38.2%, but
  still the thing most worth fixing. The merges that remain are predominantly
  SAME-subtype, which no class-based mechanism can reach.

- Objects are predicted SMALLER than annotated. Median predicted/annotated area
  ratio ran 0.47-0.82 per class on the v3 checkpoint. Partly fixed by the min_area
  recalibration; not re-measured on v4. If you compute areas or morphometrics from
  this output, that bias is a direct bias in your numbers.

- Seven slides, one scanner, two conditions. Nothing here has been validated across
  scanners, stains, species or organs.

- Encoder never adapted. 7.31 M trainable parameters sit on a completely frozen
  ViT-g/14 that has never seen mouse kidney. This is the largest untapped capacity
  in the system.

- The touching channel under-fires. Measured p(touching) = 0.142 on true walls
  versus 0.014 on ordinary contours — 10x discriminative where it fires, but the
  merges that survive happen exactly where it is silent. That is a recall problem
  on one channel.

10. HOW TO RUN IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Always use the `tubule_segmentation` conda env. Never set PYTHONNOUSERSITE=1 — it
breaks skimage/cv2/shapely in this environment.

Whole slide, best quality (8x TTA, ~45 min for a 32000x18000 slide):

    conda run --no-capture-output -n tubule_segmentation python -u \
      scripts/wsi_infer.py \
      --checkpoint runs/v4_panoptic/best_pq.pt \
      --slide /path/to/slide.tif \
      --out-dir results/<name> \
      --tta d4 --save-viz --gpu 1 \
      --set wsi.level=0 wsi.manual_mpp=0.44068

    Drop --tta for a ~8 min run at lower quality. For .ndpi slides in this cohort
    the 0.44 um/px plane is LEVEL 1, so use wsi.level=1 and omit manual_mpp. The
    flat .tif exports are level 0 and REQUIRE manual_mpp — their only resolution
    tag is a 300-DPI print artefact that the reader refuses on purpose.

Outputs in results/<name>/:
    instances.geojson   QuPath-loadable; exterior rings only, repaired via shapely
    instance_map.npz    slide-wide int32 label map + class_of_id + score_of_id
    summary.json        counts, areas, densities per class
    overlay.png         downsampled visual check
    tissue_mask.png

Held-out evaluation:

    python scripts/eval_regions.py \
      --checkpoint runs/v4_panoptic/best_pq.pt \
      --config configs/data.yaml \
      --manifest data/manifest_eval_nx050.csv \
      --split val --size 4096 --n-regions 5 --gpu 1 --set infer.tta=d4

    Use the Nx_4wk_050-only manifest. eval_regions does not understand the `roi`
    block columns, so pointing it at the full val split lets it sample regions
    overlapping Ctrl_1A2's TRAINING blocks and quietly inflate the score.

Retrain:  bash run_v4_train.sh   (run it in tmux; ~3.5 h)
Tests:    python -m pytest tests/     (164 tests)
Gate:     python scripts/overfit.py --config configs/data.yaml --gpu 1
          Must reach >=10x loss reduction and mean PQ >=0.80 before any long run.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. WHAT TO TRY NEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ranked, given that merging is the priority and no further annotation is planned:

1. LoRA-adapt the top encoder blocks. Largest untapped capacity. Rank 8-16 on the
   last 2-4 blocks adds adaptable parameters at controlled overfit risk; a full
   unfreeze would overfit on 7 slides.

2. Recall-biased loss on the touching channel alone. The wall channel is
   discriminative but under-fires (section 9). A Tversky loss weighted toward
   recall on channel 3 specifically targets that. One retrain, one variable.

3. Weight EMA / snapshot ensembling. Epoch 35 of 54 was the peak; 19 epochs were
   thrown away. An EMA of the weights is nearly free.

4. Re-measure the predicted/annotated size ratio on v4. It was measured on v3 and
   the min_area recalibration should have moved it, but "should have" is not a
   measurement.

5. Validate on an IR slide before trusting any cross-condition comparison
   (section 7c).

Deliberately NOT recommended: replacing the watershed with a query-based instance
decoder, or a learned-affinity graph cut. Both are defensible methods and both
want far more than 7 slides.
