# Part C: spatial zonation and spatial gradient methods

Scope: tissues analysed along one continuous anatomical axis (liver lobule, villus, intestinal length,
kidney cortico-medullary axis and proximal tubule, cortical depth, hair follicle, thymus), methods that
separate smooth gradients from sharp boundaries, spatially variable gene (SVG) tests, and common
coordinate frameworks. Every PMID below was checked with `lit.py summary` on 2026-10-14 (title, year
and journal match). Details were then checked against the full text of each paper on 2026-10-14. The
per-paper notes, with sources and the corrections made here, are in `full-text-notes/<PMID>.md`. The tag
after each citation says what was read. Not read in full: Harnik 2024 (abstract and public Extended Data
legends only); Moor 2018 and Ben-Moshe 2022 were read as bioRxiv v1 preprints, not the journal versions.

### D. Gradient vs boundary methods (along a coordinate)

| # | Citation | What it does | Plot types | Replicates | Software | What it adds to OUR project |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | Chitra U et al. *Nat Methods* 2025 (GASTON). PMID 39849132 [full text, supplement not read]; GASTON-Mix: Chitra U et al. *Bioinformatics* 2025, PMID 40662777 [full text, supplement not read] | Learns an **isodepth** (a 1-D "elevation" over the 2-D slice) with a neural field. **Poisson** counts with a library-size offset; expression is **piecewise linear in isodepth**: domain-specific intercept and slope, with jumps allowed at domain boundaries (segmented regression). Breakpoints are learned (Belayer's DP), and the number of domains comes from a likelihood elbow. Only the slope has a test (Poisson LLR, χ², used to zero out non-significant slopes, with no multiple-testing correction). "Discontinuity" and "intradomain variation" are called by effect-size thresholds (percentile cut-offs over all genes), not tests. ## Cross-cutting: how the field summarises and shows zonation

- **Coordinates:** discrete layers or zones are the norm: 9 layers (#1), 8 marker gates (#2), 8 reconstructed layers (#3), a percentile-binned landmark score (#5), tertile zones (#7), 30 segments (#9). Analysis then runs on bins. Continuous coordinates appear as DPT pseudo-space (#4), expression-derived pseudospace (#10, #12), normalised depth (#11), distance to a landmark (#6, #19), isodepth (#17), conformal-map relative depth (#18), and a landmark-based CCF (#15). Most continuous-axis papers still **bin for statistics** (3 DPT bins in #4, 15 bins in #10, 10 levels in #15) and use the continuous coordinate for display.
- **Per-gene summaries:** max-normalised profile; **centre of mass**; argmax layer; **end-ratio bias** (log2 PP/PC); mid-zone score (max in middle / max elsewhere); Kruskal-Wallis or ANOVA across bins, usually with cells or spots as units; per-domain slope plus boundary jump (#17, #18; called by thresholds or ranks, not tests); FSV or length scale (#22); sigmoid t0 and k (#21).
- **Pathway placement:** enrichment of zonated genes per zone (hypergeometric, #1); **gene-set mean profile ± s.e.m.** (#1, #5, #8); GSEA on a ranked zonation score (#5); over-representation per model category or pattern class (#3, #17); boundary-agnostic modules (SOM #4, NMF #9); a pairwise **enzyme-discordance score** (#1); a gene set's mean COM against random sets of the same size (#2). Nobody we found runs a *competitive* test of a pathway's **position or shape** against background genes with correlation adjustment.
- **Figures:** COM-sorted max-normalised heatmaps; line profiles ± s.e.m.; mean-vs-COM scatter; ortholog bias or COM scatter (species vs species, #5, #9); binned-axis heatmaps of cell-type abundance; label densities along the axis (#10, #11); dendrograms of ordered bins; pattern-class bar charts.
- **Group comparisons on one axis:** per-gene bias or COM ortholog scatter (#5, #9), with a rank-sum across genes (#5); cosine similarity plus bin × group ANOVA interaction, with sections as units (#15); mixed model with replicate intercept and BIC categories (#3); a "flattened gradient" judged by eye (#12); injured cells against coordinate-matched controls (#7). None uses specimens as the units of a calibrated test.

## (b) Concrete ideas to adopt

1. **Pattern classes by nested NB models plus BIC, with specimen terms** (Droin #3 × GASTON #17 × switchde #21). Per gene, fit a nested ladder on pseudospace with a per-specimen offset: flat → linear → smooth spline (graded) → **sigmoid** (switch; t0 and width = 4.39/k) → **piecewise linear with jumps at the S1/S2 and S2/S3 transitions** (boundary). Choose by BIC and keep the model weights. Droin fitted Gaussian models to 80 per-mouse layer means; BIC weights from unit-level NB fits would be near 0 or 1. So fit the ladder on per-specimen binned profiles, or calibrate the class calls by balanced specimen relabeling. GASTON has no test for a jump, so we must supply one. Ransick's sharp S2/S3 genes (#13) and the Schuh and Kaminska protein priors (#14) give positive controls. *Figure:* a bar chart of class counts, a small-multiples panel of exemplar curves per class, and a **2-D scatter of within-segment slope vs boundary jump** (our figure; GASTON reports class counts), coloured by class. This replaces the stand-alone logistic-edge call with a call that has an alternative model.
2. **Fixed-bin shape-vs-level comparison across groups** (OrganAxis #15; Yakubovsky #5; Zwick #9). Bin pseudospace with the same cut-offs for every specimen. For each gene and pathway program, report the shape similarity of per-specimen **centred** binned log profiles (their Pearson correlation; OrganAxis's uncentred cosine is not level-free) and an **NB bin × group interaction** with specimen terms (shape difference). Do this for human vs mouse and AKI vs control. Report human-vs-mouse agreement next to mouse-vs-mouse agreement computed the same way (Aizarani #4). *Figure:* a sorted bar (shape similarity) with overlaid dots (interaction −log10 q) per pathway, plus a **human-vs-mouse ortholog scatter of COM (#9) and of S3/S1 bias (#5)** (log2 late/early) with discordant genes labelled.
3. **Position-based pathway analysis** (Halpern #1; Ben-Moshe #2; Yakubovsky #5). (a) Run a competitive test on **member-gene COM** (or argmax+COM score) against background genes, with correlation adjustment. This asks whether the pathway is *located* earlier or later than expected, not just whether it is positional. Ben-Moshe's random-set COM test (#2) is the uncorrected precedent; Yakubovsky's is ranked-list GSEA on a COM-based score. (b) For ordered metabolic pathways, test **reaction order**: rank correlation of step index with peak or COM position, against permuted step labels. This test is new. Halpern (#1) and Moor (#8) show order for single pathways without a test, and Halpern's formal score is pairwise discordance (division of labour), which we can also run. *Figure:* COM vs reaction-step plot per pathway with a permutation band, and pathway member COMs as a strip plot on the pseudospace axis.
4. **Discrete-domain check on the continuous axis** (Zwick #9; Belayer #18). Cluster ordered pseudospace bins (adjacency-constrained hierarchical clustering) and run a shared-breakpoint DP. Ask whether the transcriptome prefers discrete domains, how many there are, and where they sit relative to the S1/S2/S3 label transitions. Neither paper calibrates this: the gap statistic always returns some partition, and Belayer has no P values or breakpoint uncertainty. Use a smooth-gradient null (curves simulated without steps) for the number of clusters, and per-specimen fits or a bootstrap over units for breakpoint uncertainty. *Figure:* a dendrogram of ordered bins with label-transition ticks, plus NMF/SOM module curves along pseudospace that do not depend on boundaries.
5. **Per-specimen curve features pooled by random effects** (C-SIDE #24; per-sample COM in #5). Compute COM, slope, jump and sigmoid width per specimen. Then pool with between-specimen variance, using a t-based or relabeling-calibrated interval, because C-SIDE's z-test is not shown to hold with 2 specimens. With so few specimens, show every specimen's value. *Figure:* a forest-style dot plot per gene or pathway (one dot per specimen, a pooled estimate, an interval), grouped by species or condition. Pair it with two comparators, both calibrated the same way as the continuous test (specimen relabeling), because their own tests are spot-level: a **DESpace-style NB test on S1/S2/S3 labels with specimen terms** (#23), and a **SPARK-X-style test with the labels as covariates** (#22).
6. **Label densities along the axis** (MERFISH normalised depth #11; Joost #10 on an expression-derived axis). For each specimen, plot S1/S2/S3 label kernel densities along pseudospace. This shows directly where discrete labels overlap and how wide each transition is. For AKI, also plot unit density per condition, and compare IR units with control units resampled to the same position distribution (Ben-Moshe 2022, #7). *Figure:* stacked density ridges, one row per specimen.

## (c) Gaps: what nobody seems to have done

- **Replicate-aware gradient-vs-boundary inference.** GASTON, Belayer, SPATA2 and switchde all fit one sample or one pooled trajectory. GASTON and Belayer do not test a jump even within one sample (they use thresholds or ranks). Droin has a random intercept but no shape random effects. C-SIDE pools coefficients but does not classify shapes, and its pooling is not calibrated for few samples. DESpace adds only a fixed sample intercept. SPARK-X tests, gene by gene, for spatial signal left after conditioning on discrete labels, but in one sample, with unit-level permutation and no count offset. Joost 2016 compared a binned continuous axis with cluster labels in one NB model, but dataset-wide and with cells as units (a tie). I found no method that classifies "sharp vs graded" with **specimens as replicates**, tests a step at known anatomical boundaries, or tests whether boundary *position* or *width* differs between groups.
- **Repeated physical units as replicates of one axis.** Liver and villus papers pool cells from many lobules or villi into one profile. novoSpaRc (#12) reconstructs a repeated 1-D structure from unordered units but returns one averaged, "stereotypical" profile. Imaging (#14) shows that the length of PCT over which protein uptake occurs varies between tubules of one kidney (about 600–1,100 µm) and is longer in deeper nephrons. No transcriptomic method estimates unit-level variation in where transitions occur. Rood et al. (#16) name repeated units as an open problem.
- **Coordinate uncertainty.** The methods here treat the coordinate (layers, isodepth, DPT) as fixed when testing genes; switchde's authors warn this may inflate FDR. Probabilistic embeddings (novoSpaRc, used by #12) average expression over each unit's position distribution. That smooths profiles and can blur sharp boundaries. Rood et al. (#16) call for mapping confidence. No method in this part carries coordinate uncertainty into a test or interval for slope, boundary position or width, which matters for calling "sharp" on a pseudospace.
- **Pathway position and shape tests.** Pathways are placed by enrichment within zones, GSEA on per-gene zonation scores, or mean profiles. Ben-Moshe 2019 (#2) tests a gene set's mean COM against random sets, without correlation adjustment. We found no competitive, correlation-adjusted test of a pathway's **location (COM) or sharpness** relative to background. We also found no cross-species test of pathway **shifts along the axis** with species-specific probe panels; Yakubovsky (#5) compares species on polyA Visium, so it does not face this.
- **Continuous PT zonation in situ.** Kidney work found here is discrete (S1/S2/S3 clusters, #13), cortico-medullary within the collecting duct (#12), or protein and imaging based (#14). Lake's only continuous PT analysis is an injury/repair pseudotime (#13), and dissociated atlases under-sample S3 (#13). A PubMed search for "proximal tubule zonation single cell" returned no relevant hits. A genome-wide continuous S1→S3 atlas, with human vs mouse and injury on the same axis, appears to be new.
