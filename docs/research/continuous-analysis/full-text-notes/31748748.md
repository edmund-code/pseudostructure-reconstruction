## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Assumption ("structural correspondence"): over the whole tissue, cells closer in space are on average closer in expression; checked by plotting mean expression distance against physical distance (Figs. 2b,f; 3b; ED Fig. 16b,f). The authors stress it is a tissue-wide average, not a per-gene or per-pair claim.
- Model: a probabilistic transport plan T (cells x locations) minimising (1 - alpha) x Gromov-Wasserstein discrepancy between graph-based distance matrices in expression and physical space + alpha x mismatch to a reference atlas (if any) - epsilon x entropy, with marginal constraints p (cells) and q (locations) (Methods "Mathematical formulation"). Solved by projected gradient descent through Sinkhorn-type OT sub-problems. Output: per-cell probability over positions, and a "virtual in situ" S = A T' (genes x locations) that "represents the expression patterns over an averaged, stereotypical tissue".
- 1-D repeated structures: intestinal villus (7 zones, Moor 2018 data) and liver lobule (9 layers, Halpern 2017 data) reconstructed de novo onto a line; median correlation of reconstructed vs original patterns for the top 100 variable genes 0.99 (intestine) and 0.94 (liver); fraction of cells within one layer of their original layer 0.98 and 0.73 (Fig. 2; ED Fig. 3). Grid resolution can be changed without loss (ED Fig. 5).
- Other tissues: Drosophila embryo (BDTNP in-silico dissociation; scRNA-seq stage 6), zebrafish embryo, Slide-seq cerebellum (binned), whole mouse kidney de novo onto a 2-D target for six cell types (qualitative, ED Fig. 15), and individual fly embryos.
- Identification: de novo reconstruction is defined only up to global transformations (reflection, rotation, translation) of the target shape; orientation needs a marker or prior (main text; Methods "limitations").
- Null / significance: no test for the reconstruction itself beyond comparisons with random assignments (e.g. K-S test of per-cell spatial SD vs randomised embedding, ED Fig. 10). For genes on the reconstructed 1-D axis: Kendall's tau between expression and zone order for "zonated" genes, then ranking by centre of mass (Methods "Identification of zonated genes"); Moran's I (PySAL, k = 8 neighbours) for "spatially informative" genes in 2-D. Cells/locations are the units; no replicates.
- Validation: simulations from a Gaussian-bump generative model (accuracy rises with SNR, markers, informative-gene fraction; non-monotone in alpha; ED Fig. 2); benchmarks against Seurat and DistMap (ED Fig. 18; Extended Table 1).
- Plot types: expression-distance vs physical-distance curves; probabilistic confusion heatmaps (original vs reconstructed zone; Fig. 2c,g); max-normalised zonation heatmaps; virtual in situ maps; archetype clusters; correlation-vs-number-of-markers curves.
- Software: Python package novoSpaRc, https://github.com/rajewsky-lab/novosparc (Code availability).
- Limitations (authors): embeds into a predefined shape; symmetry ambiguity; may struggle where the assumption is weak (many cell types or domains). For OUR design: the entropic, probabilistic embedding averages each cell over neighbouring positions, which smooths reconstructed profiles and will blur sharp transitions; there is no specimen layer and no test of shape.

## Review check

Nature 2019, PMID 31748748)" and "novoSpaRc (optimal transport) embeds cells on 100 one-dimensional positions" - VERDICT: correct (generalized OT: entropic Gromov-Wasserstein plus optional atlas term; Hinze chose 100 positions).
- c:32, Software "novoSpaRc (Python)" - VERDICT: correct.
- The continuous-analysis review does not otherwise discuss Nitzan. VERDICT: minor omission. novoSpaRc is a published de novo reconstruction of a repeated 1-D structure (villus, lobule) from unordered units with no tissue coordinates, with a probabilistic position per unit and a stated reflection ambiguity. The project's methods survey already lists it for the reconstruction line (docs/research/pseudostructure-methods-survey.md:21), so a cross-reference from c:32 is enough. Note for that survey: on villus and lobule data the reported accuracy is high for the intestine (0.98 of cells within one zone) but lower for the liver (0.73).
- c:83 (Gaps), "Every method treats the coordinate ... as fixed when testing genes. None propagates uncertainty in a reconstructed coordinate into boundary-width or slope estimates" - VERDICT: mostly correct but needs a nuance: novoSpaRc (and Hinze's use of it) computes expression along the axis by averaging over each cell's position distribution, so position uncertainty does enter the profiles, as smoothing; it does not enter any test or interval. That smoothing biases towards graded patterns. Proposed wording: "Probabilistic embeddings (novoSpaRc) average expression over each unit's position distribution, which smooths profiles and can blur sharp boundaries, but no method carries coordinate uncertainty into a test or interval for slope, boundary position or width."

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
High relevance to the reconstruction line (already cited in the methods survey), low to the gene-statistics line. It is the obvious published baseline for "recover an ordered axis of a repeated structure from unordered units without x/y" and shares our identification limits (orientation up to reflection; tissue-average assumption). For sharp-vs-graded calls, any profile built from its probabilistic map is smoothed by construction, so conclusions like "smooth transitions" from novoSpaRc profiles are partly built in.
