## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Two separate methodological axes (Introduction): (1) the null hypothesis: competitive, H0comp "the genes in G are at most as often differentially expressed as the genes in Gc", versus self-contained, H0self "No genes in G are differentially expressed"; (2) how the p-value is computed: gene sampling (genes are the i.i.d. units; e.g. hypergeometric test, gene permutation) versus subject sampling (subjects are the units; subject permutation or classical models). The axes are linked ("A competitive null hypothesis is natural ... in a gene sampling model, just as a self-contained null hypothesis is natural in a subject sampling model", Discussion) but are distinct issues.
- Arguments: self-contained tests are more powerful and reduce to single-gene testing for singleton sets, but can be "too powerful: in a situation in which there are many differentially expressed genes almost all gene sets may be called significant" (Section 3); the self-contained null "may not always be biologically interesting, e.g. when comparing cancer versus normal tissue". Gene-sampling p-values refer to replicating the experiment with new genes on the same subjects, not new subjects, and inflate the apparent sample size to the number of genes (Section 4.3). Subject permutation changes the tested null to the "complete null" (Section 4.2), and "permutation tests are not adequate ... in time series or when covariates are present".
- Simulation (Section 4.4, Table 3): 10,000 genes, 20 subjects (10 vs 10), 100 sets of 100 genes, within-set correlation rho 0 to 1, 5,000 data sets per rho, no true DE: the hypergeometric test becomes anti-conservative at rho 0.2-0.3, with rejection rates up to 50,000 times the nominal level in the extreme tail.
- Recommendation (Section 5, Discussion): use self-contained tests with subject-sampling p-values (global test, ANCOVA, Tomfohr's PCA test, or a subject-permutation version of Tukey's higher criticism, Table 4); "We strongly recommend against the use of gene sampling models in gene set testing."
- Replicates: subjects; the whole argument is that subjects, not genes, are the replication unit.
- Plot types: none (tables only).
- Software: none.
- Limitations for OUR design: with 2 versus 2 specimens, subject permutation has almost no permutations; the authors' preferred self-contained null is nearly always false along PT.

## Review check
- b-continuous-pathways.md:26 (row 9, point iv) - "Competitive and self-contained tests have different null hypotheses, and they differ in whether genes or subjects are resampled." - VERDICT: partly wrong: the paper treats these as two separate questions (which null; which sampling unit for the p-value), linked but not the same. A competitive statistic can be given a subject-sampling p-value (GSEA, Table 2 hybrid), and that changes the null that is actually tested. Proposed: "Competitive and self-contained tests have different null hypotheses. A separate question is whether the p-value treats genes or subjects as the independent units; gene-sampling p-values assume independent genes and say nothing about new subjects."
- b:26 - Fit (3): "Most PT genes vary with position, so self-contained tests call nearly every set. Competitive tests ask 'more positional than the typical gene', a narrower and more useful question." - VERDICT: misses an important point. The first sentence is supported (the paper warns self-contained tests may call "almost all gene sets" when many genes change). But the paper's main recommendation is the opposite of ours: self-contained nulls with subject sampling, because a competitive null is "much more difficult to test" validly and is tied to a gene-sampling model. Proposed addition: "Goeman and Bühlmann (2007) recommend self-contained tests with subject sampling and warn that competitive tests rest on a gene-sampling model. Our competitive choice is defensible only if the set statistic carries the inter-gene correlation (CAMERA-style variance inflation) and is calibrated at the specimen level (our balanced relabeling)."
- b:120 (gaps) - not specific to this paper.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
The paper frames our situation exactly: our competitive cameraPR p-values are gene-sampling p-values unless the correlation is modelled, so they speak to "new genes, same specimens", not to new specimens. Keep the competitive question (the self-contained one is trivially true along PT), but treat the specimen relabeling as the subject-sampling check that gives the result meaning for new specimens, and report gene-sampling p-values as rankings, not as evidence.
