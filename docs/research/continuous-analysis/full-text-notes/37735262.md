## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- **Model / statistic.** Non-negative factor analysis with a Poisson-type reconstruction loss.
  - E[X_ij] = (g_j + delta) * alpha_i^T theta_j, where:
    - alpha_i = cell loadings (>= 0);
    - theta_j = the gene's distribution over K factors (sums to 1);
    - g_j in [0,1] = a learned gene scale factor that absorbs high or housekeeping expression;
    - delta (default 0.001) is a floor on g_j.
  - Cell-type extension: global factors plus cell-type-specific factors, with a hard block-sparsity pattern on the loadings and a cell-type-specific gene scaling g_cj. The g_cj absorbs identity markers, so factors do not become cell-type markers.
  - Prior: each input gene set becomes a clique in a gene-gene graph A. Edge probability is P[A_ij = 1] = (1-kappa)(1-rho) theta_i^T B theta_j + kappa(1-rho), where:
    - B is a learned "factor interaction matrix", whose diagonal eta = dependence of each factor on the graph;
    - kappa and rho are background edge-addition and edge-removal rates, learned by default.
  - Edges are weighted by 1/C(|G_k|,2), so large sets do not dominate; weights add up for genes in several sets.
  - Objective: lambda * Poisson reconstruction + Bernoulli graph likelihood, called a "pseudolikelihood" because the input is normalised or log data, not counts. Recommended lambda: 0.01 (stay close to the gene sets) to 0.1 (allow deviation).
  - Optimisation: Adam in PyTorch by default, or EM. Initialisation from the gene sets.
  - Number of factors: (number of gene sets + 1) per cell type, or estimated by bulk eigenvalue matching.
  - Output:
    - cell scores q_k * alpha_ik;
    - gene scores ((g_j + delta)/(g_j + delta + offset)) * theta_jk, with offset 1 or 0;
    - eta per factor (eta < 0.25 = "new" factor, >= 0.25 = "modified");
    - a posterior graph;
    - factor importance (relative loss increase when the factor is masked) and information scores (Mimno coherence over the top 30 genes).
- **Null / calibration / significance.** The model itself gives no P value, FDR or uncertainty for factors or for gene membership.
  - Downstream, cell scores are averaged per sample (mean of positive cells, score > 0.001). Patients are then compared with Mann-Whitney U, or Wilcoxon signed-rank for paired samples, with BH correction.
  - Composition changes use Milo (NB GLM on per-sample neighbourhood counts, spatial FDR).
  - Factors are labelled by overlap coefficient of their top 50 genes with input sets (> 0.2), or by a hypergeometric test (Enrichr) with BH.
- **Replicates.** None in the model. Patient-level comparisons come from post-hoc per-sample aggregation (for example Fig. 3f, n = 40 samples per time point; Ext. Data Fig. 4b). The Salcher atlas (19 studies, 318 individuals) was fitted without batch correction, and batch-specific factors were identified by cross-study entropy (Fig. 6b).
- **Validation.**
  - PBMC perturbation ground truth (IFNg, LPS, PMA; 3 donors; Fig. 1d).
  - Held-out genes: 30-40% of gene-set members removed, then recovery measured in the top-200 gene scores (Fig. 2e; Ext. Data Fig. 5a-c).
  - Coherence on held-out cells (Fig. 2f).
  - Three simulations:
    - correlated factors (Ext. Data Fig. 5d; N = 20 cells, p = 500, K = 3, 10 trials per correlation);
    - gene-set overlap bias of score_genes (Ext. Data Fig. 5e);
    - recovery of active gene sets against Slalom (Ext. Data Fig. 5f).
  - Runtime and memory up to 200,000 cells.
  - Real data: Bassez breast cancer (97,863 cells, 42 patients), Zhang (150,985 cells), Caushi lung (MANA-specific TCRs), Salcher atlas (1.28M cells).
  - Comparators: Slalom, expiMap, scHPF, NMF, netNMF-sc, scanpy score_genes.
- **Continuous-state result relevant to the review.** In macrophages (12,132 cells), Spectra cell scores were smoothed along diffusion components DC2 and DC4. The smoothing used Palantir's GAM (y = b0 + f(DC)), and the results were shown as z-scored trend heatmaps (Fig. 5a; Methods "Macrophage analysis").
  - Along DC2 (monocyte to macrophage maturation) there are successive peaks: TNF signalling, CYP activity, glycolysis, a NEW "invasion program" (factor 182, eta = 0.24, no matching input set), then complement.
  - Milo neighbourhoods that expand under therapy only in non-responders are high in the invasion program (Fig. 5b-c). This replicated in the Zhang data, using score_genes on the program's top 50 genes.
- **Plot types.**
  - Cell scores on t-SNE or force-directed embeddings (Figs. 2b,d; 3b-c; 4d; Ext. Data Figs. 3, 4a, 7e).
  - Z-scored GAM trend heatmaps of program scores along a continuous axis (Fig. 5a).
  - Per-cell-type mean score and positive-fraction dot matrix (Figs. 4a, 6b).
  - Top-50 marker gene lists with new and held-out genes marked (Figs. 3e, 4b; Ext. Data Fig. 5a).
  - Overlap-coefficient plots (Figs. 2c, 4c, 6c).
  - Eta vs overlap scatter (Ext. Data Fig. 2).
  - Importance vs information scatter (Ext. Data Figs. 6a, 7a, 8g).
  - Per-sample boxplots (Figs. 3f, 6e-f).
- **Software.** Python (PyTorch): https://github.com/dpeerlab/spectra. The immunology knowledge base is the Cytopus package (github.com/wallet-maker/cytopus).
- **Limitations the authors state.**
  - Factors are assumed to combine linearly (Discussion).
  - Finer cell-type granularity lowers the effective sample size for cell-type-specific factors (Methods "Determining cell-type granularity").
  - Intermediate differentiation states between subtypes should generally NOT be given as separate cell types.
  - The objective is non-convex and sensitive to initialisation.
  - EM is memory-heavy.
  - Slalom and expiMap comparisons required subsetting gene sets.
- **Limitations for OUR design.**
  1. No uncertainty or test; replicates appear only after aggregation.
  2. The input is normalised (non-count) data; there is no exposure offset per unit.
  3. Cell types are assumed discrete. S1/S2/S3 sit on a continuum, so following the authors' own rule PT should enter as ONE cell type, and positional variation must be captured by factors.
  4. With lambda 0.01-0.1, most factors stay close to their input sets (median overlap 88% at 0.01, 82% at 0.1, Bassez). So membership refinement is modest unless lambda is raised.
  5. Gene-program factors along PT will partly re-learn the axis.

  - VERDICT: correct for the model. Misses that the paper aggregates cell scores per sample (mean of positive cells) and tests patients with Mann-Whitney or Wilcoxon, and uses Milo for neighbourhood abundance.
  - Optional replacement: "No replicate model; per-sample mean scores are compared afterwards."
- **b:30, software.** "Python: cnmf, Spectra."
  - VERDICT: correct for Spectra (Python/PyTorch, github.com/dpeerlab/spectra).
- **b:30, fit.** "Spectra seeded with curated pathways lets the data refine membership: only co-varying members keep weight. Program usage is then fitted along PT per specimen. Built for thousands of cells, so it needs many units per specimen."
  - VERDICT: partly unsupported, and misses important points.
    - (a) "only co-varying members keep weight" overstates the pruning. Edges are dropped through a learned background removal rate, and genes are added from the data. At the recommended lambda (0.01-0.1), the median overlap of factors with their input sets stayed at 88% or 82%. Low-expression genes are also down-weighted through g_j and the gene-score offset, not only non-co-varying genes.
    - (b) The paper gives no minimum cell number. It fitted 10^4 to 10^6 cells, but its correlated-factor simulation used N = 20 cells. The real constraint it states is effective sample size per cell type.
    - (c) Missed point: cell types are assumed discrete and known, and the authors advise against entering intermediate continuum states as cell types. PT must therefore be ONE cell type, and S1/S2/S3 labels must not be used as Spectra cell types.
    - (d) Missed point, directly relevant to our median-of-members program curve: the paper's simulation (Ext. Data Fig. 5e; Methods "Biasedness of gene set averaging for overlapping gene sets") shows that a simple gene-set average (scanpy score_genes) becomes an increasingly biased proxy for program activity as gene-set overlap rises. The same mechanism applies to our median curve where KEGG/Reactome sets share PT transporter and metabolic genes. This supports the concern raised in row 3 (FUNNEL).
    - (e) No uncertainty or test is produced, and the reconstruction loss is a Poisson pseudo-likelihood on normalised data with no exposure offset.
  - Replacement: "Spectra seeded with curated pathways lets the data add co-expressed genes and drop unsupported edges. At the recommended prior strength, most factors stay close to their input sets (median overlap 82-88%). Enter PT as a single cell type: the method assumes discrete, known cell types and advises against using states on a continuum as types. Fit program cell scores along PT per specimen afterwards. Spectra gives no uncertainty or test, so calibration stays ours. Its simulation also shows that plain set averages are biased when sets overlap, which applies to our median-of-members curve."
- **Synthesis** (continuous-analysis-literature.md): not cited. Nothing to check.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Spectra is an exploratory tool, not an inference method. It is useful for refining curated PT pathway membership against shared-gene overlap, with PT entered as a single cell type and program scores then fitted along the axis per specimen. Its strongest contribution to our review is evidence that set averages are biased under gene-set overlap (Ext. Data Fig. 5e), not the factorisation itself. It changes no synthesis recommendation, but it strengthens the "check overlap and heterogeneity before summarising a pathway" caution.
