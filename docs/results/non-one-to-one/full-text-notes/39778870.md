## What the paper does (with sections/figures)
- **How homology enters.**
  - Seven methods (Seurat v4 CCA, Harmony, fastMNN, BBKNN, scVI, scGen, Scanorama) get **OrthoFinder 1:1 orthologs
    only** (Methods, Datasets; Table S1, S4).
  - SAMap and SATURN get each species' full gene list plus protein sequences, from NCBI, Ensembl and other sources
    (Table S4). SAMap uses a BLAST-based gene graph and SATURN uses protein-language-model embeddings. Neither is
    re-implemented; both are run from their own tutorials with default settings.
- **Model and outputs evaluated.** Only cell-level integration is evaluated: 13 scIB-style metrics, 7 for batch
  removal and 6 for bio-conservation, including DPT-based trajectory conservation. The overall score is
  0.4 x batch + 0.6 x bio. **Each sub-metric is min-max scaled within each experiment**, so scores are relative to the
  panel of methods (Methods, Ranking and metric aggregation). No gene-level output is assessed: not SAMap gene pairs,
  not SATURN macrogenes, not paralogs. Paralogs appear only as a stated challenge (Introduction).
- **Null / significance / calibration.**
  - There are no significance tests of method differences.
  - ARI and ASW are repeated 20 times on 80% subsamples.
  - Robustness is shown as three runs at each subsample level (Table S8, with ± values).
  - The 1:1-vs-homolog ablation has one task per taxonomic level and no spread (Fig. S42).
  - Cell-type trees have jumble and scjackknife support values (Fig. 6).
- **Replicate handling.** Species is treated as the batch label. A "nested batch" task, task4, pairs human Microwell-seq
  from 11 adult donors with mouse; it is scored as a batch-mixing problem, not used for replication (Fig. 5B; Table S2).
  Donors are never the unit of inference.
- **Validation.** 36 tasks, 20 species, 4.7M cells (Table 1).
  - Cross-genus: cat vs tiger.
  - Cross-family: cat vs dog, and human vs macaque from the AH atlas.
  - Cross-order: human vs mouse (task4, Microwell-seq 84,638 cells; task21, AH atlas with 21,307 human vs 1,323 mouse
    cells and 11 common types), mouse/macaque/human vs pig, and sea anemone vs coral.
  - Cross-class and cross-phylum tasks extend out to Trichoplax.
- **Close-species results.**
  - SATURN ranks first at cross-genus and cross-family, with scGen, Seurat v4 CCA and scVI close behind (Fig. 2A,
    3A, 7B).
  - SAMap is weak at close distances. The authors attribute this to "the insensitivity of BLAST-derived features to
    taxonomic close species" (Discussion). SAMap also struggles with non-atlas data that have few cell types
    (Results, cross-class).
  - In the human-vs-X series (Fig. 4B-D), **the human-mouse overall scores of all nine methods are bunched together**
    (by eye, about 0.63-0.73). Methods separate mainly from zebrafish onward.
  - Fig. 7C recommends SATURN, scGen, Seurat v4 or scVI for cross-genus and cross-family integration. Three of these
    four use only 1:1 orthologs.
- **1:1 orthologs vs homologs, the direct test (Results, Influence of phylogenetic distance; Fig. S42; Tables S5,
  S10).** SATURN and SAMap were each rerun with 1:1 orthologs as input, on one task per level (score drop, SATURN /
  SAMap):

  | Level | Task | Species | SATURN | SAMap |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Cross-genus | task3 | cat vs tiger | -0.027% | -27.1% |
  | Cross-family | task18 | cat vs dog, different tissues | -14.03% | -0.71% |
  | Cross-order | task21 | **human vs mouse** | **-21.23%** | **-4.89%** |
  | Cross-class | task32 | zebrafish vs rhesus | -12.51% | -15.16% |
  | Cross-phylum | task12 | Ciona vs Nematostella | -7.42% | -12.39% |

  - The text says the average drops are 11.04% (SATURN) and 12.05% (SAMap). It also claims "For SATURN, there was a
    clear trend: the greater the phylogenetic distance between species, the more substantial the performance
    decline". **The plotted values do not show a monotonic trend.** The largest SATURN drop is at cross-order (human
    vs mouse), and the drop is about zero at cross-genus.
  - Task21 in detail (Table S10, scaled scores):

    | Method | Overall | Bio-conservation | Batch correction |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | SATURN | 0.539 | 0.774 | 0.187 |
    | SATURN(one2one) | 0.425 | 0.564 | – |
    | Seven 1:1-only methods | 0.428-0.533 | – | – |
    | scGen (best 1:1-only method) | 0.533 | – | – |

    With homologs, SATURN is essentially tied with scGen. In the main-panel scaling of Table S9, without the one2one
    variants, the gap is SATURN 0.552 vs scGen 0.502. SATURN(one2one) is below every 1:1-only method. So for human vs
    mouse, homolog input helps SATURN's own architecture but does not beat the plain 1:1 methods by a large margin.
    This rests on one task with 1,323 mouse cells and no spread.
  - The text adds that SATURN(one2one) "still ranked as one of the best performers" (Fig. S42B). For the cross-order
    level, the figure does not place it in the top 3.
- **Other relevant findings.**
  - Trajectory: on frog vs zebrafish time series, SATURN and SAMap conserve trajectories worse than scGen and Seurat
    v4 CCA (Fig. 5A, S48A).
  - In the human vs mouse nested-batch task, SATURN "failed to integrate muscle tissues" (Results; Fig. S48).
  - SATURN has the lowest batch-correction scores almost everywhere (Fig. 7A).
  - For more than two species, the 1:1 set shrinks; the authors argue that homolog-aware methods are more robust then
    (Discussion).
- **Limitations stated.**
  - The benchmark covers scRNA-seq only.
  - Current methods "may overlook valuable information in one-to-many and many-to-many homologous relationships", and
    this is listed as future work.
  - No method excels at both batch correction and bio-conservation.
  - The coarse cell-type tree is preliminary.
  - Cell types are matched across species by annotation name (Discussion; Methods).
- **Limitations for our design.**
  - No gene-level, paralog, spatial, aggregated-unit or probe-panel evaluation exists.
  - The metrics reward label clustering and species mixing, not shape along a continuous coordinate. The DPT
    trajectory metric is the only exception, and on it SATURN and SAMap underperform.
  - The relative min-max scaling makes the reported "% drops" depend on which methods are in the panel.

## Fit to our question
- The benchmark tests only cell-level integration, so it says nothing about gene-level paralog results.
- On whether non-1:1 genes help at close distance, the evidence is weak and mixed.
  - Human-mouse scores are bunched across methods.
  - At cross-genus and cross-family level the recommended set is mostly 1:1-only methods.
  - SAMap is weak at close distance.
  - The single human-mouse ablation (task21) shows homolog input helping SATURN's own model by about 21% (relative,
    n = 1). It still leaves SATURN only level with, or modestly above, the best 1:1-only method (0.539 vs 0.533 in
    Table S10; 0.552 vs 0.502 in Table S9).
- Nothing here suggests that our 1:1 shared space loses material signal for human vs mouse PT, or that SAMap or SATURN
  would add a testable non-1:1 result beyond our orthogroup and substitution analyses.
