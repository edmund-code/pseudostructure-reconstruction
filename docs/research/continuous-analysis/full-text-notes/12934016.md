## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Representation: each gene's time course is a cubic B-spline; control points of co-expressed genes are constrained to
  co-vary (borrowing strength across a gene class) to cope with noise and missing values (Methods, from their J Comput
  Biol 2003 method).
- Alignment before comparison: the reference series' time axis is warped onto the test series with a LINEAR warp
  (shift and stretch parameters) chosen to minimise a global error (Methods "Data Preprocessing"). This is one global
  linear warp per pair of experiments, not per gene and not DTW.
- Test (Methods "Hypothesis Testing"): H0 "C2 is a noisy realization of C1" vs H1 "the two curves are independent".
  The global difference D(C2, C1) is the averaged squared distance between the two curves over the overlap interval of
  the alignment. Under H0 they find the maximum-likelihood curve C at the same global distance from C1 that best fits
  the reference samples (conservative), combine it with a per-sample Gaussian noise model (sigma^2 from the few
  repeats), and compute a log-likelihood ratio referred to a chi-square with q degrees of freedom (q = number of spline
  control points). Value-dependent variance is an option (Appendix III). The test is asymmetric (reference vs test); a
  symmetric version is in Appendix II.
- Replicates: designed for the case of no or few repeated time series; only the per-point noise variance needs repeats.
- Validation: synthetic curves under several noise models (P < 0.005 separates the noisy copy from two other curves;
  details in SI); yeast cell cycle, WT alpha-factor series (Spellman) vs fkh1/fkh2 knockout (Zhu), which differ in
  sampling rate and cell-cycle length (Table 1). Of 800 cell-cycle genes, 56 differentially expressed at P < 0.005;
  37 of 56 (66%) explained by binding of six relevant factors (P = 4e-11); overlap with Zhu's clustering list 21 of 42;
  22 of ~5,000 non-cycling genes at P < 0.001.
- Plots: WT vs KO spline curves per gene (Fig. 3); bar chart of factor-binding percentages in the selected vs all genes
  (Fig. 2A); k-means clusters of the 56 genes' KO profiles (Fig. 2B).
- Software: algorithm figure (Fig. 1) and a project web page (www.psrg.lcs.mit.edu/DiffExp/DiffExp.html); no package.
- Limitations stated: none formally; the method needs a reference curve and a per-sample noise model.
- Limitations for our design: Gaussian noise on log ratios; a pointwise variance from repeats, not from biological
  replicates of whole curves; the chi-square reference ignores the alignment and spline-fitting steps (the warp is fitted
  first and treated as known).

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
A clean, early precedent for "align globally, then test each gene's curve difference" and for a level+shape global
distance between two curves. The statistics (Gaussian, chi-square, warp treated as fixed) do not transfer to NB counts
with 2 vs 2 specimens; the order of operations and the global linear warp do.
