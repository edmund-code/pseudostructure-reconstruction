## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Design: scRNA-seq (MULTI-seq barcoding) of epithelial cells from 30 equal-length segments of the whole small intestine, 2 Lgr5-GFP mice and 2 human deceased donors (human segments analysed in pairs); second mouse cohort (2 mice, 30 segments) for validation; diet experiment (chow / high-fat / high-carbohydrate, 3 mice each, 15 segments). One mouse cohort had a barcode collision for segments 9-16, resolved by a sex classifier (Methods "Initial data processing").
- Per-gene zonation: Kruskal-Wallis across positions, BH q<0.05, cells as units (only cell types with >40 cells per domain): >80% of enterocyte genes zonated in both species; 46% of crypt-cell genes in mouse (Results; Methods "Calculation of percent regionalization").
- Domains: average expression of the top 150 regionalised enterocyte genes per segment; hierarchical clustering of segments on Euclidean distance (not adjacency-constrained) nearly reproduced anatomical order; the gap statistic (clusGap) peaks at 5 clusters in both species -> domains A-E. Stable with 75-100 genes (Ext. Data Fig. 4b); Jensen-Shannon divergence on UMAP-based densities per mouse gives nearly the same tree (Ext. Data Fig. 4c). ISCs give 3 regions; the absorptive lineage goes 3 ISC -> 3 TA -> 4 progenitor -> 5 enterocyte groups (Fig. 5b).
- Continuous views: domain signature scores (mean scaled expression of top 20 domain genes, with s.e. bands) along segments (Fig. 2c,d); cNMF modules described as "agnostic to putative domain boundaries" (Results), plotted along segments (Fig. 2e,f, Fig. 4c with 95% CI); domains A and B "directly overlap" proximally, and domain C is the least zonated.
- Cross-species: per-gene centre of mass over fifths of intestinal length; mouse-vs-human ortholog scatter of COM, Spearman R = 0.29 for 6,191 enterocyte genes (Fig. 2a) and R = 0.18 for 7,668 crypt-cell genes (Fig. 5e).
- Domain prediction: cFIT transfer learning trained on mouse cohort 1 predicts domain identity per cell in cohort 2, in published Haber 2017 data, and in diet mice (prediction-proportion curves along segments, Figs 3d, 4b). The B/C boundary shifted by 2-3 segments in the test cohort. High-carbohydrate diet expands domain C. No human classifier ("high variability between human samples").
- Pathways: ReactomePA over-representation per domain (adj. P<0.02) and per NMF module.
- Mechanism: ChEA3 + SCENIC TF ranking; organoid retention of domain signatures; CRISPR Cdx1 (trend only for Fabp6) and Ppar-delta knockouts in domain-E organoids (bulk RNA-seq with edgeR; dispersion estimated without replication).
- Plot types: segment x gene heat maps with dendrograms and gap-statistic panels (Figs 1f-i, 5a,b); COM ortholog scatter (Figs 2a, 5e); signature-score and NMF-module curves along length (Fig. 2c-f); domain-prediction curves (Figs 3d, 4b); pathway dot plots (Fig. 4a); Swiss-roll smFISH.
- Software: R (Seurat 3/4, pheatmap, cluster::clusGap, cNMF, cFIT, clusterProfiler/ReactomePA, Slingshot, philentropy), MATLAB for villus zonation; custom code on Zenodo record 10223562.
- Limitations (authors): human variability; too few donors for a human classifier; domain C plasticity. Limitations for us: cells pooled per segment; Kruskal-Wallis with cells as units; the gap statistic on segment profiles always returns some partition and was not compared with a smooth-gradient null, so it does not by itself show that the axis is discrete rather than continuous.

## Fit to our design
Medium. Same design scale (2 mice, 2 human donors; species compared by per-gene centre of mass along a 1-D anatomical axis) and the same question (discrete domains vs continuum along an epithelial tube). Copy: COM ortholog scatter, signature-score curves, boundary-agnostic modules. Do not copy: cell-level Kruskal-Wallis and an uncalibrated gap statistic as evidence for discreteness.
