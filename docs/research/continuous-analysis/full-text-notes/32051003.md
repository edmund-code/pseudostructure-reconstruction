## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: a benchmark, not a new method. Tools: bulk footprint resources PROGENy (14 pathways: Androgen, Estrogen, EGFR, Hypoxia, JAK-STAT, MAPK, NFkB, PI3K, p53, TGFb, TNFa, Trail, VEGF, WNT; weighted sum of expression x footprint weights) and DoRothEA regulons with VIPER; GO terms with GSEA; single-cell tools SCENIC/AUCell, AUCell with DoRothEA or PROGENy gene sets (D-AUCell, P-AUCell; weights ignored; top 5% of the ranking), metaVIPER with 27 GTEx ARACNe networks.
- Null / calibration: performance by AUROC/AUPRC for recovering the perturbed TF/pathway (activity contrasts perturbed minus control); cluster purity by silhouette width with ANOVA/Tukey. No set-level P values.
- Replicates: not a topic; contrasts are perturbed vs control cells/samples.
- Validation: (1) bulk microarray perturbations with artificially reduced gene coverage (25 repeats): PROGENy robust down to 500 covered genes (AUROC 0.724 -> 0.636); DoRothEA dropped markedly by 2,000 genes; GO-GSEA no better than random below 2,000 genes (Fig. 1). (2) Simulated single cells sampled from bulk RNA-seq perturbations (97 TF experiments, 15 pathway experiments on 7 pathways): DoRothEA best for TFs; PROGENy best with 500 footprint genes (AUROC 0.856 bulk, 0.831 single cells); P-AUCell best at 100 genes (0.788 / 0.712) (Fig. 2). (3) Real CRISPR scRNA-seq (Perturb-seq 7 d and 13 d; CRISPRi) for TFs only: DoRothEA/D-AUCell best; SCENIC and metaVIPER near random on CRISPRi; all poor at 13 d (Fig. 3). There were no real single-cell pathway-perturbation data, so PROGENy's single-cell performance rests on simulation. (4) PBMC + HEK293T across 13 protocols: TF activities cluster cell types as well as an equal number of HVGs and better than TF expression; pathway activities carry cell-type information but are too few for clustering; SCENIC regulons overlapped little across protocols (24 TFs) and their activities were less consistent across protocols (median r 0.34 vs about 0.63 for the others), which the authors flag as possible overfitting because SCENIC regulons are built from the data they score.
- Conclusions stated: footprint gene sets work on scRNA-seq and partly beat single-cell tools; performance is "more sensitive to the gene sets than to the statistic"; weights and sign (PROGENy linear model vs AUCell) helped (HEK proliferative pathways detected only by PROGENy).
- Plots: AUROC vs gene coverage violins; ROC/PR curves; AUROC difference heatmaps over simulation parameters; UMAPs of activities; silhouette comparisons; TF/pathway activity heatmaps by cell type.
- Software: code in R at https://github.com/saezlab/FootprintMethods_on_scRNAseq; data on Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.3564179.
- Limitations (authors): simulation assumes homogeneous populations; Perturb-seq guide misassignment and compensation confound truth; the gene-set-versus-statistic claim is a hypothesis to be tested by decoupling them. For us: all human; no spatial or aggregated-unit data; the activity statistic is computed per cell without inference.

## Review check
- continuous-analysis/b-continuous-pathways.md:25 (row 8) - "single-cell benchmark (Holland 2020, PMID 32051003) ... Holland 2020 used 14 [pathways]" - VERDICT: correct.
- b:25 - "Holland: bulk footprint tools work on scRNA-seq, and performance depends more on the gene sets than on the statistic." - VERDICT: correct but needs two qualifiers. For pathways, the single-cell evidence is simulated only (no real single-cell pathway perturbations existed); the gene-set-over-statistic point is stated as a hypothesis.
  Replacement: "Holland: bulk footprint tools (DoRothEA, PROGENy) work on scRNA-seq and partly beat single-cell tools; for pathways this rests on simulated single cells. The authors suggest the gene sets matter more than the statistic, and found that keeping footprint weights and signs (PROGENy's linear model) beat an unweighted rank score (AUCell)."
- b:24 (row 7, SCENIC as "a regulatory layer") - not a citation of Holland, but Holland is direct evidence the review misses: SCENIC regulons built from the analysed data performed near random on CRISPRi, covered few TFs, and were least consistent across protocols (possible overfitting); curated regulons (DoRothEA) with VIPER did best. For a TF layer along PT, curated regulons are the better default, and regulons inferred from our own units would share the circularity problem the review raises in row 9 (vi).
  Proposed addition to row 7: "For TF activity, curated regulons (DoRothEA) scored with VIPER or a linear model outperformed SCENIC's data-derived regulons in Holland 2020; SCENIC regulons built from the same data were least reproducible."

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Supports using curated, weighted footprint resources (PROGENy, DoRothEA) per unit with a weight-aware statistic, then per-specimen curves along PT. It argues against building regulons from our own units. Gene coverage is not a problem for Visium HD aggregated units, but footprint sets must be restricted to genes on both species' panels.
