## What the paper does (with sections/figures)
- **How homology enters.** No ortholog table is used at all. Each gene's protein is embedded with ESM2. The paper says
  "the protein embeddings of all proteins available for the gene are averaged" (Methods, Generation of protein
  embeddings). The genes of all species are soft-clustered by k-means into "macrogenes". The initial gene-to-macrogene
  weight is a rank function of embedding distance (eq. 2). The weights stay strictly positive and are trained during
  ZINB-autoencoder pretraining. A loss term (eq. 12) keeps the weight geometry close to protein-embedding similarity.
  The weights are frozen during metric-learning fine-tuning (Methods, Macrogene initialization, Pretraining, Metric
  learning). Each species keeps its own gene list, so one-to-many, many-to-many and species-specific genes all enter
  the same way. BLASTP is used only to evaluate the result (Fig. 2d) and to feed SAMap. ENSEMBL 1:1 homologs are used
  only for the Harmony, scVI and Scanorama baselines (Methods, Determining gene homologs).
- **Model and outputs.**
  - Cell embedding: per cell, e_c = ReLU(LayerNorm(W_s^T log(X+1))) (eq. 3), then an encoder.
  - Fine-tuning: a triplet loss mined from within-species labels only (eq. 14-15). Its objective "forces cells to
    cluster according to their cell types" (Discussion). SATURN "requires cell clusters as an input for each dataset".
  - Gene-level output: the gene-to-macrogene weight matrix (Supp Table 1) and "differential expression on macrogenes".
    This is a Wilcoxon rank-sum test over cells, one cell type against the rest, in Scanpy (Methods, eq. 16-17;
    Fig. 2a-c).
- **Paralog-relevant examples.** All are anecdotal and none is tested.
  - Macrogenes contain frog-specific paralogs (Arhgdib, Arhgdia) and Cebpa/b/d (Fig. 2b).
  - In ionocytes, a claudin macrogene is DE although none of its genes are 1:1. The 1:1 claudins are not in the top
    200 DE genes (Results, SATURN performs DE on macrogenes).
  - Human MYOC sits in a different macrogene from the Myoc of the other four species, together with human A2M
    (Fig. 4d). This is offered as "potentially different functions"; no statistic is given.
  - 35% of the top-20 DE macrogenes have weights >0.5 only to genes of one species (Results, DE section).
- **Null / significance / calibration.**
  - Homology recapture (Fig. 2d): 56% of macrogenes contain a BLASTP homolog pair among the top-1 genes per species,
    and 91.2% among the top-10. A random gene-to-macrogene assignment gives 0.25% and 18.8%.
  - GO analysis (Supp Note 5): uses weights >0.5 and sets of at least 10 genes, giving 88 human and 79 mouse sets.
    The random-assignment null gave 0 significant sets. 14 macrogenes had gene sets in both species, and 11 of the 14
    share at least one enriched GO term by string matching.
  - There is no null for macrogene DE beyond the cell-level Wilcoxon test. The units are cells, and no specimen or
    donor term exists.
  - Benchmark spread is shown over 30 runs (random seeds).
- **Replicate handling.** None. Species is the only grouping variable. A species one-hot covariate was tried and did
  not help (Supp Fig. 5). Donors and individuals are never modelled. The mammalian atlas UMAP adds a tissue covariate
  (Supp Note 4).
- **Validation.**
  - The main quantitative benchmark is frog vs zebrafish (cross-class). Zebrafish-to-frog label-transfer accuracy is
    85.8%, against 93% achievable (Fig. 3b). SAMap is the runner-up. Harmony, scVI and Scanorama on 7,175 1:1
    homologs fail to integrate (Fig. 3d; Supp Note 2).
  - Mammalian atlas (human, mouse lemur, mouse; 335k cells; 3,000 macrogenes): there are qualitative reannotation
    examples (Extended Data Figs 1-4).
  - Quantitative mammalian comparison, against SAMap only (Supp Fig. 3, 30 runs). The main text says "SATURN also
    outperforms the best baseline approach on the mammalian cell atlas dataset (Supplementary Fig. 3)". That holds for
    mouse vs lemur and human-to-lemur. **For human vs mouse (Supp Fig. 3b) SAMap's median is at or slightly above
    SATURN's in both directions.** By eye: human-to-mouse is about 0.79 for SATURN and about 0.83 for SAMap;
    mouse-to-human is about 0.61 and about 0.63.
  - No 1:1-only baseline was run on any mammalian pair.
  - There is a five-species aqueous-humour (AH) atlas that includes human and mouse, with qualitative reannotation
    (Fig. 4a-c).
- **Close species and 1:1-only vs many-to-many.** The paper argues that subsetting to 1:1 "leads to losing a large
  portion of biologically relevant genes" (Overview). The 1:1-vs-all comparison is shown only for the distant frog vs
  zebrafish pair, and there it is confounded by a different model (Fig. 3). Nothing isolates the effect of non-1:1
  genes at mammalian distance.
- **Limitations stated (Discussion).**
  - SATURN needs a reference proteome.
  - It averages over isoforms and ignores splicing.
  - It needs per-dataset clusters, whose resolution limits label transfer.
  - Small clusters integrate poorly (germline).
  - Macrogenes cannot always be given an interpretable function.
- **Limitations for our design.**
  - The metric-learning step is built to pull cells into label clusters, which works against a continuous S1->S2->S3
    coordinate. The NAR benchmark (39778870) also finds SATURN weak on trajectory conservation.
  - Macrogene DE is cell-level Wilcoxon, which is pseudoreplication with our 2 mice and 1 donor.
  - Macrogenes are effectively soft protein-similarity families. Expression barely shapes the weights: the correlation
    between a gene's expression and its maximum weight is 0.08 and 0.05 (Results, DE section). They are therefore
    close to our HCOP family sums, with no paralog-specific test.
  - Probe-ambiguous paralogs (shared probes) would enter as separate genes with non-independent counts. SATURN has no
    mechanism for this.
  - The Discussion says SATURN "does not require subsetting them to a set of common genes" for subset-panel spatial
    data such as MERFISH. This is a claim only; no spatial or aggregated-unit data were tested.

## Fit to our question
- SATURN's gene-level object, the macrogene, is a learned, protein-embedding-weighted version of the family sums we
  already computed.
- Its only gene-level inference is a cell-level Wilcoxon rank-sum test with no replicate term and no positional model.
  It therefore offers no paralog substitution, compensation or division-of-labour test to compare with ours.
- The paper gives no evidence that non-1:1 genes help at human-mouse distance. Its own human-mouse benchmark shows
  SATURN no better than SAMap, and neither was compared with a 1:1-only method.
- The supervised clustering objective would flatten the PT continuum.
- At most, a SATURN embedding of S1/S2/S3-labelled tubule units could serve as a cell-level sanity check. It would
  not add to our non-1:1 analysis.
