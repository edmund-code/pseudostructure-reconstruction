## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: one linear mixed model per gene set (Methods, Eq 1). Expression y_gpi (gene g, patient p, time i) = set intercept + fixed gene effect beta_g + random intercept c_gp (grouped by gene within patient) + f_g(t_i) + error. Every time coefficient of f_g is split into a fixed set-average part eta and a random gene-specific part h_g ~ N(0, Sigma_h). f_g is linear, cubic polynomial or natural cubic spline. Eq 1bis: a more parsimonious version with one patient random intercept and a gene random effect. Eq 2: group-stratified trends f_{g,m} for multi-group designs (null: the same time trend in every group).
- Null / calibration: one likelihood-ratio test of the fixed AND random time effects together (Methods, step 2). Because variance components sit on the boundary, the null is a mixture of chi-squares (Self and Liang); this assumes the tested random effects are independent and was checked by simulation (S1, S2 Figs: 100,000 null simulations). ML, not REML, because the two models differ in their fixed parts. Benjamini-Yekutieli FDR across sets is recommended. The null is SELF-CONTAINED ("interest is focused on self-contained null hypotheses", Introduction). A set is significant if its average trend is non-zero OR its genes' trends are heterogeneous: random gene-trend variance alone makes a set significant (Introduction; Discussion, citing Shahbaba).
- Replicates: patients enter as random intercepts (c_gp, or c'_p in Eq 1bis). Random time trends are grouped on GENES, not patients, in the main model; a patient-grouped random trend is mentioned as an extension that is in the R package (Discussion). ML handles unbalanced, missing-at-random data.
- After the test: BLUPs give a smoothed profile per gene per patient (shrunk toward the set mean). Predicted gene profiles are clustered hierarchically and the number of trends is chosen by the gap statistic; each trend is summarized by its cluster median (Methods, "Dynamic of a significant gene set"). Sets are ranked by likelihood-ratio percentile.
- Validation: simulation of 20 patients x 8 time points, 5,000 genes in 100 non-overlapping sets of 50, with 0%, 27% or 85% of sets under H1 (75% homogeneous, 25% heterogeneous); 100 runs (Table 2). Unadjusted type I error 0.039-0.065; after BY correction < 0.001; power about 0.88 (0.81-0.85 after correction). Comparators: globalANCOVA (power about 10% lower), GSEA for time series and CAMERA (very low power, e.g. CAMERA 0.013 after correction at 85% H1). Real data: DALIA-1 HIV vaccine trial (18 patients analysed, 14 time points split into pre-ATI and post-ATI; 260 Chaussabel modules): 69/260 sets significant pre-ATI (cubic) where gene-by-gene, GSEA-time-series and CAMERA found 0; 216/260 post-ATI (Table 1). Flu/pneumococcal vaccine study (6 volunteers per arm, 9 time points, 62 modules): most modules differ from placebo. S3 Fig: post-ATI 73/75 KEGG and 101/131 GO sets significant.
- Plot types: Figs 1-2 spaghetti plots, each line = median over patients of one gene, with smoothed-median trend lines; Fig 1 homogeneous interferon module M1.2; Fig 2 heterogeneous cell-cycle module M4.16 whose "mean expression inside this gene set stays close to zero" but which shows two distinct trends. Figs 3-4: raw versus model-estimated member curves side by side; distinct trends coloured (M4.16 and M7.1 show three dynamics in Fig 4). Fig 5: heatmap, one row per trend within a significant set, columns = time points, rows standardized, either hierarchically clustered or ranked by likelihood-ratio percentile.
- Software: R package TcGSA on CRAN (Implementation section).
- Limitations stated: random-effect normality is questionable for small sets (31% of modules have < 20 genes); bigger sets are more easily significant; models are probably misspecified; try several time functions. Self-contained versus competitive choice depends on the question. Count data: suggested (not implemented in the paper) Poisson GLMM or voom weights (Discussion).
- Limitations for OUR design: (1) self-contained: in a strong-signal setting it called 216/260 modules and 73/75 KEGG sets; along PT, where most genes are positional, it would call nearly every pathway, so it does not select pathways for us. (2) Heterogeneity alone counts as signal, so a set with rising and falling members is "significant" even with a flat average; good for flagging cancellation, but not a direction-specific test. (3) The fixed time effect is tested against residual and gene-level variation; patient-level variation in the trend is not in the main model. With our hundreds of units per specimen treated as observations, the test would be pseudoreplicated unless observations are specimen-by-bin pseudobulk and calibration is external (our relabeling). (4) Gaussian model on log scale; counts need the extensions the authors only suggest.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Borrow the sub-trend step (cluster BLUP or fitted member curves, gap statistic, cluster medians, row-per-trend heatmap) to check our median program curves. Do not adopt the TcGSA test as a pathway selector: it is self-contained, treats heterogeneity as signal, and would call almost every PT pathway; its replicate model (random intercepts) does not test the positional trend against specimen-to-specimen variation, so any p-value needs our relabeling calibration.
