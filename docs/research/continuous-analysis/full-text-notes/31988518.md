## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model: per gene, a generalized linear spatial model (spatial GLMM) for counts: y_i ~ Poisson(N_i * lambda_i), log(lambda_i) = x_i' beta + b_i + e_i, with b ~ MVN(0, tau1 * K) a stationary Gaussian process and e_i ~ N(0, tau2) an over-dispersion "nugget" (Online Methods "SPARK: Model and Algorithm"). N_i = total counts of the spot (a library-size offset); x_i can include covariates such as batch. Note: over-dispersion comes from the log-normal nugget, not a negative binomial.
- Kernels: 10 by default, 5 Gaussian (squared-exponential) and 5 cosine (periodic), with length scale / period set from 5 of 10 log-spaced values between half the smallest and twice the largest pairwise distance (Supp. Notes 1.1).
- Inference / significance: null model fitted by penalized quasi-likelihood with an average-information algorithm; score test of H0: tau1 = 0 for each kernel, P from a scaled chi-square (Satterthwaite) approximation to the mixture-of-chi-square null; the 10 P values combined by the Cauchy (ACAT) rule; Benjamini-Yekutieli FDR across genes (Online Methods; Supp. Notes 1.2-1.3). A Gaussian version (SPARK-G) for normalized data is also provided (Supp. Notes 1.4).
- Calibration: null simulations (10 x 10,000 genes on olfactory bulb coordinates) and coordinate permutations of each real dataset (10 or 100 permutations) as an empirical null (Fig. 1b, 1d, 2a, 3a, 4a). SPARK is calibrated; SpatialDE is "overly conservative" (not inflated) in these tests; Moran's I (spdep) was "highly inflated" under permutation (Discussion; Supp. Fig. 10a).
- Replicates: none. One section per dataset (MOB Replicate 11, breast cancer Layer 2, one MERFISH slice from one mouse, one seqFISH field). Covariates can be added, but no multi-sample model is described.
- Validation: two simulation designs (GLSM-based on olfactory bulb coordinates; Trendsceek-style hotspot/streak/gradient on random point patterns); four real datasets: olfactory bulb (772 SE genes vs 67 for SpatialDE at FDR 5%), breast cancer (290 vs 115), MERFISH hypothalamus (power judged against 5 blank-control genes), seqFISH hippocampus (17 vs 11). Validation by marker lists, Harmonizome, CancerMine, Allen ISH.
- Plot types: QQ plots under permuted coordinates (Figs. 2a, 3a, 4a); power vs FDR curves (Figs. 1c, 1e, 2b); spatial maps of genes; hierarchical clustering of SE genes into spatial patterns (Fig. 2e; Supp. Fig. 3e-f); Venn diagrams; GO bubble plots; per-kernel QQ plots (Supp. Fig. 13).
- Software: R package SPARK with C++ via Rcpp, www.xzlab.org/software.html; data and code at https://github.com/xzhoulab/SPARK (Online Methods; Data/Code availability).
- Limitations (authors): count modelling gains little for high-count data (hence SPARK-G); equal kernel weights (1/10) may be suboptimal; tissue structure is not used in the kernels (Discussion). For OUR design: single-sample, unit-level permutation null, stationary kernels (they detect "any" pattern, they do not classify shape), PQL can be unstable on sparse counts (shown in the SPARK-X paper, Table S2 there).

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
Low for testing (single section; unit-level permutation null; it detects "a pattern", it does not classify shape). Two methodological points transfer: (1) its calibration check - QQ plots of P values after permuting coordinates - is the unit-level analogue of our specimen relabeling, and (2) the Cauchy combination is a calibrated way to combine several correlated tests (e.g. several shape alternatives per gene) into one P value without a min-P penalty, which could serve our model-ladder screen.
