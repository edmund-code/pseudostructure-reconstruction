## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model: mean mu(t) = impulse function, "the scaled product of two sigmoid functions" with initial (h0), intermediate/peak (h1) and steady-state (h2) levels, two transition times t1, t2, and ONE shared slope beta for both sigmoids "to reduce the number of parameters" (eq. 1). Counts are NB with a sample size factor (library size) and gene-specific batch factors in log space from a model matrix (eq. 2); the gene dispersion is pre-estimated with DESeq2 and held fixed (eq. 4). BFGS fitting with multiple initialisations.
- Tests: chi-square deviance (likelihood-ratio) tests. Case-only: impulse vs constant. Case-control: separate impulse fits per condition vs one combined impulse fit; batch correction is done within each condition in the full model.
- Transient vs permanent (Results, last section): a third model, a monotone sigmoid. Transient = impulse significantly better than sigmoid and the impulse fit is not monotone; permanent = not transient and sigmoid significantly better than constant; up/down from the impulse fit. This classification is applied to one condition's trajectory (shown case-only on hESC differentiation), not to a case-control difference.
- Null / calibration: BH-adjusted LRT P values; replicates are i.i.d. observations per time point with fixed batch factors; no random effects.
- Validation: simulation (case-only, 5-12 time points, 3 replicates, 1,500 DE genes from linear, sigmoid or impulse shapes + 1,000 flat): continuous models (ImpulseDE2, DESeq2 with splines, edge, limma) beat categorical DESeq2 in AUROC; ImpulseDE2 beat ImpulseDE (Fig. 2A); on random deviations from a flat trend, continuous unimodal models made fewer discoveries than categorical models (Fig. 2B, discussed in Results). Real data: seven datasets (Table 1); a "gene responsiveness" score from ImmuneSigDB as a proxy truth on LPS dendritic cells (Fig. 3); DESeq2 dispersion-outlier handling misses zero-containing genes (Fig. 4); limma/edge call many low-expression genes (Fig. 4F).
- Plots: four impulse shape classes (Fig. 1A); LRT illustration with NB densities over the fits (Fig. 1B,C); z-score heat maps of clustered DE genes (Fig. 1D-H); ROC/FDR (Fig. 2); responsiveness ECDFs (Fig. 3); P-value scatter plots (Fig. 4); class-split heat map "sorted by their peak times within each class", with GO/MSigDB enrichments beside each class (Fig. 5).
- Software: R/Bioconductor `ImpulseDE2`; GitHub YosefLab/ImpulseDE2. Instructions for DESeq2 with a spline basis in Supplementary Data S3.
- Limitations stated: non-linear fits (local optima; guarded by multiple starts); needs >= 6 time points; unimodal shapes under-fit multimodal profiles, which then look like noise; few replicates make multimodal profiles hard to distinguish from noise. Limitations for us: shared slope means equal onset and offset sharpness; fixed DESeq2 dispersion; units as replicates would over-call; transient/permanent is a per-condition shape class.

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
The closest published template for rungs 1, 3 and 4 of the proposed ladder (flat, sigmoid, bump) on NB counts with size factors, and a published precedent for the class-split, peak-sorted heat map. For PT we would add the exposure offset, specimen factors, a boundary-step model and separate slopes for the two edges, and calibrate by specimen relabeling rather than chi-square LRTs on units.
