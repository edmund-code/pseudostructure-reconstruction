## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: a simulation study, not a new method. Simulated NB counts per cell nested in individuals (individual mean drawn around a gamma-distributed grand mean; between-individual variance linear in the mean; within-individual dispersion logarithmic in the mean; dropout; cells per individual fixed, Poisson or NB) (Fig. 2, Methods "Simulation engine"). Compared 10 DE approaches within one cell type, two groups: MAST default, MAST after ComBat correction for individual, MAST with a random effect (RE) for individual, tweedie GLMM and GLM (glmmTMB), GEE1 (geepack, exchangeable), pseudobulk mean and sum (DESeq2, size factors fixed to 1), Tobit (Monocle), modified t (ROTS) (Table 1 footnotes; Methods "Evaluation of type 1 error"). All except DESeq2 and ROTS were run on log(x+1).
- Null / calibration: type 1 error from 250,000 null genes per condition; 5, 10, 20, 30 or 40 individuals per group; 50-500 cells per individual (Table 1).
- Key numbers (Table 1, alpha = 0.05): methods treating cells as independent are badly inflated (MAST default 0.56-0.89; tweedie GLM 0.34-0.71; Tobit and ROTS 0.34-0.72) and get worse with more cells. ComBat-by-individual makes it worse. MAST RE 0.041-0.069 and tweedie GLMM 0.053-0.084. Pseudobulk mean 0.021-0.026 and sum 0.008-0.035 (conservative). GEE1 inflated (0.054-0.114) until about 30 individuals per group. At the smallest design tested (5 individuals per group) the mixed models were themselves above nominal: MAST RE 0.064-0.069, tweedie GLMM 0.081-0.084 (Table 1; legend attributes this to the two-part test and few units).
- Power: increasing individuals is the best way to increase power; little gain beyond about 100 cells per individual (Fig. 3, Discussion). Pseudobulk is "only slightly underpowered" relative to mixed models when cells per individual are balanced, and loses more power when they are imbalanced (Results "Power analyses"; Supp. Figs 6-8).
- Replicates: the whole point. Individual is the experimental unit. Recommendations (Discussion): MAST with RE; alternatives are MAST with individual as a fixed effect ("particularly when the number of independent experimental units is modest"), a tweedie GLMM, or permutation testing that "must randomize at the independent experimental unit (e.g., individual)" (conditional permutation).
- Small designs (Discussion): "while mixed models will compute for only two individuals per treatment group, many genes will fail due to complete separation when the sample size is so small"; with few individuals the inter-individual variance has wide bounds; with one case and one control it is not estimable.
- Validation: simulation calibrated on pancreatic alpha cells (E-MTAB-5061); intra- vs inter-individual Spearman correlation shown on four public datasets (Fig. 1).
- Plots: boxplots of intra- vs inter-individual correlation (Fig. 1); simulation schematic (Fig. 2); type 1 error table (Table 1); power curves (Fig. 3).
- Software: simulation code at https://github.com/kdzimm/PseudoreplicationPaper (R 3.5.1). Methods used: MAST, glmmTMB, geepack, DESeq2, Monocle, ROTS.
- Limitations stated: simulation modelled on plate-based data; random-effect models are slow; with tiny samples variance estimates are poor. Limitations for OUR design: no continuous axis; nothing tested below 5 individuals per group, so the "GLMM beats pseudobulk" result does not transfer to 2 vs 2; the paper's own advice for few units points to fixed individual effects or unit-level permutation, which is what we do.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Supports our core choice (specimens are the replicate; permute whole specimens). Its headline advice (GLMM with a random effect beats pseudobulk) was shown for 5-40 individuals per group, not 2; at 5 per group the GLMMs were already inflated. For our 2 vs 2 design it argues for fixed specimen effects and specimen-level permutation, not random-effect GAMMs.
