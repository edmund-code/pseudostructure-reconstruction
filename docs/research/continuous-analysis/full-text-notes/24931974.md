## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: DyNB. Per gene, read counts Y_j(t_i) (replicate j, time t_i) are negative binomial with mean
  f(t_i) and variance sigma^2(t_i); f is a Gaussian-process realisation with a squared-exponential kernel (Methods 2.1-2.2).
  The NB variance is not fitted per gene: it is taken from a degree-2 polynomial of variance against read count fitted
  across genes and time points (DESeq-style, Methods 2.4). Library size enters as DESeq scaling factors applied to the GP
  samples, not to the counts (2.4).
- Time rescaling (Methods 2.3, Results 3.2): each replicate j gets a multiplicative factor k_j, t_j = t / k_j, with one
  reference replicate fixed at k = 1 for identifiability. The start point is fixed (scaling, not shift; they chose this
  because all cultures were activated at the same moment). The scaling is discrete, in 4 h steps at 72 h, from -32 h to
  +32 h. Results 3.3 states "DyNB allows each gene to have its own time scalings between replicates": the warp is
  estimated per gene, not shared across genes. An informative shared delay prior was tried (Supp. Fig. S3) and not
  preferred.
- Inference: Metropolis-Hastings MCMC over f, k and the kernel length-scale (Algorithm 1); priors set per gene from the
  data range. Marginal likelihood estimated by the harmonic-mean estimator (Newton & Raftery; eq. 12). Convergence by
  Gelman-Rubin PSRF.
- Null / significance: no p-values. Differential dynamics between Th0 and Th17 is a Bayes factor of M1 (one DyNB per
  condition) vs M0 (one DyNB fitted to both conditions) (eq. 13). Calls: BF > 10 and fold change > 2 at >= 1 time point
  -> 698 genes (Results 3.2). No calibration or FDR analysis.
- Replicates: 3 biological replicates (cord-blood donors/cultures) x 5 time points (0, 12, 24, 48, 72 h) x 2 conditions
  (Th0, Th17). Replicate-specific rescaling is the model's handling of replicates; there is no other between-replicate
  variance term.
- Validation: one simulation (IL17A profile as template, one replicate delayed so 72 h corresponds to 48 h; method
  recovers the delay, Fig. 2B-C); qRT-PCR on the same samples confirms delayed IL17A/IL17F in replicates 1 and 3
  (Fig. 4); comparison with time-point-wise DESeq (823 genes; 546 of DyNB's 698 overlap, Fig. 6) and next-maSigPro
  (weakest agreement, Supp. Fig. S3C).
- Plots: posterior mean curve with 95% band per condition with replicate points (Figs 1, 3, 7); arrows showing how
  measurements move under rescaling (Figs 2-3); 2D density of per-gene time differences Th0 vs Th17 per replicate
  (Fig. 5A); posterior of time-shift at 72 h against the prior (Fig. 5B-C); Venn/precision against DESeq (Fig. 6).
- Software: "will be available at http://research.ics.aalto.fi/csb/software/" (abstract). Language not stated in the text.
- Limitations stated: simple MH sampler may be inefficient if more parameters are marginalised (Discussion).
- Limitations I see for our design: per-gene warps with 2 specimens per condition cannot separate a specimen axis
  stretch from a gene effect; the harmonic-mean marginal likelihood is a notoriously unstable estimator, so the Bayes
  factors are not calibrated; the scaling-only warp is anchored at t = 0, which on our axis would pin S1 and stretch S3;
  the NB variance is a mean-variance trend, not a per-gene dispersion.

## Review check
- d-comparing-trajectories.md:30 (row 7) - "DyNB combines an NB likelihood, a GP and per-replicate time rescaling
  (differentiation-efficiency warps), with MCMC tests of differential dynamics between conditions" - VERDICT: misses an
  important point. The NB + GP + per-replicate rescaling is correct, but the "test" is a Bayes factor (BF > 10 plus a
  2-fold change), not a test with p-values, and the rescaling is a multiplicative scaling fixed at t = 0, estimated per
  gene, on a discrete 4 h grid.
  Replacement: "DyNB (Äijö 2014) fits each gene with a Gaussian process inside a negative binomial likelihood. Each
  replicate gets its own time-scaling factor (t/k, start fixed, one reference replicate), estimated separately for every
  gene. Two conditions are compared with a Bayes factor (one shared fit vs one fit per condition); genes were called at
  BF > 10 and 2-fold change. There are no p-values and no calibration."
- d-comparing-trajectories.md:30 (Replicates column) - "DEtime and DyNB: replicated time courses" - VERDICT: correct
  (3 replicates x 5 time points x 2 conditions).
- d-comparing-trajectories.md:30 (Software column) - "DyNB code from the authors" - VERDICT: correct (paper promises it
  at the Aalto CSB software page).
- d-comparing-trajectories.md:30 (For us) - "DyNB's per-replicate warp is the right idea for specimen-specific axis
  stretch" - VERDICT: misses an important point. In DyNB the warp is per replicate AND per gene, and the paper only shows
  it working with 3 replicates on 5 time points; with 2 mice per condition a per-specimen warp would be confounded with
  the condition effect unless the warp is shared across genes (one stretch per specimen).
  Replacement: "DyNB's replicate time-scaling is the idea to borrow, but estimate one stretch per specimen shared by all
  genes (DyNB fits one per gene). With two mice per condition a per-gene warp cannot be told apart from a real IR effect."

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Useful as the precedent for a replicate-specific axis rescaling inside an NB curve model. Not usable as a test: Bayes
factors from a harmonic-mean estimator, no calibration, per-gene warps. For us, a single per-specimen monotone stretch
(shared across genes) checked against the S1/S2/S3 boundaries is the transferable part.
