## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: a framework running 11 activity estimators on an input matrix (normalized counts per sample, or contrast statistics such as limma t-values / logFC) against a prior-knowledge network (sources -> targets with optional mode of regulation and weight): AUCell, UDT, MDT, (norm)fgsea, GSVA, wsum (+ norm/corr), wmean, ORA, ULM, MLM, VIPER, plus a consensus (mean of sign-preserving z-scores across methods) (Sec. 2; Supp. "Methods overview").
- ULM (Supp.): per sample and regulator, a linear model of ALL features' values on the regulator's weights (features not in the set get weight 0); the t-value of the slope is the activity. MLM: same with all regulators jointly. With 0/1 weights the ULM t is a two-group (members vs non-members) t-statistic across genes.
- Null / significance: ULM, MLM, VIPER, wsum/wmean (permutation), ORA, fgsea, consensus report p-values (Supp. Table 2). For ULM/MLM these are the regression p-values with genes as the observations, i.e. they assume independent genes; the paper does not discuss inter-gene correlation or calibration of these p-values.
- Replicates: none modelled; each sample (or contrast) is scored separately.
- Validation: recovery of perturbed regulators only, not pathways. Transcriptomics: 92 knockdown/overexpression experiments of 40 TFs (Holland 2020 collection) with DoRothEA regulons, limma t-values as input. Phospho-proteomics: 63 experiments, 14 kinases, kinase-substrate network (Hernandez-Armenta 2017). AUROC/AUPRC with negative-class downsampling; robustness to adding/deleting 25-75% of network edges (Supp. "Benchmark design").
- Results: median Spearman between methods 0.52 (RNA) and 0.65 (phospho); top-5% Jaccard 0.23 / 0.21; best: consensus, MLM, ULM (median AUC 0.67, 0.67, 0.66) vs 0.54-0.65 for the rest (Supp. Table 3); weight-aware methods do better with weights; deleting edges hurts more than adding (Sec. 4).
- Plot types: method-correlation heatmap and performance plots (Fig. 1B-C); no pseudotime or spatial plots in the paper.
- Software: R (Bioconductor decoupleR) and Python (decoupler-py, github.com/saezlab/decoupler-py); benchmark package decoupleRBench; OmniPath wrappers.
- Limitations stated: none beyond "we welcome further methods"; the benchmark is TF/kinase recovery only.
- Limitations for OUR design: PROGENy is not named or benchmarked in this paper (only "pathway footprints" via OmniPath, Sec. 2). ULM p-values treat genes as independent, the same flaw cameraPR's correlation term fixes. Our units are aggregated counts per specimen-by-bin, so scoring is feasible, but any test must be calibrated at specimen level.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Useful as a scoring library: ULM on centred specimen-by-bin profiles gives a members-vs-rest t-score per bin, and weight-aware footprints can be plugged in. The paper gives no calibration for pathway activity curves and no replicate model, and its "linear models are best" result is about TF/kinase recovery. Use it to compute scores; keep our specimen-level calibration for inference.
