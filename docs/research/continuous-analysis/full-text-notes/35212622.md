## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Question: are per-cell signature scores biased when cells differ in the number of detected genes ("gene counts", the inverse of dropout)?
- Methods compared: ssGSEA and GSVA (bulk, GSVA Bioconductor defaults), AUCell, SCSE (re-implemented in R), and a new score JASMINE (mean rank of expressed signature genes among expressed genes, averaged with an odds ratio / likelihood ratio of signature enrichment among expressed genes; both scaled 0-1) (Methods).
- Data: 10 cancer scRNA-seq datasets (5 SMART-seq, 4 10x, 1 C1); 7,503 MSigDB C2/C3/Hallmark sets with >= 20 genes; Seurat regularized-NB normalization.
- Statistic: Cohen's d between tumour and normal cells; |d| > 1 called up/down. No p-values: none of the scores yields a test statistic (author response 1b). Cells are the units.
- Findings:
  - Tumour cells detect more genes than normal cells in all 10 datasets (Fig. 1A).
  - ssGSEA/GSVA call 25%/23% of sets up and 6%/5% down vs about 9%/12% for single-cell methods (Fig. 1B); their effect sizes correlate with set size (Fig. 1C).
  - Cell-cycle example: ssGSEA scores non-cycling tumour cells higher than non-cycling normal cells (Fig. 1D).
  - Gold-standard sets built from MAST DE genes plus 0-80% noise genes: ssGSEA detects down sets poorly (about 30% at 80% noise vs 70-80%) (Fig. 2A-B).
  - Null by downsampling the same 100 tumour cells to 50% depth and renormalizing: ssGSEA calls 11% up and 46% down; AUCell 7.5%/1.2%; JASMINE 4%/1%; SCSE over-calls down in 4 datasets (25.4%) (Fig. 2C).
  - Downsampling 20-80% shifts ssGSEA calls from up to down (Fig. 3A).
  - Toy simulation: tied zero ranks in the dropout zone distort ssGSEA (Fig. 3B).
- GSVA was dropped after the first comparison (slow; highly correlated with ssGSEA; Fig. 1-fs3).
- Plot types: bar plots of genes detected; dot/heatmap plots of % sets up/down; QQ-free; recovery-rate dot plots; score-vs-gene-count panels.
- Software: JASMINE (R, github.com/NNoureen/JASMINE).
- Limitations stated / raised in review: tumour-vs-normal compares different lineages; only one bulk method fully benchmarked; JASMINE's OR needs a b=0 fix; combining the two components by an average is ad hoc (decision letter and responses).
- Limitations for OUR design: the bias mechanism is many tied zeros per profile. Our units are aggregated cross-sections, so dropout is far lower than in single cells, but small units with fewer spots will still detect fewer genes. The paper does not test count-model or control-gene (Tirosh) scores.

## Review check
- b-continuous-pathways.md:26 (row 9, item i) - "Bulk-style rank scores (ssGSEA, GSVA) are biased by how many genes each cell detects." - VERDICT: correct (Figs 1-3). Note that AUCell also showed some false positives in the downsampling null (7.5% up; Fig. 2C), and SCSE over-called down sets in 4 datasets.
- b:26 (plot types "Calibration/QQ plots") - VERDICT: not applicable to this paper (it uses Cohen's d dot plots, no QQ plots). Minor; the cell describes the group row.
- b:26 (fit column, item 2) - "If unit size or depth changes along PT, unit-level rank scores show false gradients, so use count-model curves or matched control sets." - VERDICT: correct as an inference from the paper (downsampling alone produces "differential" sets, Fig. 2C, 3A). The paper does not test count-model curves or matched control sets, so these remedies are the review's, not Noureen's.
  Replacement (optional, for precision): "Noureen et al. show that depth alone (downsampling the same cells) makes ssGSEA call 11% of sets up and 46% down, and even AUCell 7.5% up. If unit size or depth varies along PT, unit-level rank scores can show false gradients. Use count-model curves with an offset, or matched control sets (not tested by Noureen)."
- b:24 (row 7 caveat) - "rank scores per unit depend on the number of detected genes (row 9)" - VERDICT: correct; supported by Fig. 1D and 2C (AUCell less affected than ssGSEA, but not immune).

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
A cautionary benchmark, not a method we would adopt. It supports avoiding per-unit rank scores (ssGSEA/GSVA) if unit size or detected-gene count varies along PT or between species/conditions, and supports our count-model curves with exposure offsets. Its null (downsampling the same cells) is a cheap check we could run on any unit-level score we add.
