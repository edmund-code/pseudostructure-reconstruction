## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Purpose: a rebuttal of Irizarry et al. 2009 "Gene set enrichment analysis made simple" (SEA: one-sample z-score of gene t-statistics plus a chi-square statistic, both with a theoretical N(0,1) null that assumes independent genes; a gene-sampling, "Class 1" null in Barry et al.'s terms), compared with GSEA (weighted KS statistic, sample-permutation "Class 2" null).
- Statistic / null: variance inflation factor Gamma = 1 + n_k * rho (mean intra-set correlation times set size, Eq 12, after Barry et al. 2008). Benchmark of 50 data sets; for each, 1,000 random phenotype permutations (gene-gene correlation kept, no biology), and a second run also permuting gene labels (correlation destroyed).
- Results: SEA calls far more sets than GSEA (Pancreas: 570 sets at q < 0.05 versus 121 GSEA sets at q < 0.25, of 1,368). Under random phenotypes the sample-permutation variance of the SEA z-score is 2.9 (P53) and 4.12 (Pancreas) versus about 1 for gene sampling (Fig 1); intra-set correlations are mostly small and positive (0-0.3) yet inflate variance several-fold (Fig 2); SEA gives q < 0.05 for 11.5% (P53) and 20.1% (Pancreas) of sets with no signal, GSEA about 0 (Fig 3); across all 50 data sets SEA over-calls (Fig 4); when gene labels are also permuted the inflation disappears (Fig 5), so it is caused by gene-gene correlation. Cites Gatti et al. 2010 (about 200 data sets, inflation 1-6).
- Also (section "The complexity of gene score distributions"): gene sets are often multi-modal; high enrichment is frequently driven by a subset of members, sets can mix several processes (Fig 6).
- Replicates: subjects (samples); the recommended null resamples samples, not genes.
- Plot types: null z-score histograms by resampling scheme; histograms of intra-set correlation and VIF; p- and q-value histograms under randomized phenotypes; GSEA enrichment plots.
- Software: GSEA desktop application; SEA in R (their reimplementation).
- Limitations stated: no ground truth for false-positive rates in real data; recommends better (less redundant, more coherent) gene set databases and correlation-aware methods (lists ROAST, de-correlation, correlation-adjusted t-scores).
- Limitations for OUR design: phenotype permutation needs many samples; with 2 versus 2 specimens there are almost no permutations, so the correlation must be handled by an estimated VIF or by our specimen relabeling.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Supports two things we should do: estimate each set's intra-set (residual) correlation and inflate the variance of any mean-of-members statistic by 1 + n*rho, and check the calibration of every set statistic under a null that keeps gene-gene correlation (our specimen relabeling does this; gene-label permutation does not). The multi-modality finding also backs the sub-trend check before summarizing a pathway into one curve.
