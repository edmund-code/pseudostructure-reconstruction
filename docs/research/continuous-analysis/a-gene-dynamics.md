# Part A. Single-gene dynamics along a continuous axis: describing, classifying, ordering, visualising

Literature review, 2026-10-14. I checked every PMID with `lit.py summary`; title, year and journal match.
Method details were checked against the full text of each paper on 2026-10-14. Per-paper notes are in
`full-text-notes/` (one file per PMID or DOI), and each citation in the tables carries a read-status tag. Entries
without a PMID give the URL or DOI I opened.
Not read in full: Heinonen 2015 (web summary of the full text only), GeneSwitches 2020 (bioRxiv preprint v1 and README; journal version paywalled), Trapnell 2014 and Sander 2017 (methods supplements not accessible), TCseq and SiZer (package documentation only).

"Replicates?" asks whether the method models **samples/specimens** as the replicate unit. "Cells as
independent observations" counts as **no**.

## (a) Entries

### A1. Switch, onset and pattern-class models (where a gene changes and how sharply)

| # | Method / citation | What it does (model, inputs, outputs) | Plots it introduces or uses | Replicates? | Software | What it would add to OUR project |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **switchde**: Campbell & Yau 2017, *Bioinformatics*, PMID 28011787 [full text, supplement not read] | Fits a sigmoid to each gene, μ(t) = 2μ0 / (1 + exp(−k(t − t0))), on log expression (log(TPM+1)-like values, not counts), with optional zero inflation. The low state is fixed at zero, so there is no free baseline. Returns the activation position t0, the activation strength k (steepness) and a likelihood-ratio test against a constant model (χ², 2 df). The authors warn that the FDR may be inflated because pseudotime is treated as fixed. | A per-gene sigmoid over the points (Fig. 1). A t0 vs k scatter is not in the paper but follows from its outputs. | No | R / Bioconductor `switchde` | Our logistic edge fit shares switchde's position and steepness parameters (10–90% width = ln 81 / \|k\|). switchde differs in three ways: its low state is fixed at zero, it uses a Gaussian likelihood on log expression, and it treats cells as replicates. The new pieces are the LRT-vs-flat framing and a t0 × k scatter as a summary figure. It does **not** fix our gaps: no NB counts, no transient genes, no specimen-level uncertainty. |
| 2 | **GeneSwitches**: Cao et al. 2020, *Bioinformatics*, PMID 32058565 [preprint v1, not journal version] | Binarises each gene, then runs logistic regression of on/off against pseudotime. The package README (and, per a web summary, the journal version) binarises with a two-Gaussian mixture per gene; the bioRxiv preprint used one global threshold. The switch point is where P(on) = 0.5, and McFadden pseudo-R² measures fit quality. Switch points have no interval. It also orders GO terms and surface markers as "functional events" and compares orderings between two trajectories. | **Switching timeline**: genes placed at their switch point along the axis, with up- and down-switches shown differently. A density plot of switching genes per over-represented ontology. | No | R `GeneSwitches` | The timeline is the clearest figure for the S1→S3 marker order and for mouse-vs-human order. Binarising graded probe counts throws information away, so keep our NB fits and borrow only the figure. |
| 3 | **Ouija**: Campbell & Yau 2019, *Bioinformatics*, PMID 29939207 [full text, supplement not read] | A Bayesian nonlinear factor model on a small marker panel. The user decides in advance whether each gene is a sigmoid (switch time) or a Gaussian bump (peak time, i.e. transient); Ouija does not choose between shapes. The posteriors over switch and peak times give a **Bayesian test that gene A is regulated before gene B**: the 95% credible interval of the difference excludes zero. It finds "metastable" discrete states by clustering the matrix of cell-ordering probabilities. | Posterior intervals of switch and peak times per gene. Heatmap of ordering probabilities. | No | R `ouija` (Stan); Python `ouijaflow` | It has the two shape families we need (switch and transient) and posterior intervals on *position*, which our edge fits lack. Our coordinate is fixed, so we would use only the gene-model half (e.g. Stan/brms with specimen effects). Its metastable states come from uncertainty in the cell ordering. Our fixed coordinate has no such uncertainty, so using this idea to ask whether S1/S2/S3 behave as discrete states would first need the curve refitted on resamples. |
| 4 | **ImpulseDE / ImpulseDE2**: Sander et al. 2017, *Bioinformatics*, PMID 27797772 [main text only; methods supplement not accessible]; Fischer et al. 2018, *NAR*, PMID 30102402 [full text, supplement not read] | The impulse model is the product of two sigmoids, with initial, peak and steady-state levels and two transition times. ImpulseDE (2017) fits it by least squares with sampling/bootstrap P values. ImpulseDE2 (2018) puts it inside an NB model with size factors and **batch factors**. Both sigmoids share one slope, and the dispersion is pre-estimated by DESeq2 and held fixed. It runs an LRT against a constant model or case vs control. It calls genes **transiently vs permanently up/down** by also fitting a monotone sigmoid. That class describes one condition's trajectory, not a case-control difference. | Per-gene impulse fits. A heatmap split into transient/permanent up/down blocks, sorted by peak time within each block, with enrichment per block (ImpulseDE2 Fig. 5). | Yes in ImpulseDE2 (fixed batch factors in the NB model; no random effect) | R / Bioconductor `ImpulseDE2` | Its transient-vs-permanent test is the class we are missing for genes that peak mid-PT (S2). The NB model with specimen factors matches our count model. Its class-split heatmap is a published precedent for idea 6. One shared slope makes both edges equally sharp, so different S1/S2 and S2/S3 widths would need two slopes. Caveats: Spies et al. 2019 (*Brief Bioinform*, PMID 29028903) [full text, supplement not read] found that on short series (4 time points) pairwise tests were the most accurate. ImpulseDE2 was the only time-course tool that came close (F1 0.88 vs 0.89), but it was more sensitive to noise and got worse on longer series. The tradeSeq benchmark ranked it below tradeSeq. |
| 5 | **TDEseq**: Fan et al. 2024, *Genome Biol*, PMID 38622747 [full text] | A Gaussian linear additive mixed model on log-normalised expression (not counts), with shape-constrained spline bases: quadratic I-splines for growth and recession, cubic C-splines (convex/concave) for peak and trough. A random intercept per individual is the only replicate term. Each pattern gets a p-value from a mixture-of-Beta null, and the four are combined by the Cauchy rule. It was built for multi-stage designs with real time points. Mixed TDEseq lost calibration with strong batch effects and with high UMI counts. | Heatmaps per pattern class. | Yes (random intercept per individual) | R `TDEseq` | A principled monotone/peak/trough call that does not depend on where a fitted curve's maximum lands. TDEseq is Gaussian on log data and lost calibration at high counts, which aggregated tubule units have. So we would apply the same idea with shape-constrained NB smooths (e.g. `scam`) on our continuous coordinate. Its "peak" is a concave fit plus a linear term, so it also fits monotone saturating curves; a concave fit is not necessarily a mid-axis bump. On pseudotime input it did about as well as tradeSeq, not better. |
| 6 | **Trendy**: Bacher et al. 2018, *BMC Bioinformatics*, PMID 30326833 [full text, supplement not read] | Segmented regression with 0..K breakpoints per gene. Breakpoints are changes of slope in a continuous broken line, not jumps in level. The number of breakpoints is chosen by BIC, and genes are kept by an adjusted-R² cutoff; there is no gene-level P value. It reports each breakpoint's location and each segment's direction (up/down/flat). The global summary is the **distribution of breakpoints along the axis**. The authors say it applies to "time-course or spatial-course" data, and it does not require replicates. | Per-gene segmented fit; density of breakpoints across the axis; Shiny viewer. | Not modelled | R / Bioconductor `Trendy` | A breakpoint-density track shows where slopes change along PT. It is only half of a continuous-vs-discrete figure. Genes with a single gradient have no breakpoints, so they add nothing to the track and must be counted separately. A step at a boundary appears as a pair of breakpoints around a steep segment. The authors also compared Trendy peak times between mouse and human on a common scale (Barry et al. 2017), a precedent for level-free cross-species timing. |
| 7 | **scLANE**: Leary et al. 2026, *NAR*, PMID 41533563 [full text] | An NB GLM built from MARS hinge functions, with a per-cell depth offset. **Knots are chosen per gene** and read as change-point locations, each segment with its own slope. Candidate knots near the ends of the axis are excluded. Three modes: GLM, GEE, and GLMM (random intercept plus per-subject random slopes, via glmmTMB). It returns population-level and subject-specific estimates. Tests are asymptotic. | Fitted curves with 95% CI and knot lines; distribution of knot locations (supplementary Fig. S3D). | Yes | R / Bioconductor `scLANE` | The closest off-the-shelf model for "where and how fast" on NB counts with specimens. With 2 mice per group, neither the GLMM's random slopes nor the GEE's robust standard errors can be trusted (the GEE sandwich variance needs many subjects). Specimen fixed effects are not a described scLANE option and would need custom code. So run it per specimen in GLM mode and compare knot positions across specimens. Knot positions are point estimates with no interval. Knots are chosen on the same data that are tested, and the paper reports no null-calibration check, so its p-values need our specimen-level calibration. |

### A2. Trend tests and smoothing (one or more samples)

| # | Method / citation | What it does | Plots | Replicates? | Software | What it would add to OUR project |

| 8 | **tradeSeq** + **condiments**: Van den Berge et al. 2020, *Nat Commun*, PMID 32139671 [full text]; Roux de Bézieux et al. 2024, *Nat Commun*, PMID 38280860 [full text] | NB-GAM per gene and lineage. The user picks one number of knots for the data set from AIC (`evaluateK`); knots sit at pseudotime quantiles, and smoothness is set by a GCV penalty. Observation weights handle zero inflation. Wald tests: `associationTest`, `startVsEndTest` (which extends to any two regions of a lineage), `patternTest`, `earlyDETest` (between two chosen knots), `diffEndTest`. | 9 | **PseudotimeDE**: Song & Li 2021, *Genome Biol*, PMID 33926517 [full text] | NB/ZINB GAM. It builds the null by subsampling 80% of cells without replacement, re-inferring pseudotime on each subsample, then permuting pseudotime across cells. It fits a Gamma distribution (or a mixture) to the null statistics to get calibrated p-values. This carries the **uncertainty in the coordinate** into the test, but only through the null: the observed statistic uses the full-data coordinate. The authors reject the bootstrap because duplicated cells break some trajectory methods. | Calibration QQ plots; per-gene curves. | No (one sample) | R `PseudotimeDE` | Suggests refitting our elastic curve on 80% subsamples of units, drawn within each specimen, and carrying coordinate uncertainty into onset and peak intervals. That use is our own extension: PseudotimeDE uses the refits only for the null of a one-sample trend test, and its null treats cells as exchangeable. Today we treat the coordinate as fixed. |
| 10 | **Lamian**: Hou et al. 2023, *Nat Commun*, PMID 37949861 [full text] | A Gaussian functional mixed model on imputed log expression (not counts), with **sample-level random spline coefficients**. It tests whether expression along pseudotime differs with a sample covariate (XDE) and splits significant genes into **mean shift (meanSig), trend difference (trendSig) or both**. P-values come from permuting covariate labels among samples (after a cell bootstrap) or from a χ² approximation. It also tests differences in topology and cell density. A full (unconstrained) sample covariance needs S > V+K+2 samples; with fewer samples Lamian assumes Ω = ω²I and still runs. | **Six-heatmap layout**: raw per group, fitted per group, trend difference, mean shift. Clustered XDE patterns. | **Yes, its central purpose** | R `Lamian` | Its meanSig/trendSig split is the right vocabulary for AKI vs control and human vs mouse, and it matches our level-vs-positional split. With 2 vs 2 mice it would always use the constrained covariance. Its permutation over 4 samples has only 6 labelings (3 distinct splits), and the paper never evaluates that size; its smallest design is 4 vs 4. Its null experiment (8 donors split 4 vs 4) is the strongest published support for specimens as replicates: cell-level methods called 5,822–8,783 false genes, while Lamian's permutation test and limma on per-sample pseudobulk called none. |
| 11 | **Monocle (VGAM tests, BEAM, Moran's I graph test)**: Trapnell et al. 2014, *Nat Biotechnol*, PMID 24658644 [main text only; methods supplement not accessible]; Qiu et al. 2017 (Census/BEAM), *Nat Methods*, PMID 28114287 [full text, supplement not read]; Qiu et al. 2017 (DDRTree), *Nat Methods*, PMID 28825705 [full text, supplement not read]; Cao et al. 2019, *Nature*, PMID 30787437 [full text] | Monocle tests a spline in pseudotime against a reduced model. The Census paper describes these as VGAM natural-spline (df 3) likelihood-ratio tests; Trapnell 2014's own methods could not be read. BEAM is an NB GLM with a spline × branch interaction, tested by LRT. It also gives each gene a branch time point (last crossing of the two branch curves, then a divergence threshold), with no uncertainty. DDRTree (Monocle 2) learns a principal tree and adds no gene test beyond BEAM. Monocle 3 (Cao 2019) tests each gene with Moran's I on a cell kNN graph that keeps only edges between cells on adjacent principal-graph nodes (`principalGraphTest`; later `graph_test`). Cao 2019 also calls genes activated, repressed or transient from where the maximum of the scaled fitted curve lies (first 20%, last 20%, otherwise). | Pseudotime heatmap and k-means trend clusters with GO (Trapnell 2014); branched heatmap (BEAM); hierarchical-clustering gene modules and an activated/repressed/transient heatmap (Cao 2019). | No | R `monocle`, `monocle3` | Little that we lack: every test treats cells as replicates. BEAM assumes branching. Its branch-time rule is a cheap point estimate of where two curves part, but the BGP paper shows it is biased toward the global branch time and picks the wrong crossing for transient genes. Moran's I is a fast screen that says nothing about shape. Cao 2019's argmax rule is the published baseline that a model-ladder classification (idea 1) should beat. |
| 12 | **scFates**: Faure et al. 2023, *Bioinformatics*, PMID 36394263 [full text] | Principal tree or curve (including ElPiGraph). `test_association` fits a Gaussian GAM on normalised expression and tests it against a flat model (F-test); the package docs add an amplitude threshold. `fit` and `cluster` (Leiden) of the fitted features follow. A **deviation-from-linearity score** ranks transient features across a bridge: it asks whether bridge cells are a linear mix of the flanking populations. It gives a ranking, not a p-value. A between-condition test gives an "amplitude" p-value from the pseudotime × condition interaction term and a "trend" ANOVA that drops that same term. Bifurcation tools are also included. | Trend heatmaps; module plots. | No explicit sample effect | Python `scFates` | Already part of our pipeline. The linearity-deviation score is a cheap screen for transient mid-PT bumps, but it does not test, and S1/S3 reference populations must be chosen. Neither condition test checks a constant level shift, so they do not map onto Lamian's mean-shift vs trend split. |
| 13 | **Palantir / CellRank 2 gene trends**: Setty et al. 2019, *Nat Biotechnol*, PMID 30899105 [full text]; Weiler et al. 2024, *Nat Methods*, PMID 38871986 [full text] | Gene trends from GAMs weighted by fate probabilities. Palantir fits them to MAGIC-imputed log expression with a Gaussian GAM; its band is a residual SD, and there is no trend test. Palantir clusters z-scored trends and annotates the clusters with GO. CellRank 2 draws a heatmap of the top genes chosen by correlation with fate probability, **sorted by pseudotime peak**. | Peak-sorted smoothed heatmap (CellRank 2); trend-cluster panels (Palantir). | No joint model; Palantir was fitted on three donors separately and compared across them | Python `palantir`, `cellrank` | Only the figure conventions and the clustering of z-scored (level-free) trends transfer. | 14 | **maSigPro family**: Nueda et al. 2014 (Next maSigPro, NB GLM), *Bioinformatics*, PMID 24894503 [full text]; Srivastava et al. 2024 (scMaSigPro), *Bioinformatics*, PMID 38976653 [full text] | Polynomial GLM with stepwise selection of time terms and group × time terms, plus an R² filter. | # | Method / citation | What it does | Plots | Replicates? | Software | What it would add to OUR project |

| 19 | **DEtime** + **nsgp**: Yang et al. 2016, *Bioinformatics*, PMID 27288495 [full text, supplement not read]; Heinonen et al. 2015, *Bioinformatics*, PMID 25355790 [web summary only, not verbatim] | DEtime uses two GPs that share one profile before a "perturbation time" and diverge after it. | 21 | **Derivative-based change detection**: Simpson 2018, *Front Ecol Evol*, no PMID, DOI 10.3389/fevo.2018.00149 [full text]; Swain et al. 2016, *Nat Commun*, PMID 27941811 [full text]; SiZer, Chaudhuri & Marron 1999, *JASA* 94:807–823, no PMID (https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/feature/html/SiZer.html) [docs/README only] | Simpson computes the first derivative of a GAM smooth by finite differences, with a **simultaneous** interval from simulation of the coefficient covariance. ## (b) Concrete ideas we could adopt (method → the figure it produces)

1. **Shape classes with probabilities, as a model-selection problem across specimens.**
   *Sources:* Droin 2021 (classes and BIC weights), ImpulseDE2 (a flat → sigmoid → impulse ladder on NB counts),
   TDEseq (shape-constrained fits), Ouija (the bump shape only).
   - For each gene, fit a small family of NB models on the PT coordinate. Every model has specimen offsets and the same exposure offset.
   - The family: (i) flat; (ii) monotone sigmoid, switchde-style, with t0 and k; (iii) impulse or bump, ImpulseDE2's two-sigmoid (with a separate slope for each edge; ImpulseDE2 shares one) or Ouija's Gaussian; (iv) shape-constrained monotone, convex or concave (`scam`); (v) a free spline.
   - Assign classes with AIC/BIC weights, so every gene gets P(flat), P(monotone up or down), P(peak), P(trough) and P(complex).
   - Fit on per-specimen binned profiles, as Droin fitted per-mouse layer means, or calibrate the class calls by balanced specimen relabeling. BIC weights from thousands of unit-level observations would be near 0 or 1 and would treat units as replicates.
   - This extends our logistic edge fits. Transient genes become their own class instead of failing a sigmoid fit. Each call carries a probability instead of a hard threshold. The baseline to beat is Cao 2019's argmax rule (activated, repressed or transient by where the scaled curve peaks).
   - *Figure:* a "pattern atlas". One panel per class, with thin per-specimen curves and the class median. Next to it, a stacked bar of class composition per species and condition. Then an **alluvial plot** of class membership from mouse to human, or from control to AKI, for orthologous genes.

2. **A PT "switch timeline" with positional intervals.**
   *Sources:* GeneSwitches, Ouija, BGP, Swain, Simpson.
   - For monotone genes, estimate the half-maximum position t0 and the 10–90% width. For transient genes, estimate the peak position and the full width at half maximum.
   - Get intervals by computing t0, width and peak on each posterior draw of the NB-GAM coefficients (Swain's device). These draws reflect only within-fit uncertainty, so combine them with per-specimen refits. Refitting the elastic curve on subsamples (as PseudotimeDE does for its null) would also capture coordinate uncertainty.
   - Report P(A before B) for chosen marker pairs, as Ouija and BGP do. With specimens as replicates, the matching check is whether specimen-level differences in position agree in sign.
   - *Figure:* a horizontal PT axis with S1/S2/S3 drawn as background bands. Up-switching genes sit above the axis and down-switching genes below. Each point is t0, the bar is its 90% interval, and colour shows steepness. Draw mouse and human mirrored on the same axis.

3. **Derivative maps and a "change-density" track.**
   *Sources:* Simpson 2018, SiZer, Trendy, scLANE.
   - Compute d log μ / d position for each gene and program, with **simultaneous** bands from posterior simulation. Take the bands from specimen-level variation: Simpson warns that ignoring dependence makes periods of change anti-conservative.
   - Mark positions where the band excludes zero.
   - Sum across genes into a track: the number of genes significantly rising or falling at each position. Unlike Trendy's breakpoint density or scLANE's knot histogram, this counts linear gradients at every position.
   - *Figures:* (i) each gene curve stacked over its derivative panel, with significant stretches shaded; (ii) the genome-wide change-density track with the annotated S1/S2 and S2/S3 boundaries overlaid. This tests whether change is confined to the boundaries (discrete is enough) or spread along PT (continuous adds information). A penalised smooth blurs a sharp step into a ramp, which biases the track toward "spread out", so read it beside the step model of idea 1. (iii) SiZer maps for showcase genes, showing that an "edge" holds across smoothing scales.

4. **Split each contrast into mean shift vs trend difference, and locate where curves diverge.**
   *Sources:* Lamian, tradeSeq/condiments, DEtime, Heinonen nsgp, TrendCatcher. (scFates' condition tests target shape only and do not give this split.)
   - For AKI vs control and human vs mouse, split each gene's difference curve into a constant offset and a shape part, as Lamian's nested models do. Report meanSig, trendSig and bothSig.
   - With 2 vs 2 mice, use the effect sizes descriptively and keep our balanced specimen-relabeling control as the calibration. Do not quote Lamian-style permutation p-values.
   - Then find **where** the curves differ: the intervals where the simultaneous band on the difference curve excludes zero. nsgp instead thresholds a likelihood ratio with no error control, and TrendCatcher permutes group labels per interval without correction.
   - Divergence-point models (DEtime, BGP) assume identical curves before the divergence. For IR vs control, run them from each end of PT; for human vs mouse, use level-free curves first.
   - *Figures:* the Lamian six-heatmap layout (raw per group, fitted per group, trend difference, mean shift). A "divergence cascade": one horizontal segment per gene covering where AKI differs from control, sorted by start position. This shows whether the injury signature starts in S3 and spreads, or is uniform.

5. **Model pathway programs hierarchically instead of taking a median of centred curves.**
   *Sources:* Hensman 2013, DPGP, moanin, Mfuzz.
   - First check member directions. Sign-align or split members that rise and fall, because a hierarchical pathway curve is a shrunk mean of its members and still cancels them.
   - For a curated pathway, fit a pathway curve, gene deviations from it, and specimen deviations from each gene, as an NB-GAM with nested factor smooths or a hierarchical GP (Gaussian, so on log-scale data).
   - Report the program curve with a credible band, plus a coherence measure: the share of member-gene shape variance explained by the pathway curve. Hensman 2013 does not report one; it would come from the fitted variance components.
   - For data-driven programs, cluster [0,1]-rescaled fitted curves (moanin; rescale each species separately and keep only genes with a strong positional change), use soft membership (Mfuzz), or use DPGP to get a co-clustering matrix.
   - *Figures:* program ribbons with the member-gene curves faintly behind them. A DPGP co-clustering heatmap restricted to one pathway's members, which shows sub-programs.

6. **Heatmap conventions that suit continuous data.**
   *Sources:* ImpulseDE2's class-split heatmap, Ben-Moshe/Halpern COM sorting, CellRank 2 peak sorting, condiments/Lamian side-by-side images, TrendCatcher TimeHeatmap.
   - Split the gene heatmap into blocks by shape class from idea 1, as ImpulseDE2 does for transient and permanent genes. Within the blocks: decreasing genes sorted by t0, transient genes by peak position, increasing genes by t0. Use COM when a gene's class is uncertain.
   - This gives a clean cascade without the argmax artefact, where monotone genes pile up at the two ends. CellRank 2 sorts every gene by its peak, which is where that artefact comes from.
   - Add side annotations for steepness (10–90% width) and class probability.
   - Draw human and mouse heatmaps mirrored with the **same row order**, each scaled to [0,1].
   - For pathways, draw a TimeHeatmap: pathways × PT windows, coloured by the sign and size of the significant derivative. (TrendCatcher colours by the mean log2 fold change against the previous window.)
   - Add a mean-expression vs COM scatter (Ben-Moshe, who drew it for miRNAs) as an overview.

## (c) Gaps: what nobody seems to have done

- **Onset/peak positions with specimen-level uncertainty when there are very few specimens.** Two groups of methods exist, and neither fits.
  - switchde, GeneSwitches and Ouija treat cells as replicates.
  - Lamian and scLANE's GLMM give samples random effects but are not built for 2 vs 2. Lamian needs S > V+K+2 samples for a full sample covariance and otherwise falls back to a single variance parameter; its smallest published design is 4 vs 4. scLANE's GLMM fits a random intercept and random slopes on every hinge basis per subject. The paper states no minimum number of subjects, but these variances cannot be estimated from 2 subjects per group.
  - Nothing targets 2 vs 2 specimens with thousands of aggregated units each. Lamian already permutes sample labels, but only with 8 or more samples, and its null is untested at 2 vs 2. Specimen fixed effects plus balanced relabeling remain our own construction.
- **A one-dimensional *anatomical* axis built from repeated structures.** The only comparable precedents are the liver zonation papers (layers or sorted gates; Halpern pooled the mice, while Ben-Moshe and Droin used them as replicates). The pseudotime tools assume single cells, branching, or real time. condiments ran its tests on a central–portal liver axis (its TCDD case), but still on single cells.
  - Trendy and GPcounts say they handle "spatial" data. Trendy's means a spatial ordering of conditions; GPcounts' means 2-D tissue coordinates.
  - None treats the units as aggregates of segmented tubules with an exposure offset.
- **Testing continuous vs discrete gene by gene.** I found no method that asks of each gene whether a step function at the *known* S1/S2/S3 boundaries explains it as well as a smooth or free-position sigmoid curve. Close relatives:
  - Ouija's metastable states, which need uncertainty in the ordering. scFates' linearity-deviation score, which asks whether mid-axis expression is a mix of the two flanking populations; it ranks genes but does not test them.
  - Trendy's and scLANE's estimated breakpoints, which could be compared against the annotated boundaries. Trendy's breakpoints are slope changes, and scLANE excludes knots near the axis ends.
- **Comparing shape classes and onset positions across species measured on different probe panels.** Comparison methods (condiments, Lamian, maSigPro group terms) assume the same measurement in both groups. Divergence-point models (DEtime, BGP) assume identical curves before divergence, so they too need level-free curves. Level-free summaries such as onset, peak, COM, width and shape class are the natural cross-species currency. Trendy's authors compared peak times between mouse and human on a common scale (Barry et al. 2017), but nobody has formalised a test for such summaries.
- **Pathway-level dynamics with uncertainty.** TrendCatcher's TimeHeatmap rests on enrichment in time windows. Published pathway timings (GeneSwitches' median switch time, DEtime's per-term CDF of divergence times, Waterfall's averaged on/off states) summarise member positions; none gives a pathway curve with a band. Hensman 2013 proposed its hierarchical GP for pathways as future work, but as far as I found it has not been used to give a pathway curve with a credible band and a gene-coherence measure.
- **Propagating coordinate uncertainty into onset and peak intervals.** PseudotimeDE propagates it only into the null of a trend test. Outside genomics, Simpson 2018 (Sec. 4.8) refits a GAM over 100 draws of an estimated time axis (an age-depth model) and reports the timing of change as an interval. I found no genomics method that does this for switch or peak positions.
