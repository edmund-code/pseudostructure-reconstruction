## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- **Model / statistic.** WGCNA run on aggregated profiles.
  - Cells are first collapsed into "metacells". Algorithm 1 (bagging) works on a KNN graph in a reduced space, summing or averaging k neighbours, with a maximum-overlap parameter m and a target count t. It is run separately per biological replicate and per cell group (`MetacellsByGroups`).
  - For Visium spots there are "metaspots": a principal spot plus its physical neighbours, at most 7 spots, at most 2 shared between metaspots (`MetaspotsByGroups`).
  - Adjacency is the signed correlation, a = (1 + cor)/2, raised to a soft power beta chosen by a scale-free fit (`TestSoftPowers`). From it a signed TOM is built, and modules are cut from 1 - TOM with Dynamic Tree Cut (Equations 2-6).
  - Module eigengene (ME): the first right singular vector of the module's genes in the SINGLE-CELL matrix, after Seurat `ScaleData` centres and scales each gene and optionally regresses covariates (Equation 7, Algorithm 2). Harmony can then correct MEs for batch.
  - kME is the correlation of each gene with the ME, and it defines hub genes.
  - Alternative module scores (UCell, Seurat AddModuleScore) are offered and correlate with MEs (Figure S3, not seen).
- **Consensus networks.** Each dataset gets its own soft power. The TOMs are scaled to their 80th percentile, and the consensus TOM is their element-wise minimum. Demonstrated on 3 AD microglia snRNA-seq datasets.
- **Projection and preservation.**
  - `ProjectModules` computes MEs for reference modules in a query dataset. The query genes must be a subset of the reference genes. It is designed to work across species (through a gene-symbol mapping table), across modalities (ATAC gene activity) and from bulk to single cell.
  - `ModulePreservation` gives Z-summary preservation statistics with 100 permutations (Langfelder 2011 method). Thresholds: Z < 5 not preserved; 5-10 moderate; >= 10 high (Figures 1H, 6J). Microglia modules were tested in 5xFAD mouse datasets (Figure 6J).
- **Null / calibration / significance.**
  - Differential module eigengene (DME) tests are two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests on single-cell MEs, Bonferroni-adjusted. Examples: ASD vs control per inhibitory-neuron cluster (Figure 5); AD vs control in each of 10 equal pseudotime bins (Figure 6G).
  - Module overlap with disease gene lists uses Fisher's exact test (GeneOverlap). GO enrichment uses Enrichr on the top 100 kME genes.
  - The replicate unit in these tests is the cell. Samples enter only through metacell grouping and Harmony.
- **Replicates.** Metacells are built within each sample, so they do not mix samples. There is no sample-level model or test. Consensus and preservation work across datasets, not across specimens within a design.
- **Validation.** No simulation.
  - EGAD neighbour-voting AUC for GO terms (median 0.549-0.592 across six cell-type networks, Figure 2C).
  - XGBoost prediction of member genes from the top 10 hubs, 5-fold CV RMSE (Figure 2E).
  - Module preservation in external datasets.
  - Metacell algorithm compared with Metacell-2 and SEACells (Figure S2, not seen).
  - Runtime and memory on 1,000-50,000 cells (Figure 2A-B).
  - A 1M-cell PBMC run.
- **Plot types.**
  - Co-expression network UMAP of genes (Figures 1F, 3B, 4C, 5B, 6C).
  - MEs on a cell UMAP or on spatial coordinates (Figures 1E, 1G, 3C).
  - Hub-gene heatmap (Figure 1D).
  - Preservation Z vs module size (Figure 1H) and a preservation heatmap (Figure 6J).
  - **ME vs pseudotime**: average ME in 50 equal pseudotime bins with a LOESS line and 95% CI (Figure 6E). For isoforms, one LOESS line per lineage (Figure 4E).
  - **DME heatmap across 10 pseudotime bins** (Figure 6G).
  - ME versus disease-score correlation heatmap (Figure 6I).
  - Violin plots of MEs by group (Figure 5E).
  - GO dot and bar plots.
- **Software.** R package hdWGCNA, built on SeuratObject. Code is at https://swaruplab.bio.uci.edu/hdWGCNA and Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.6835227. Analysis scripts are at github.com/smorabit/hdWGCNA_paper.
- **Limitations the authors state.**
  - Single-cell data are noisy and sparse. Metacells and metaspots mitigate this.
  - The metacell result depends on the dimensionality reduction used and its technical artefacts.
  - The optimal number of cells per metacell differs by tissue.
  - Pseudobulk may be preferable when there are enough samples.
- **Limitations for OUR design.**
  1. All tests treat cells (here, units) as replicates.
  2. Along the PT almost every gene co-varies with position, so modules will largely re-learn the axis. This becomes circular if the axis was built from the same genes.
  3. MEs are computed on scaled, normalised expression, with no count model or exposure offset.
  4. Consensus across species needs ortholog mapping, and the query genes must be a subset of the reference genes. With species-specific panels, the shared-gene set limits this.

- **b:28, fit.** "Consensus modules across mouse and human specimens give data-driven programs with eigengene curves along PT, to set beside the curated pathways. Eigengene versus median: the eigengene weights genes by variance and has an arbitrary sign; the median weights members equally and is robust, but it cancels opposing sub-trends."
  - VERDICT: partly wrong.
    - In hdWGCNA (and WGCNA) the ME is computed AFTER each gene is centred and scaled (ScaleData, Algorithm 2). Genes therefore do not enter weighted by their raw variance. Each gene's weight is its loading on the first PC of the standardised module matrix, which follows its correlation with the module's common pattern (kME). The eigengene is dominated by genes that share the main pattern, not by high-variance genes.
    - The sign point is not addressed in the paper.
    - The review also misses that hdWGCNA's `ProjectModules` plus `ModulePreservation` is a ready-made cross-species route: learn modules in mouse, project them into human through an ortholog table, and get a permutation-based preservation Z. That is the same use as the CoGAPS/projectR row 16.
  - Replacement: "Consensus modules across specimens, or mouse modules projected into human with ProjectModules and tested with ModulePreservation (permutation Z-summary), give data-driven programs with eigengene curves along PT. The eigengene is the first PC of the centred and scaled member genes. It weights members by how closely they follow the module's main pattern (kME), not by raw variance, and its sign is arbitrary. The median weights members equally and is robust, but it cancels opposing sub-trends. Caveats: hdWGCNA tests cells (units) as replicates, and on the PT most genes co-vary with position, so modules partly re-learn the axis."

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Useful mainly for two things:
- the figure idiom: binned module score along the axis with a LOESS band, and a per-bin condition-difference heatmap (Fig. 6G), which is a template for "where along PT does AKI act";
- mouse-to-human module projection with permutation preservation Z, if restricted to shared orthologs.
Its inference is cell-level (Wilcoxon on single-cell MEs) and needs replacing with specimen-level calibration. Modules learned from position-dependent genes partly re-learn the PT axis, so use held-out genes. It adds no set-level test we lack.
