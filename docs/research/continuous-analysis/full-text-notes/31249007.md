## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- A narrative review; no new method, data or statistics.
- Key concepts: pseudotime is a sequence of cell states, not real time or rate ("does not convey any information about
  real elapsed time nor the progression rate of cells"); direction needs prior knowledge; ordering assumes similarity is
  dominated by the process of interest and that a continuum of co-existing states was sampled (Concepts; Limitations).
- Limitations listed: sampling density; feature selection biases the ordering ("cells will be ordered based only on the
  selected factors"); prior clustering assumed correct; highly expressed genes dominate similarity; confounding
  continuous processes (cell cycle, "spatial patterning, cellular stress or cell size"); oscillations; branch points as
  instantaneous events; fixed topologies; cells from different times or locations may not co-exist; transcript state
  alone may miss fate (Limitations section). The same section notes pseudotime ideas transfer to non-temporal continua
  ("an axis in pseudospace").
- Gene-level fitting: piecewise-constant GLMs over intervals or GLMs on a spline basis of pseudotime; "one can achieve
  a higher resolution of sequential gene activation based on continuous cell states compared with discretised
  approximations of trajectories (Fischer et al., 2019)" (section on genes and pathways). Gene expression along
  trajectories is descriptive, not causal.
- Comparing conditions (disease section): "approaches for comparing continuous structures such as pseudotime or lineage
  trees to unravel altered lineage allocation are still largely unexplored". Options discussed: compositional
  over/under-representation of a mutant projected onto a wild-type map (Wagner 2018); DTW alignment (cellAlign;
  Cacchiarelli 2018), with caveats that branch-point calls depend on the input gene-set size, DTW robustness is untested,
  and DTW "may remove too much of the biologically relevant information, similar to batch effect correction". They warn
  that projecting a new condition onto a control embedding can hide a new state that is orthogonal to the control data,
  and recommend a joint embedding.
- Plots: schematic only (Figs 1-4: Waddington landscape, embeddings, expression vs pseudotime, compositional
  differences, aligned global pseudotime, divergence, arrest).
- Software: none; points to dynverse (Saelens 2019) and states its best performers (PAGA, Slingshot, SCORPIUS) and its
  advice to confirm results with several methods.
- Limitations for our design: written for single-cell developmental data, not tissue sections; no statistics for
  replicates.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Background citation for limitations, plus two concrete cautions that apply to us: the axis can be shaped by which genes
built it (supports our held-out-gene/fold protection), and projecting IR units onto a control-built curve can hide
injury states. It also supplies a citable sentence that condition comparison along continuous trajectories was largely
unexplored in 2019.
