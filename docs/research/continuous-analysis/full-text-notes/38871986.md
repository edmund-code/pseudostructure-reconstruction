## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic
  - Cells are states of a Markov chain. "Kernels" build the cell-cell transition matrix from one view: VelocityKernel, PseudotimeKernel (kNN edges down-weighted when they point to lower pseudotime; soft or Palantir-like hard thresholding), CytoTRACEKernel (gene-count-based potential turned into a pseudotime), RealTimeKernel (WOT optimal-transport maps between time points plus within-time-point similarity), and a metabolic-labelling kernel (cell- and gene-specific transcription and degradation rates). Kernels can be combined with weights (Methods, "Kernel combination").
  - "Estimators" (GPCCA) find macrostates and label initial/terminal states; fate probabilities are absorption probabilities, computed 30x faster by merging representative cells into one pseudo-state (Methods).
  - Lineage-correlated ("driver") genes: correlation of gene expression with fate probability, usually restricted to progenitor and target clusters (compute_lineage_drivers; Methods "Datasets"; Discussion says ranking is "solely based on correlation").
  - Gene trends: GAMs of expression over pseudotime with each cell weighted by its fate probability for that lineage (Fig. 2d legend; cellrank.models.GAM in Methods). The model family and smoothing are not specified here; the paper refers to the original CellRank (ref. 14).
  - Cascade heatmap: "smoothed gene expression along the CytoTRACE pseudotime for the automatically identified top 50 lineage-correlated genes, sorted according to their pseudotime peak" (Fig. 3e legend; cellrank.pl.heatmap in Methods). Used to say which regulators peak before FOXA2/SOX17.
- Null / calibration / significance: no calibrated test of gene trends in the paper. Kernel comparisons use the CBC score with one-sided Welch t-tests across cells and the terminal-state identification (TSI) score; driver rankings are scored against curated marker lists (relative AUC).
- Replicates: none modelled. Each dataset is analysed as one pool of cells.
- Validation: human hematopoiesis (24,440 cells; PseudotimeKernel vs VelocityKernel), embryoid bodies (31,029 cells; CytoTRACEKernel vs Palantir and DPT), MEF reprogramming (165,892 cells) and pharyngeal endoderm (55,044 cells; RealTimeKernel vs WOT), intestinal organoids with scEU-seq labelling (vs dynamo and CellRank 1). Benchmarks are against known terminal states and literature drivers; no simulation.
- Plots: UMAP coloured by fate probabilities; random-walk and projected-stream visualisations; per-lineage gene-trend lines (Fig. 2d); peak-sorted cascade heatmap (Fig. 3e); ranked driver lists (Fig. 4e,f); kinetic-rate trends (Fig. 5e,f); circular fate embeddings mentioned in Methods.
- Software: Python `cellrank` (github.com/theislab/cellrank, BSD-3), reproducibility repo theislab/cellrank2_reproducibility; labelling inference in scVelo.
- Limitations (authors): kernels give different results when their assumptions fail (e.g. RNA velocity in hematopoiesis, Supp. Note 1); PseudotimeKernel needs a unique initial state and unidirectional change; driver ranking is correlational, not causal. Limitations for us: no replicate or calibration layer; the peak-sorted heatmap is exactly the argmax ordering the synthesis wants to avoid for monotone genes.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Fate probabilities, kernels and absorption have no role on a single non-branching PT axis with no RNA velocity or time points. Only the cascade-heatmap convention and the fate-weighted GAM idea transfer, and both are weaker than our per-specimen NB curves with explicit shape classes. Nothing here addresses replicates or calibration.
