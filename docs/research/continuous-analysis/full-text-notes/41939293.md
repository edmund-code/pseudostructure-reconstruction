## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model (Methods, "Fitting temporal gene expression patterns"): per gene, a VGAM Tobit GAM of normalized expression on pseudotime, y = f(pt) + e with left-censoring at 0.1, spline sm(pt, df = 2) fixed (not data-tuned). BH-adjusted P < 0.05 keeps a gene. Fitted values are scaled to mean 0, SD 1. Not a count model.
- Switch points (Methods): default = cells where the scaled fitted curve crosses 0; options: threshold crossing (user gamma) or maximum absolute first derivative. Results robust across the three definitions (Fig. S1) and to fitting parameters (Fig. S2A-B) and to pseudotime noise sd 0.5 (Fig. S3).
- Patterns: direction at each switch point gives I/D codes; genes grouped into monotone increasing, monotone decreasing, then nonmonotone (ID, DI). Within each pattern, genes are ordered by their FIRST switch point. Top 1,000 genes by adjusted P shown in a heatmap.
- Enrichment: (1) GO ORA per temporal pattern; (2) "temporal GO": within each pattern, the ordered gene list is cut into overlapping windows of 20% of genes, shifted by 10% (about 10 bins), each tested by clusterProfiler GO enrichment; terms shown if q < 0.05 (Fig. 2C, 4D). Window ranking robust to bin/stride (Fig. S2C). GO only (org.Hs.eg.db / org.Mm.eg.db).
- Multiple samples (Methods, "Identifying switch points"): integration/batch correction BEFORE pseudotime so pseudotime is shared; per-sample fits and switch points; the overall pattern is the majority pattern among samples where the gene is significant; genes kept if >= 75% of samples agree ("confidence score"); a mean fitted trajectory across supporting samples gives the reported switch point; CI = mean +/- 2 SE of per-sample switch points. Genes are compared by non-overlapping CIs (GATA1 earlier than HBB/HBA1/HBA2; Fig. 4B, 5B). No formal test or multiplicity control for these comparisons.
- Validation: qualitative; HCA bone marrow HSC -> erythroid (single sample, then multiple samples; number of samples not stated in the text) and GSE253355 bone marrow (12 donors). Comparison against hierarchical clustering, k-means (k = 4), tradeSeq clusterExpressionPatterns, ImpulseDE2 (25 quantile bins x 3 random pseudobulk replicates), NMF, WGCNA (140 modules) - judged visually by whether known erythroid genes appear in order (Fig. 3).
- Pathway curves: can take ssGSEA pathway scores instead of genes as input (Methods "Input"; Fig. S4).
- Plot types: cascade heatmap of scaled fitted curves ordered by pattern then switch point, with marker labels and optional switch-point markers (Figs 2A, 4A, 5A); GO dot/bar plot per pattern (Fig. 2B, 4C); temporal-GO plot of GO terms x successive switch-point bins (Fig. 2C, 4D); switch-point CI plot for selected genes (Fig. 4B, 5B).
- Software: R, github.com/changxinw/Pseudotimecascade (v1.0; VGAM, ComplexHeatmap, clusterProfiler).
- Limitations stated: none explicit beyond the design choices (fixed df = 2 to keep "broad" trends).
- Limitations for OUR design: Gaussian Tobit model on normalized values (no exposure offset); cells as units within a sample; CIs as mean +/- 2 SE need many samples - with 2 specimens per group they are unstable, and the 75% agreement rule reduces to "both specimens agree"; df = 2 cannot express sharp edges; zero-crossing of a z-scored curve is the midpoint of its range, close to our logistic edge position only for monotone genes.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Useful as a figure and workflow template: direction-specific cascade heatmaps, temporal ORA windows, and per-gene switch-point intervals. Its statistics are light (Gaussian Tobit on normalized values, mean +/- 2 SE across samples, CI overlap as the comparison), so with 2 specimens per group we should keep our NB edge fits and specimen relabeling for inference, and borrow only the display and the within-direction windowing.
