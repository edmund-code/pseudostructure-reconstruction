## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic
  - Four modules (Fig. 1). Module 1: TSCAN cluster-MST trajectory on harmonized PCs; branch "detection rate" = share of 1,000 cell bootstraps in which the branch reappears (Jaccard overlap above the 0.99 quantile of a random-set null) (Methods "Constructing pseudotemporal trajectory"). Module 2: branch cell proportion per sample, binomial (default) or multinomial logistic regression on sample covariates, Wald test, BH FDR. Module 3: gene expression (TDE, XDE). Module 4: cell density (TCD, XCD).
  - Gene model (Methods "Modeling gene expression along pseudotime"): a Gaussian functional mixed model. Each sample's curve is Ys(t) = sum_k phi_k(t) b_sk on K+1 B-spline bases with K equidistant knots; K chosen per gene by BIC over 0..20. Cell-level noise eps_sc ~ N(0, sigma_s^2) (sample-specific variance, inverse-Gamma prior). b_s = B x_s + u_s, with fixed covariate effects B ((K+1) x (V+1)) and sample random effects u_s ~ N(0, sigma_s^2 Omega). Fitted by EM (Supplementary Note 1.1). It is not a count model: inputs are log2 library-size-normalized, SAVER-imputed expression (Methods "Data harmonization and preprocessing": "All DE methods used imputed values except tradeSeq and condiments").
  - Covariance rule, quoted from Methods: the degrees of freedom for Omega after V+1 covariates "are S − (V + 1) and one needs at least K + 1 degrees of freedom"; "if the sample size S does not exceed V + K + 2, we do not have enough information to estimate an unconstrained Ω. In that scenario, we add a constraint by assuming Ω = ω2I". So an unconstrained Omega is used only when S > V+K+2; otherwise Lamian still runs, with an isotropic (one-parameter) Omega. (The df sentence, S − (V+1) ≥ K+1, and the stated cut-off, S > V+K+2, differ by one inside the paper itself.)
  - XDE (Methods "Detecting differential expression associated with sample covariate"): nested models M0 (beta_.v = 0), M1 (all beta_kv equal = constant shift, because the B-spline bases sum to 1), M2 (beta_.v free). Overall XDE test M0 vs M2; mean test M0 vs M1; trend test M1 vs M2. Classes meanSig (mean only), trendSig (trend only), bothSig; "otherSig" = XDE overall but neither mean nor trend FDR < 0.05. Three FDRs per gene (FDRoverall, FDRtrend, FDRmean).
  - TDE: H0 all beta_k0 equal (flat) vs free; generalizes with covariates.
  - Density (Methods "Analysis of cell density changes"): pseudotime cut into 100 equal bins; r_st / L_s (cell share per bin per sample) treated like expression in the same Gaussian functional model (they say a Poisson/NB version would need MCMC, so they use this simpler approach).
- Null / calibration / significance
  - Default Lamian.pm: likelihood-ratio statistic; null from 100 permutations by default. XDE permutation: "we first bootstrap the cells ... and then we permute the values of the covariate Xv among the samples"; the null LLRs are smoothed with a kernel density estimate and the p-value is its upper tail; BH across genes. TDE permutation instead permutes pseudotime of cells within each sample (after a cell bootstrap).
  - Lamian.chisq: chi-squared asymptotic null; faster, "slightly underestimated the real FDR" (Results; Fig. 4e).
  - Replicate unit = sample (donor/patient). No minimum sample number is stated for the permutation; the paper does not discuss how few distinct relabelings small designs allow. Smallest design shown: 8 donors, 4 vs 4 (HCA-BM, sex comparison and null).
- Null experiment (Results "Lamian is more powerful..."; Methods "Simulations - XDE detection"): erythroid branch of HCA-BM, 13,269 cells, 8 donors randomly split 4 vs 4; group differences removed by shifting the lower-median group's expression to the other group's median within each of 100 pseudotime intervals. At 5% FDR: Lamian.pm 0 XDE genes; Lamian.chisq 62; limma on per-sample pseudobulk 0; monocle2TrajTestCorr 7,846; tradeSeq patternTest 8,783; tradeSeq earlyDETest 7,259; tradeSeq diffEndTest 5,822; PhenoPath 7,400; condiments 8,753 - "which are all false positives". For tradeSeq and condiments "cells from replicate samples were pooled and treated as if they came from a single sample"; tradeSeq was run treating the two groups as two lineages with cell weights 0.99/0.01 (Methods "Comparisons with existing methods"). PhenoPath was given sample dummy columns but does not separate sample- from cell-level variance. A TDE null (pseudotime permuted within samples) likewise gave many false positives for Monocle2/3, tradeSeq and TSCAN and none for Lamian.pm (Supplementary Note 3.1, Fig. S14a-b; counts are only in the figure).
- Replicates / multiple samples: central purpose. Batch and other confounders enter the sample-level design matrix (Methods "Adjusting for confounding variables"; Supplementary Note 4). Pseudotime uncertainty is handled only by the cell bootstrap inside each permutation; trajectory uncertainty is reported separately (detection rate), not propagated into DE (Discussion).
- Validation
  - Spike-in simulation on the null: 20% of genes (1,814) made XDE (trend only, mean only, both) at four signal strengths; Lamian.pm's real FDR stayed below reported FDR for overall, trend and mean tests; all other methods underestimated FDR; limma pseudobulk matched Lamian's power for mean shifts but had zero power for trend-only genes (Fig. 4e-f).
  - Real data: HCA-BM (8 donors) sex-chromosome enrichment of sex XDE genes (Fig. 4i, S12, S13); COVID-19 PBMC CD8 T cells, 66 mild vs 48 moderate (split-half reproducibility, Fig. 5g); TB memory T cells, 184 donors in 38 batches (Fig. 6). Topology simulations with 10,000 runs (Fig. 2h); TCD/XCD simulations 1,000 runs each (Fig. S15).
- Plot types: tree/PC plots with detection rates (Fig. 2); branch-proportion heatmap plus bar (Fig. 2d); TDE heatmaps raw vs fitted with k-means row clusters (Fig. 3a-c, S4); the XDE layout - six heatmaps in Fig. 3d (raw per group x2, fitted per group x2, trend difference, mean shift), described in Methods "Visualization" as four white-bar-separated panels (original, fitted, standardized fitted group difference = trend difference, mean shift, white = not significant); per-sample fitted curves coloured by group (Fig. 3e, 4j, 5c); per-sample cell-density curves (Fig. 3f-g, 5d, 6c); cluster-averaged group difference (Fig. 5f); split-half overlap curves (Fig. 5g, 6e).
- Software: R package Lamian v0.99.1, https://github.com/Winnie09/Lamian; analysis code https://github.com/Winnie09/trajectory_variability (Code availability).
- Limitations stated: Lamian.pm is slow (4.2 h, 25 CPUs for 8 samples / 33k cells; 114 h for TB); trajectory uncertainty is not propagated into DE tests (Discussion); modules 1-2 support only TSCAN; density model uses a Gaussian approximation; model formulated for scRNA-seq.
- Limitations for OUR design: Gaussian model on imputed log expression, not NB counts with an exposure offset; with S = 4 (2 vs 2), V = 1, an unconstrained Omega needs K+3 < 4, so Omega is always the isotropic constraint; the covariate permutation among 4 samples has 6 assignments and, for a symmetric two-group LLR, only 3 distinct partitions - the cell bootstrap plus KDE smoothing would produce a continuous-looking null from these, which the paper never evaluates at this size; the human donor (1 sample, 2 sections) cannot enter as a group with sample variance.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
The meanSig/trendSig nested-test split (M0 vs M1 vs M2 on spline coefficients) maps directly onto our level-vs-shape split and can be re-implemented in our NB per-specimen framework. Lamian itself does not fit: it is Gaussian on imputed log expression, has no exposure offset, would always use the constrained Omega at S = 4, and its permutation null at 2 vs 2 rests on 3 distinct splits. Its null experiment is the strongest published support for specimens-as-replicates, and it also shows a pseudobulk-per-sample test stays clean.
