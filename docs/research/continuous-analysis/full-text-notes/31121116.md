## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: scCoGAPS is Bayesian NMF (D ~ A x P) on log-transformed CPMs of high-variance genes, with an "atomic" prior (each element zero or gamma-distributed, Poisson prior on the shape: adaptive sparsity) sampled by Gibbs/MCMC (STAR Methods). Cells are split into 100 sets of about 1,200; patterns are learned per set and merged into consensus patterns (hierarchical clustering of gene weights; R >= 0.7 to the cluster mean). projectR projects new data onto the learned gene weights A by least squares (limma lmFit with A as the design matrix); each target sample gets a projected pattern weight with a Wald test per sample-by-pattern. Cross-species projection maps genes by homology.
- Null / calibration: per-sample Wald test of projected weights (BH-corrected); AUCs of pattern weights against annotations; gene-set Wilcoxon test (limma) on pattern gene-weight z-scores for GO/KEGG sets with 5-100 genes. No model of biological replicates for inference; when replicates exist, the CoGAPS noise SD can be taken across replicates, otherwise 10% of the signal (min 0.1).
- Replicates: not modelled beyond the noise SD option.
- Validation: 120,804 developing mouse retina cells (E11-P14, 10x) gave 80 patterns covering cell types, cell cycle, sex (Xist), doublets, and technical features (genes detected, batch). Projections: P14 mouse Drop-seq retina (mature-type patterns AUC > 0.7; progenitor pattern absent); human bulk retina time course, gestational days 52-136 (AUCs per time point recover birth order and the earlier human onset of photoreceptor programs); human macula vs periphery (differential pattern use at days 73 and 132); mouse RPC ATAC-seq without batch correction; developing human cortex (2 data sets), mouse midbrain, and Tabula Muris (67/80 patterns significant somewhere). Compared with SVD, PCA, gradient NMF and DCA.
- Plots: UMAPs coloured by pattern weights; pattern x annotation correlation heatmap (Fig. 3B); alluvial plots of patterns to cell types; ROC curves and density plots of projected weights; heatmap of AUCs of projected patterns across human time points (Fig. 5A); ATAC TSS profiles per pattern.
- Software: CoGAPS (Bioconductor 3.8) and projectR (Bioconductor 1.0; v0.99.2 at https://github.com/genesofeve/projectR); analysis code "available upon request".
- Limitations (authors): choice of dimension k is open; least-squares projection not compared with alternatives; nonlinear methods without gene weights cannot be projected. For us: projected weights are regression coefficients on the target's expression levels, so gene-specific level offsets in the target (for example a different probe panel) feed directly into the projected values. The paper's cross-species and cross-assay comparisons are made within the target data (ranking or AUC across target samples), not by comparing absolute weights between source and target.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
A reasonable exploratory way to measure mouse-learned PT programs in human and IR units, and its human retina time-course example is a close analogue of projecting along an axis. It gives no replicate-level inference and its projected weights inherit gene-level offsets, so for our panels use gene-centred inputs and compare positional shapes within each target.
