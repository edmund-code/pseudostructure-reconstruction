# Part D: comparing continuous trajectories between conditions or species, plus reviews and benchmarks

Literature review, 2026-10-14. Every PMID below was checked with `lit.py summary` (title, year and journal match).
Details were checked against the full text of each paper on 2026-10-14. The per-paper notes, with what each paper
does and a check of every claim made here, are in `full-text-notes/` (one file per PMID). Each citation carries a
read-status tag; several papers were read without their supplements, and the tags say so.
Not read in full: cellAlign (Alpert 2018; abstract, README and code only), Heitz 2025 (abstract only), Rayon 2020 (methods supplement not accessible), and Kanton 2019, Ben-Moshe 2022 and Saelens 2019 (preprints, not the journal versions).
"For us" means the project as described in RULES.md: units are tubule cross-sections, there is one continuous PT
axis (S1 -> S3), replicates are specimens (2 control mice, 2 IR mice, 1 human donor), and the probe panel differs by
species.

Types of difference these methods detect. I use the same names throughout:
- **level**: a constant offset (Lamian calls it a "mean shift"; Droin calls it "additive", meaning additive on the log
  scale);
- **shape**: the curve itself changes (Lamian "trend difference"; Droin "interaction");
- **timing or warp**: the same programme runs earlier, later, faster or slower along the axis (cellAlign, G2G
  warps, Rayon's "allochrony");
- **divergence or mismatch**: the curves agree up to a point and then separate, or one condition has a state the
  other lacks (DEtime/BGP "perturbation or branching time"; G2G insertions and deletions, which a plain level
  difference also triggers);
- **density**: units are distributed differently along the axis (condiments progressionTest, Lamian XCD, Milo).

## (b) Concrete ideas to adopt (method, then the figure it produces)

1. **Five-class gene and programme classification for condition x position, after Droin 2021 and Lamian.**
   - Model: per gene, nested NB GAMMs with a specimen intercept: flat; position only; condition only (level);
     position + condition (same shape, shifted level); position x condition (shape change). This is our adaptation:
     Droin fitted Gaussian models to log2 per-mouse layer means, with quadratic shapes only. Lamian's nested
     mean-shift and trend tests give the same level-versus-shape split.
   - Classes are assigned by BIC weights. Droin computed them on 80 aggregated points per gene; from thousands of
     unit-level fits they would be near 0 or 1 and would treat units as replicates. Fit per-specimen binned profiles,
     or read the weights only after calibration.
   - Calibration: use the balanced specimen relabeling already in use. Droin had 10 mice and only a random
     intercept, so its interaction class was judged against within-mouse noise, not mouse-to-mouse shape variation.
   - Human versus mouse: report only the interaction class, because the level term is confounded with the probe panel.
   - Figures:
     - Lamian-style heatmap row, sorted by peak position (Lamian itself orders rows by k-means clusters): control
       fitted | IR fitted | mean shift | trend difference.
     - A stacked bar per pathway showing class probabilities.

2. **"Where along PT does IR act": a difference curve with a specimen-based band, plus a divergence point.**
   - Model: an NB GAMM in mgcv gam() with one smooth per condition plus specimen factor-smooths (bs = "fs", m = 1).
     Δ(x) = f_IR(x) - f_ctrl(x) is the difference of the two condition smooths. A GS model alone (global smooth plus
     specimen smooths) has no condition term and cannot give Δ(x).
   - The band is model-based and rests on a specimen variance estimated from 4 mice. Pedersen et al. give no
     calibration for so few groups, and Zimmerman found mixed models already inflated at 5 individuals per group.
     Calibrate the band with balanced specimen relabeling before reading it as specimen-level uncertainty. Do not use
     likelihood-ratio p-values between HGAM structures (Pedersen et al. advise against them).
   - Add a DEtime-style posterior for x*, the first position where the curves diverge. Run it from each end of PT,
     because the model assumes identical curves before x*.
   - Figures:
     - An **effect-location map**: a heatmap of Δ(x) for responsive genes, ordered by where |Δ| peaks, with the
       S1/S2/S3 boundaries ticked.
     - A density of divergence points along the axis, and per pathway the CDF of member divergence points (as DEtime
       ranks GO terms). If IR acts at S3 this shows directly. The positive controls are injured-S3 and failed-repair
       genes that are on our panel (see #20).

3. **A level-free gradient coefficient per gene, after Weiss 2026 and Yakubovsky 2026.**
   - Statistic: a per-specimen slope, S3/S1 log bias or centre of mass, on the log scale with the exposure offset.
     A constant probe-panel offset cancels.
   - Summarise across specimens and test with balanced relabeling. A specimen random intercept alone (Weiss's
     model) would still test the slope at the unit level; Yakubovsky's bias is computed from sample-averaged profiles
     with no specimen model.
   - Do not rescale genes by their noise, and use centre of mass for mid-PT genes, whose linear slope is near 0.
   - Figures:
     - Human-versus-mouse slope scatter, with correlation and quadrant counts (conserved S1, conserved S3, reversed).
     - Control-versus-IR slope scatter.
     - "Gradient lost / kept" bars split by S1-high versus S3-high genes.
     - NES-versus-NES scatter of pathway rankings in each species or condition.
   - It is cheap, and each point can be replicated per specimen.

4. **Global warp first, then gene-specific residual shifts, after Bar-Joseph 2003 and Kanton 2019, with
   cellAlign/G2G.** Rayon 2020 supplies only the first step: one global factor that fits genes and clusters alike.
   - Step 1: fit one monotone human-to-mouse axis warp from many genes (DTW on centred or scaled smoothed curves, or
     a monotone spline). Check it against the S1/S2/S3 boundaries. On our normalised axis, expect a non-uniform warp
     (different S1/S2/S3 proportions) or a partial one (different covered extent), not a uniform stretch. If the
     extents differ, trim to the shared extent first (Kanton; TrAGEDy).
   - Step 2: for each gene, compute the residual centre-of-mass shift after the warp and the G2G alignment
     similarity, on curves standardised within species (G2G scores level differences as mismatches).
   - Stability: small gene groups give unstable warps (Aach & Church 2001). Check residual shifts by gene
     half-splits and specimen agreement, with a mouse 1 versus mouse 2 warp as the baseline.
   - Figures:
     - A cellAlign pseudotime-mapping plot of the warp, or Rayon's correlation heat map with the fitted warp line.
     - A scatter of residual Δ centre of mass against alignment similarity.
     - G2G aggregate-alignment paths for each pathway programme on the time-point matrix, coloured by how many
       members match at each position pair.
   - This prevents a global (possibly non-uniform) warp from being reported as many shifted genes.

5. **Joint clustering with conservation tiers, after Cardoso-Moreira 2020 and Sepp 2024.**
   - Method: cluster human and mouse curves (or control and IR curves) together, after centring curves within
     species. For each orthologue pair, compute the probability that both fall in the same cluster from the
     membership probabilities.
   - Calls: "different" at BH FDR 5% (Cardoso-Moreira 2020), or three tiers (preserved, intermediate, diverged) from
     a similarity P computed from the two membership vectors (Sepp 2024). Neither uses replicate variation, so
     calibrate the diverged call with balanced specimen relabeling and check whether the two control mice agree.
     Report how the calls change with the number of clusters. With one human donor, "diverged" genes are candidates
     only.
   - Figures:
     - An alluvial (cluster-switch) diagram, as in Sepp.
     - Clusters ordered by centre of mass.
     - Fraction diverged per pathway.

6. **Unit density along the axis before any expression comparison, after condiments, Lamian XCD and Milo.**
   - Plot per-specimen ridge plots of unit position along PT, control versus IR. Ben-Moshe 2022 plotted the
     coordinate distribution per time point, and condiments' TCDD liver case found treatment shifted hepatocytes
     along the central-portal axis.
   - Test with Milo's design moved onto the axis: count units per specimen in overlapping windows along PT, fit an
     NB GLM per window (condition contrast; specimens as replicates; normalised for total units per specimen), and
     use a weighted BH across overlapping windows. With 2 vs 2 mice, share dispersion across windows and check
     calibration with balanced specimen relabeling. Unit counts per window also depend on how the section cuts the
     tubules.
   - Show an imbalance-score strip along the axis (our 1-D translation; condiments computes its score on the
     embedding).
   - Compare IR units with control units matched on position distribution, as Ben-Moshe did for regenerating
     hepatocytes.
   - Check IR unit positions first: injury can change the genes that build the coordinate (Ben-Moshe), and units
     projected onto a control-built axis can hide injury states off that axis (Tritschler). Report distance to the
     curve for IR units.
   - This separates "IR removes S3 units" from "IR changes S3 expression".

## (c) Gaps: what nobody seems to have done

- **Alignment that accounts for replicates.** cellAlign, G2G, TrAGEDy and CAPITAL each align one pooled reference to
  one pooled query. None tests whether a mismatch or warp is larger than replicate-to-replicate variation; TrAGEDy
  only shows a replicate-versus-replicate alignment as a visual check. Aach & Church 2001 found that a
  shuffled-time-point null gives no usable significance for alignment scores. A per-specimen alignment with a
  between-specimen null is missing.
- **Calibrated tests for very small designs.** Lamian, TDEseq and HGAM/GAMM handle replicates in principle, but none
  is calibrated for 2 versus 2 animals plus 1 donor:
  - Lamian needs S > V+K+2 for a full sample covariance and otherwise falls back to one variance parameter; its
    smallest published design is 4 vs 4, and permuting sample labels over 4 mice gives only 3 distinct splits, a
    size never evaluated;
  - TDEseq's calibration was shown with 3 samples per stage and failed under strong sample-level effects;
  - Pedersen et al. give no calibration for so few groups and advise against likelihood-ratio p-values between HGAM
    structures;
  - Zimmerman tested nothing below 5 individuals per group and warns that mixed models fail for many genes at 2.

  Nobody provides exact or small-sample guidance for between-condition curve tests. Lamian permutes sample labels,
  but only with 8 or more samples; balanced relabeling at 2 vs 2 is our own.
- **Separating platform level from biological level.** Alignment methods scale the data, which removes level;
  regression methods keep level, which carries the panel offset. Divergence-point models (DEtime, BGP) and G2G read
  a level offset as divergence or mismatch. The working fix in the literature is to use within-species shape or
  slope only (Weiss β1, Yakubovsky bias, Droin interaction class), but none of these faced a species-specific probe
  panel: Weiss compared slopes across two MS platforms without modelling the platform, and Yakubovsky used polyA
  Visium in every species. No method models a species-specific probe or platform offset explicitly.
- **AKI along the continuous anatomical PT axis.** Kidney AKI studies are regional or cluster-based (Dixon, one
  Visium section per time point; Melo Ferreira, one mouse per model at 6 h), or they build an injury-state
  pseudotime (Kirita) whose healthy end points are segment groups, so it mixes anatomical position with injury
  state. Liver has injury-along-the-axis studies: Weiss 2026 (a continuous lobule coordinate; desmoplasia in 4
  patients; slopes tested at the cell level) and Ben-Moshe 2022 (cells binned into three zones per time point;
  cell-level tests). A two-axis kidney model (anatomical position x injury) appears not to exist.
- **Pathway-level differential trajectories with uncertainty.** G2G aggregate alignment (a majority vote over genes,
  with no uncertainty), NES-on-slope correlation and DEtime's per-term divergence timing exist, but there is no
  competitive set test on **difference curves** Δ(x) with specimen replication. No method compares programme curves
  (median of member genes) between conditions with a calibrated null.
- **No independent benchmark of between-condition trajectory methods.** We found none: Saelens 2019 benchmarks
  inference only, and Tritschler 2019 called condition comparisons "still largely unexplored". The comparisons were
  made by each method's own authors, and they disagree: G2G's paper shows TrAGEDy calling false matches between
  unrelated processes, while TrAGEDy's paper shows G2G missing a truncation and a mid-trajectory divergence. The
  time-course benchmark (Spies 2019) found false positives were the main failure of continuous tools: pairwise
  tests won on 4-point series, spline tools on 8 or more.
- **Slicing.** Heitz 2025 (abstract only) lists a bias from slicing among the challenges for spatial trajectory
  inference. We found no between-condition comparison method that models it; we could not check the review's full
  text for one.
