## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: positional information = mutual information I({g_i}; x) (Eq 7), equivalently total entropy minus noise entropy (Eq 8) or prior position entropy minus posterior entropy (Eq 10, Bayes' rule Eq 11). Positional error sigma_x(x) = width of the MAP/ML position estimate; bounded below by the Cramér-Rao bound 1/Fisher information (Eqs 13-14). Gaussian small-noise approximation: sigma_x^2 ~ (g'(x))^-2 sigma_g^2 for one gene (Eq 17); multi-gene with covariance C(x) (Eq 21). Link: I >= <log2(L / sqrt(2 pi e sigma_x^2))> (Eqs 19, 22). Toy cases (Fig 1): a sharp step carries at most 1 bit; a sigmoid with a reproducible transition carries more; a linear gradient is optimal for constant noise. Two genes can be redundant, complementary or synergistic (Fig 2).
- Estimation (the bulk of the paper): direct (histogram) estimator with extrapolation to infinite sample size (subsets of embryos, regress on 1/m) and to zero bin size (Fig 6); first and second Gaussian approximations (FGA, SGA) for more genes; adaptive Monte Carlo integration of the Gaussian-mixture total entropy (Figs 10-11); maximum-likelihood merging of pairwise-stained covariances with a regularizer chosen by cross-validation (Fig 9); profile alignment options Y (per-embryo offset and scale), X (rigid shift per embryo), T (age detrending). Data matrix: 4 genes x N embryos x 1000 position bins, middle 80% of the axis.
- Null / calibration / significance: no hypothesis tests; error bars from subsampling, bootstrap and extrapolation. Validity of the small-noise approximation checked by scaling the noise (Fig 16; < 3% difference).
- Replicates: 4 data sets of N = 24, 32, 31 and 102 embryos. The noise is embryo-to-embryo variability at the same, independently measured position; estimates agree across data sets within about 5%.
- Results: single genes 1.83-2.24 bits (Table 1); pairs about 20% redundant (Fig 12; hb/Kr pair 3.44 bits); quadruplet I = 4.3 +/- 0.07 bits, redundancy R = 0.84 (Fig 13); removing correlations changes 4.2 to 4.4 bits; X alignment adds about half a bit (4.8 bits with XYT), which the authors read as rigid embryo-to-embryo pattern shifts; quadruplet sigma_x about 1% on average, never more than a few percent (Fig 15); 1% error still leaves about 16% chance of swapping neighbouring nuclei, about 1.5 bits short of unique identity.
- Plot types: mean profile with variability envelope (Fig 5); entropy decomposition schematic (Fig 3); information versus positional error and misassignment probability (Fig 4); estimator extrapolation plots (Figs 6, 10, 11, 13); method-agreement scatter (Fig 7); per-gene information across data sets and alignments (Fig 8); pair redundancy bars (Fig 12); sigma_x(x) curves for single genes, a pair and the quadruplet (Figs 14-15); expression trajectory in gene space (Fig 14E).
- Software: none released; profile extraction with custom MATLAB routines.
- Limitations stated: assumes steady-state snapshot (gap genes are dynamic); dorsal-edge profiles mix nuclear and cytoplasmic signal; large data sets needed for covariances; information is a lower bound because measurement error removes information.
- Limitations for OUR design: (1) x is measured independently of expression; ours is inferred from expression. (2) The noise at fixed x is across 24-102 replicate embryos; we have 2 specimens per group. (3) The paper stops at 4 genes; the joint estimate already needs Gaussian approximations, sample-size extrapolation and regularized covariances. A pathway of 20-200 members means a 20-200 dimensional covariance per position, where plug-in information is strongly biased upward. (4) Alignment choice (X shift) moved the answer by half a bit; our per-specimen axis registration will similarly change the bits.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
The single-gene and few-gene machinery (sigma_x = noise/slope, Cramér-Rao link, sample-size extrapolation, small-noise check) transfers directly to genes along PT. Pathway-level bits are the risky part: joint information for tens of genes is not what this paper validates, so a per-pathway number should come from cross-validated decoding across specimens (held-out genes or count splitting), with the plug-in Gaussian estimate only as a secondary, bias-corrected figure.
