## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Data: single-cell Deep Visual Proteomics (laser-microdissected single hepatocyte shapes, Orbitrap Astral DIA). Healthy N = 14 individuals (11 female, 3 male), desmoplasia (tissue next to liver metastases) N = 4; ONE central-to-portal trajectory per individual, 44 cells per trajectory; 792 shapes, 649 after QC; median 2,539 proteins per shape. Mouse comparison: re-analysis of a previously published male mouse scDVP dataset, N = 3, different MS platform (timsTOF SCP) and labelling (Results "Cross-species comparison"; Methods).
- Coordinate: spatial ratio S = d_portal / (d_portal + d_central), 0 at the central vein, 1 at the portal vein (Methods "Identification and strategic selection").
- Model / statistic (Methods "Continuous analysis approach"): per protein, outlier cells removed (Tukey fences), intensities robust-scaled (median removed, divided by IQR), then an LMM y_ij = beta0 + beta1*S_ij + u_i + e_ij with a random INTERCEPT per patient only (no random slope). beta1 = "zonation coefficient"; Wald test of beta1 = 0; BH Q values; "strongly zonated" = |beta1| > 1 and Q < 0.05. Proteins kept if detected in >= 70% of samples.
- Between conditions: separate LMMs per cohort; Delta beta1 tested with a Wald test (difference over its standard error), restricted to proteins strongly zonated in healthy; hits = |Delta| > 1 and Q < 0.05 (Methods; Fig. 5b). Zone comparison of |Delta| by unpaired rank-sum test (Fig. 6a); pathway coefficients by paired Wilcoxon over proteins (Fig. 6d).
- Key results: 47% of proteins zonated (Q < 0.05); 171 strongly zonated (69 central, 102 portal) (Fig. 3b). Continuous LMM vs 20-bin one-way ANOVA (OLS on cells, no patient term): 429 proteins significant in both, 397 more only by the LMM, Spearman 0.72 (Extended Data Fig. 5a); 3-bin GSEA lost detail, 20-bin GSEA similar but without direction (Extended Data Fig. 5b-c). Human vs mouse beta1: Pearson 0.44 (Fig. 4b); 25 central and 18 portal proteins strongly zonated in both (Fig. 4c); pathway NES human vs mouse Pearson 0.68 (Fig. 4d); ribosomal proteins periportal in human only (Fig. 4e-f). Desmoplasia: beta1 shrinks toward zero without inversion (Fig. 5a); 18.1% of strongly zonated proteins with significant loss (Fig. 5b); median |Delta| 0.71 for central vs 0.31 for portal proteins (Fig. 6a); zonation kept in 41.2% of portal vs 11.6% of central proteins (Fig. 6b); NES healthy vs desmoplasia scatter (Fig. 6c). Exclusion of the most altered patient gave consistent results (Supp. Fig. 8); immunofluorescence validation (Extended Data Fig. 8c-d).
- Null / calibration: parametric Wald tests from the LMM; no permutation and no calibration check. Because the slope has no random effect, its standard error comes from cells, not from patient-to-patient slope variation.
- Plots: volcano of beta1 vs -log10 Q (Fig. 3b); top-protein profiles with LOWESS (Fig. 3c); per-patient regression lines (Extended Data Fig. 4); GSEA NES bars ranked by beta1 (Fig. 3e); human vs mouse beta1 scatter (Fig. 4b); NES vs NES scatters (Figs 4d, 6c); pathway median-z heatmaps over 20 bins (Fig. 4e); healthy vs desmoplasia beta1 scatter with regression vs diagonal (Fig. 5a); |Delta beta1| plot (Fig. 5b); paired dot plots of beta1 by zone (Fig. 6a); stacked bars of lost/maintained zonation by zone (Fig. 6b).
- Software: Python (statsmodels, scikit-learn, seaborn), code at https://github.com/BorgwardtLab/Human_scDVP_Analysis (analysis), Human_scDVP_CellSelection, CellPick; data PRIDE PXD062231.
- Limitations stated: 70% completeness filter drops spatially restricted proteins; the model is "optimized for linear protein expression gradients"; cross-species comparison confounded by sex (human cohort mostly female, mice male), metabolic state, circadian timing and MS platform. Limitations for OUR design: linear slope only (PT has mid-axis peaks); robust scaling makes beta1 a noise-standardised slope, so a noisier condition shows a smaller beta1 for the same absolute gradient; random intercept only, so slope tests are cell-level; Delta beta1 from two separate fits with Wald SEs.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
The best liver precedent for level-free slope comparisons (human vs mouse, healthy vs disease, by zone) and for the figures to copy. Its statistics do not transfer as is: slope is linear, noise-standardised, and tested at the cell level with only a random intercept. Use per-specimen log-scale slopes, S3/S1 bias or centre of mass with our offset, and calibrate differences by specimen relabeling.
