## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: a Bayesian hybrid of regression and factor analysis. For sample n and gene g, expression is a
  gene intercept + a static covariate effect (A x_n, a level difference) + a dynamic term lambda_g z_n (z_n = latent
  pseudotime) + an interaction beta_pg x_np z_n that lets a covariate change the slope of gene g along z (Results "A
  Bayesian approach..."; Methods eqs 1-4). Gene trends along z are LINEAR (stated as an assumption in Discussion: "An
  assumption made by PhenoPath is that features evolve linearly with respect to pseudotime"). Input is column-centred
  expression; the vignette says it should be "comparable to logged counts, such as log2(TPM+1)" and is centre-scaled.
  So the likelihood is Gaussian on log expression, not a count model.
- Inference: coordinate-ascent mean-field variational Bayes; sparse ARD prior on beta (precision chi_pg). Authors report
  robustness over >80 initialisation/hyperparameter combinations (Results); the vignette warns the posterior is
  multi-modal and initialisation matters.
- Significance: no p-values or FDR. An interaction is "of interest" if 0 lies outside k posterior SDs of beta's
  variational mean (Methods "Ranking covariate-pathway interactions", eq. 8). Variational posteriors are typically
  over-confident, so this is not a calibrated test.
- Replicates: none modelled. Each sample/cell is an observation; covariates can be any design matrix, but there is no
  random effect for donor or specimen.
- Validation: simulation from a nonlinear mean with NB noise (deliberately misspecified), 5-50% interacting genes,
  200 and 500 samples, two noise levels, 40 replicates per condition = 960 data sets; compared pseudotime accuracy with
  Monocle 2, DPT, Wishbone, TSCAN (Kendall tau, Fig. 1e) and interaction detection with Limma Voom, DESeq2, MAST,
  Monocle 2 two-stage workflows (35,520 workflows, AUC, Fig. 1f). Real data: Shalek LPS vs PAM dendritic cells (820 cells,
  7,500 genes; pseudotime vs capture time R^2 = 0.68), TCGA COAD with MSI status (284 samples; MLH1 as positive
  control), TCGA BRCA with ER status (1,135 samples; 1,932 genes = 42% with interactions; ESR1 positive control).
- Plots: PCA coloured by pseudotime/covariate (Figs 1d, 2a, 4a, 6a); "landscape of interactions" per data set (Figs
  3a, 5a, 7a; axes not described in the legends, so not verifiable from the text); beta vs limma p- or q-value
  (Figs 3b, 5b, 7b); expression vs pseudotime split by covariate (Figs 2e, 5d, 7d); a HISTOGRAM OF CROSS-OVER POINTS
  along the trajectory, i.e. where the two covariate-specific lines meet (Fig. 7c; formula in the supplement); GO
  enrichment bars.
- Software: R/Bioconductor `phenopath` (Data availability).
- Limitations stated: linearity in pseudotime ("checks should be conducted"); suggests Gaussian processes for nonlinear
  extension (Discussion).
- Limitations for our design: we do not need the latent-axis part (our axis is fixed); with a fixed axis the model
  reduces to a linear regression with a covariate x position interaction on log expression, which is weaker than our
  NB curve models; no specimen effects; the significance rule is not calibrated.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Low. PhenoPath's value is learning a shared latent axis when a covariate bends it; we fix the axis. What carries over
is (a) the level-vs-slope decomposition (static A vs interaction beta), which our NB models already do better, and
(b) the cross-over-point histogram as a figure idea for "where along PT do IR and control curves meet or part".
