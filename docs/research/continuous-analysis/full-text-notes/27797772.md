## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model: the six-parameter impulse function of Chechik & Koller (2009), a continuous impulse-like profile (initial change, then a second steady state). The main text does not write the equation; it is in the supplement.
- Fitting: five steps (Fig. 1A): (1) cluster genes; (2) fit the impulse model to each cluster's mean profile; (3) use the three best parameter sets (by sum of squared errors, SSE) as starting points for each gene's fit; (4) significance by "random sampling" (as in EDGE, Storey 2005) and the bootstrap; (5) BH-corrected q-values. Least-squares fitting on expression values (log2 microarray, TPM for RNA-seq); no count likelihood. Multi-threaded.
- Designs: one time course (change over time) or two (case vs control), "with at least six time points".
- Replicates: not discussed in the main text.
- Validation: T-cell microarray, TH17 vs TH0, 18 time points, 7,526 probesets: 921 DE at q < 0.01; 456 shared with all three EDGE spline settings; 45 found only by ImpulseDE (including Il21, Socs3). Hiding blocks of four time points: ImpulseDE imputed best for 9 of 13 blocks vs EDGE splines (Supp. Fig. S3). RNA-seq dendritic cells, LPS vs mock, 6 time points, 3,147 genes: 1,499 DE (395 up, 1,104 down), GO enrichment as expected.
- Plots: workflow; overlap heat map; Venn diagram vs EDGE; impulse vs spline fits for one gene (Fig. 1).
- Software: R/Bioconductor `ImpulseDE` (since replaced by ImpulseDE2).
- Limitations for us: least squares, no NB, no replicate model, no transient/permanent classification (that came in ImpulseDE2); needs >= 6 time points.

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
Superseded by ImpulseDE2 for our purposes. Its only extra point is that impulse fits imputed hidden time blocks better than flexible splines on a long series, a mild argument for a parametric transient model as one rung of a model ladder.
