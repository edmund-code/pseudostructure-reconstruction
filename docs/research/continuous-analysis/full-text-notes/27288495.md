## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model: control and perturbed time courses are noisy observations (i.i.d. Gaussian noise) of one shared GP function f before the perturbation time x_p; after x_p the perturbed condition follows g, a GP constrained to cross f at x_p (crossing value marginalised). This gives an exact joint Gaussian likelihood with a closed-form covariance (eqs 5-8). Squared-exponential kernel by default; any kernel, e.g. Heinonen's non-stationary one, can be used.
- Inference: uniform prior on x_p over a grid within the observed range; posterior by evaluating the likelihood on the grid (a histogram). Hyper-parameters are fixed beforehand by maximum likelihood with x_p -> -infinity (two independent GPs). Point estimates: posterior mean, median or MAP; intervals from posterior percentiles (5-95% in Fig. 2b).
- Replicates: "We allow the case x_n = x_{n-1} since that provides a simple way to incorporate replicates" (2.1). Replicates are repeated inputs with the same i.i.d. noise; there is no replicate-level curve or variance component.
- Pre-filter: genes are first filtered by the log-likelihood ratio of independent GPs vs one integrated GP (2.5). The authors warn this can remove genuine late-perturbation genes; an alternative filter is in the supplement. There is no P value or FDR; the x_p posterior assumes the gene is perturbed.
- Validation: simulation from three kernels (SE, Matern 3/2, Matern 1/2) x three noise models (Gaussian, Student-t 6 and 3 df), hourly 0-18 h, two replicates, 100 datasets per x_p. Ranking of x_p: Spearman 0.80-0.94 for DEtime vs at best 0.67 for nsgp's EMLL at threshold 1.0 (Table 1). Real data: Arabidopsis leaves, DC3000 vs DC3000hrpA (Lewis et al. 2015), 32,578 genes (~11 h runtime).
- Pathway use (Results 3.3): genes grouped by GO or AraCyc term; "the cumulative perturbation time for each term" computed; terms ranked by the time at which more than 50% of their genes were perturbed; heat maps of the CDF of perturbation times per term (Fig. 4; Supplementary Figs S4-S10). Example: >50% of "regulation of abscisic acid biosynthetic process" genes perturbed by 2.3 hpi; JA terms later than ABA.
- Plots: posterior of x_p above the two GP fits with 95% bands (Figs 2a, 3); posterior summaries vs ground truth (Fig. 2b); per-term CDF heat maps of perturbation times (Fig. 4).
- Software: R package DEtime (github.com/ManchesterBioinference/DEtime); also a DEtime kernel in GPy (Python).
- Limitations stated: late perturbations are hard to separate from non-DE; one perturbation per gene. Limitations for us: one-sided (identical before x_p); Gaussian noise (log-scale data needed); specimens enter only as repeated points, so the posterior width reflects pooled noise, not specimen-to-specimen spread.

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
For IR vs control, a per-gene divergence position with a posterior is a good "where" summary, and the per-pathway CDF of member divergence positions is a ready-made pathway timeline. But both ends of PT may change, so it must be run from each end (or replaced by a two-sided interval model), it needs log-scale data, and specimen-to-specimen spread is not in its uncertainty; specimen-pair agreement or our relabeling would have to supply that.
