## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model: GP regression of a time series (squared exponential, Matern 5/2 or neural-network covariance); hyper-parameters (amplitude, flexibility, noise s.d.) by maximum marginal likelihood with bounded uniform priors (BFGS, multiple starts). Because the derivative of a GP is a GP, the joint posterior of the curve, its first derivative and its second derivative is Gaussian (Methods, following Boyle); their posterior variances give the errors.
- Errors on summary statistics: sample ~100 latent functions with their derivatives from the posterior, compute the statistic (maximum growth rate, doubling time, lag time) on each sample, report mean and s.d. (Results "Estimating cellular growth rates"; Methods). Example: maximum growth rate 0.16 +/- 0.002 per hour.
- Bands: pointwise s.d. of the curve and derivatives; no simultaneous bands and no test of "derivative differs from zero".
- Replicates: "Data from replicate measurements are pooled together and the algorithm applied as for a single replicate." When noise changes with time, its relative size is estimated from the across-replicate variance at each time point, smoothed with a Gaussian filter, and scaled by a fitted constant (Methods).
- Noise model: Gaussian or log-normal (on log data).
- Validation: De Brabanter et al. test functions (100 noisy datasets each) for first and second derivatives: at least as accurate as local polynomials and penalised splines (Fig. 2); synthetic Gompertz+Richards growth curves vs smoothing splines, GP more robust as n grows (Fig. 3); real data: yeast diauxic shift (7 replicates), E. coli long lag (2 replicates), amyloid assembly (15 replicates), spindle pole separation (single replicate) (Figs 4-5).
- Plots: curve with band above derivative with band (Figs 4-5); sample derivative curves (Fig. 4a inset); error-score box plots (Figs 2-3).
- Software: Python 3 (NumPy/SciPy/Pandas), GUI; http://swainlab.bio.ed.ac.uk/software/fitderiv.
- Limitations stated: results depend on hyper-parameter bounds; Matern can give rough derivatives; neural-network kernel is sensitive to priors. Limitations for us: Gaussian noise (needs log-scale data); replicates pooled as i.i.d.; pointwise, not simultaneous, error bars.

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
The transferable idea is posterior sampling of the fitted curve to get uncertainty on any derived statistic (edge position, 10-90% width, peak, maximum slope). With an NB-GAM this is done by simulating coefficients; to reflect specimen-to-specimen spread the sampling must be combined with per-specimen refits, since pooling specimens as i.i.d. (as Swain does with replicates) would understate it.
