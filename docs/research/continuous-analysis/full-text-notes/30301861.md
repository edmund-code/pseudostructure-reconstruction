## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Question: are S1 and S2 of the mouse proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) functionally different for endocytosis? Male C57Bl/6J mice, 6-12 weeks (Methods "Animals").
- Measurements: (1) immunostaining of fixed cortex for megalin (Lrp2), cubilin, Dab2, Rab5, Rab7, Rab11, Lamp1, NaPi2a, with ROI intensity histograms (Fig. 1B: ROIs in "a total of three large field images"); (2) RT-qPCR of microdissected S1, S2, S3 segments (Fig. 1C); (3) intravital multiphoton imaging of labelled lysozyme, beta-lactoglobulin, albumin (receptor-mediated endocytosis, RME) and 10-kD dextran (fluid-phase endocytosis, FPE) (Figs. 3, 5); (4) modified CLARITY tissue clearing with semi-automatic tracing of single PCTs in Imaris to measure uptake length from the glomerulus (Figs. 6-8).
- Findings: megalin protein and mRNA similar along S1-S3 (unimodal intensity histogram); cubilin decreases S1 -> S3 and is variable in S2; Dab2, Rab5, Rab7, Rab11, Lamp1 higher in S1; Rab7 and Lamp1 intensity histograms bimodal; Lamp1 correlates with NaPi2a (Results first section; Fig. 1; Supp. Fig. 3 not read). Lysozyme and albumin uptake almost exclusively in S1 even at saturating doses; dextran taken up similarly in S1 and S2 (Figs. 3, 5). Organic anion transport (MCB) higher in lysozyme-negative (S2) segments.
- Single-tubule heterogeneity: lysozyme uptake typically "ends abruptly" (a "step change in function"); uptake length 600-1,100 um vs dextran 900-2,600 um "within a single kidney" (n = 18 tubules; Fig. 7D-E); a high-dose histogram with n = 61 tubules in one kidney (Fig. 8C); uptake distance typically greater in deeper PCTs, which the authors relate to higher GFR in juxtamedullary nephrons (Discussion). The authors state the cut-off "probably" corresponds to the S1/S2 interface, "but in the absence of definitive anatomic markers we cannot be sure of this" (Discussion).
- Statistics: GraphPad Prism; one-way ANOVA with Tukey for RNA; multiple t-tests with Holm-Sidak for intensities (Methods "Statistical Analyses"). Mouse numbers per experiment are not given in the main text.
- Plot types: ROI intensity histograms (bimodal vs unimodal) (Fig. 1B); within-cell intensity profiles from basolateral to apical (Fig. 2A); SD-vs-SD scatter (Fig. 2B); intravital images; dose-response bars (Fig. 5); histograms of uptake length per traced tubule (Figs. 7D-E, 8C); 3-D renderings of single PCTs.
- Software: GraphPad Prism 7, FIJI, Imaris 9.0, Leica HyVolution2.
- Limitations (authors): labels may alter uptake; not all low-molecular-weight proteins may behave alike; why megalin and cubilin are expressed in S2 is unexplained; clearing can swell tissue; male mice only.

## Review check
- c-spatial-gradients.md:34 (row #14), marked "[abstract only]" - now superseded: full text read from the PMC article page.
- c:34, "Receptor-mediated protein uptake happens almost only in S1, while fluid-phase uptake runs much further along the PCT. Endolysosomal proteins show a bimodal (S1-high) distribution, and individual PCTs differ markedly in uptake length." - VERDICT: correct (Figs. 1, 3, 7).
- c:34, Plot types "Segmented-tubule intensity along length; intravital imaging." - VERDICT: imprecise. They measure where uptake stops along each traced tubule, not an intensity profile along its length. Replacement: "Histograms of uptake length per traced tubule; ROI intensity histograms (bimodal Rab7/Lamp1, unimodal megalin); intravital images."
- c:34, Replicates "Several mice" - VERDICT: unsupported as stated. Replacement: "Mouse numbers not given in the main text; uptake-length heterogeneity is from tubules within a single kidney (n = 18; n = 61 at high dose)."
- c:34, What it adds, "endocytosis genes should look switch-like near S1->S2" - VERDICT: misses an important point. The step is in uptake function and in endolysosomal proteins (Rab7, Lamp1, Dab2). The receptor gene Lrp2 (megalin) is flat along S1-S3 at protein and mRNA level, and cubilin decreases gradually with high S2 variability. The qPCR has only three points (S1, S2, S3), so it cannot separate a switch from a gradient at the mRNA level. Replacement: "Prior: endolysosomal machinery (Rab7, Lamp1, Dab2) should drop near S1->S2, megalin (Lrp2) should stay flat, cubilin should fall gradually. The evidence for a step is functional (uptake) and protein-level, not mRNA."
- c:34, "transport and lipid programs more graded" - VERDICT: unsupported by this paper. It does not study lipid programs, and the one transporter it shows (NaPi2a) is higher in early PCT and co-varies with Lamp1, i.e. it looks stepwise too. If this prior comes from Kaminska 2026, cite that paper only.
- c:34, "It also predicts nephron-to-nephron variation in transition position, which a repeated-unit model should estimate, not average away." - VERDICT: correct with a caveat the review misses: uptake length depends on filtered load (deeper nephrons, higher GFR, longer uptake), and the authors are not sure the cut-off equals the S1/S2 boundary. Functional uptake length is not the same as a transcriptional transition position.
- c:82 (Gaps), "The imaging (#14) shows PT-to-PT variation in transition length" - VERDICT: imprecise. Replacement: "Imaging (#14) shows that the length of PCT over which protein uptake occurs varies between tubules of one kidney (about 600-1,100 um), and is longer in deeper nephrons."
- c:85 (Gaps), kidney work "protein and imaging based (#14)" - VERDICT: correct.
- c:89 (Notes), "Schuh 2018 (#14) is also abstract only" - out of date; the full text is open on PMC.

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
Strong biological prior for S1 vs S2 endolysosomal function and for tubule-to-tubule variation in where that function stops, but the evidence is protein and uptake, not mRNA, and the variation in uptake length is partly load-dependent. For our transcript-level calls, expect Lrp2 flat, endolysosomal genes stepping down after S1, and treat between-unit spread in transition position as a quantity to estimate, without assuming it equals uptake length.
