## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Problem: "differential state" (DS) analysis: within each discrete cell subpopulation (cluster), find genes that
  change between conditions with samples as the experimental units.
- Methods compared (16; Supp. Table 1): pseudobulk (aggregate per subpopulation x sample: sum of counts, mean of log
  counts, scaled CPM, mean of sctransform residuals; tested with edgeR quasi-likelihood, limma-voom or limma-trend);
  cell-level mixed models with ~1 + group_id + (1|sample_id), REML, Satterthwaite d.f. (MM-dream/dream2 with
  variancePartition weights, MM-vst on sctransform values via lme4, MM-nbinom GLMM on counts with library-size
  offset via glmmTMB nbinom1 or blme Poisson); Anderson-Darling tests (by sample or by group); scDD; MAST.
- Simulation: NB parameters (sample-specific means, dispersions, library sizes) estimated from reference data
  (Kang PBMC; their mouse cortex vehicle samples); state changes of type DE, DP, DM, DB added to one condition. Main
  benchmark: 3 vs 3 samples, K = 3 subpopulations, 10% DS genes, mean logFC 2, ~200 cells per subpopulation-sample,
  5 replicates per scenario; null simulation 3 vs 3, K = 2.
- Findings (Results, Discussion, Fig. 5):
  - Pseudobulk methods and mixed models both perform well for DE, DM and DP; their results agree closely.
  - Mixed models are much slower (MM-nbinom and MM-vst three to four orders of magnitude slower than pseudobulk) and
    "their computational cost may not be worth the flexibility they provide".
  - Mixed models and limma-trend on mean log counts systematically underestimate logFCs (Supp. Fig. 5a).
  - AD tests deviate most from uniform p-values under the null; AD/scDD/MAST give many isolated (mostly false) hits;
    methods on vst residuals have high FDR.
  - Per-subpopulation ("local") FDR adjustment is recommended; global adjustment across subpopulations is very
    conservative (Fig. 2a).
  - FDR control "varies drastically with the number of cells"; for pseudobulk ~100 cells per subpopulation-sample
    were enough (Fig. 2b). Unbalanced designs had no effect except edgeR on scaled CPM; more replicates gave a
    "modest" gain (Supp. Figs. 6-8, not read).
- Real data: snRNA-seq of mouse cortex, 4 vehicle vs 4 LPS mice; edgeR on summed pseudobulk per subpopulation
  (>= 10 cells in >= 2 samples per group); 915 DS genes (FDR < 0.05, |logFC| > 1), 751 in a single subpopulation;
  consensus clustering of per-subpopulation logFCs; per-cell "effect coefficients".
- Replicates: central; samples are the units throughout.
- Continuous trajectories: not covered. Everything is per discrete subpopulation; differential abundance is named as
  a separate, complementary analysis.
- Plot types: TPR-vs-FDR points at 1/5/10% cutoffs (Fig. 2); UpSet plot of method concordance (Fig. 3); UMAP and
  pseudobulk MDS (Fig. 4b-c); pseudobulk heatmap split by subpopulation and gene consensus cluster with per-cell
  effect-coefficient violins (Fig. 4d); method-by-criterion summary heatmap (Fig. 5).
- Software: R/Bioconductor muscat; benchmark code github.com/HelenaLC/muscat-comparison.
- Limitations stated: power is biased toward large subpopulations; FDR control unstable with cell number; results
  depend on how subpopulations are defined; full-distribution methods need bespoke development.

## Review check
- d-comparing-trajectories.md:66 (row 23), "muscat: pseudobulk and mixed models for multi-sample, multi-condition DS
  analysis" - VERDICT: correct but misses the key result. Its benchmark (3 vs 3 samples) found pseudobulk and
  mixed models about equally good. Mixed models were far slower and underestimated fold changes, and the authors
  used edgeR on summed pseudobulk for their real data. This contrasts with the Zimmerman claim in the same row
  (GLMMs more powerful than pseudobulk) and should be stated.
  Replacement: "muscat (benchmark of 16 methods; 3 vs 3 simulated samples): pseudobulk (edgeR or limma on summed
  counts) and cell-level mixed models performed about equally. Mixed models were 2-4 orders of magnitude slower and
  shrank fold changes toward zero. Distribution tests (Anderson-Darling, scDD) and MAST gave false hits. It
  recommends FDR adjustment within each subpopulation. It covers discrete subpopulations only, not trajectories."
- d:66, Plots "AUCC and FDR curves; PCA of pseudobulk samples" - VERDICT: for muscat, partly wrong: it shows TPR/FDR
  points at fixed cutoffs, UpSet concordance plots, and an MDS (not PCA) of pseudobulks, plus a pseudobulk heatmap.
  (AUCC may belong to Squair; not checked here.)
- d:66, For us "The continuous-axis equivalents are pseudobulk-per-specimen-per-bin or a GAMM with specimen random
  smooths" - VERDICT: correct as an inference; supported by muscat's design (aggregate per group x sample, test
  with a bulk engine). Add: if bins act as subpopulations, adjust FDR within each bin (muscat: global adjustment
  very conservative), and muscat itself never tested fewer than 3 samples per group in the main text.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Supports specimen-level aggregation (pseudobulk per specimen per bin, NB/edgeR-style) as at least as good as mixed
models, and much cheaper. It gives no help for continuous axes, curve shapes, or 2 vs 2 designs; its main benchmark
used 3 vs 3 samples.
