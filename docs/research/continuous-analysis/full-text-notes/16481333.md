## What the paper actually does (from the full text, as extracted)
- Model / statistic: per gene least-squares regression with time as a QUANTITATIVE variable, polynomial up to degree J-1 (J = number of time points), I-1 dummy variables for experimental groups (series), and dummy-by-time interactions at each degree. Step 1: global F-test (all coefficients except the intercept zero), p-values BH-FDR corrected; genes passing go to step 2. Step 2: stepwise regression (backward, forward, stepwise backward or stepwise forward) per selected gene to find which group and time terms are significant; the significance level is corrected for the number of possible variables; an optional R-squared filter keeps well-fitted genes (example 0.6). Significant genes are then clustered (on data or on regression coefficients) for display.
- Null / calibration: F-test p-values with BH FDR; the authors state the assumptions are independence of observations, homoscedasticity and normality, and recommend repeated-measures / longitudinal methods when the same subjects are measured over time.
- Replicates: replicated hybridizations per group-time combination are separate observations; unbalanced designs allowed.
- Validation: rat bromobenzene toxicogenomics (2,665 genes; 5 groups: untreated, corn oil, low, medium, high dose; 6, 24, 48 h; 2-6 replicates; 54 slides): 155 genes at FDR 0.01 and R2 >= 0.6 (53 shared with LIMMA, 102 maSigPro-only, 10 LIMMA-only). Simulation: 600 patterned genes (continuous induction, transitory repression, multi-group induction) plus 2,000 flat, 100 runs, FDR 0.05: maSigPro held the FDR, LIMMA often exceeded it.
- Plot types: cluster plots of member profiles across all conditions with the cluster average (Fig 1); cluster average profile per group with points, joined means and fitted curves (Fig 2); simulated examples (Fig 3); FDR box plots maSigPro vs LIMMA (Fig 4).
- Software: R; Bioconductor contributed packages and author web page.
- Targets "a relatively small (<6) number of time points" with several series; for longer series the authors suggest piecewise or spline regression as future work.
- Not in this paper: RNA-seq, negative binomial GLM ("Next maSigPro" is Nueda 2014), gene sets/maSigFun (Nueda 2009).
- Limitations for OUR design: Gaussian least squares on log ratios; independence of observations (our units within a specimen are not independent replicates); polynomials of degree J-1 suit few, fixed time points, not a dense continuous axis.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Mainly of historical value for us: a Gaussian polynomial model for short, discrete time courses with independent replicate arrays. Our NB spline curves per specimen already go beyond it; its useful idea, group-by-time interaction terms selected stepwise, is better served by our specimen-level NB models with species or condition interactions.
