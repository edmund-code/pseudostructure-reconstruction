## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: three steps (main text; Online Methods). (1) GENIE3 (random-forest regression of each gene on TF expression; GRNboost = gradient-boosting variant on Spark) gives TF-target importance weights; several thresholding rules make TF co-expression modules, split by the sign of the Spearman correlation, kept if >= 20 genes. (2) RcisTarget keeps modules whose TF motif is enriched near the TSS (NES > 3) and prunes targets without motif support; the result is a regulon. Only positively correlated (activating) targets are kept; repression was dropped because negative modules had little motif enrichment. (3) AUCell scores each regulon in each cell as the area under the recovery curve of the regulon's genes in that cell's expression ranking (ties such as zeros randomly ordered), i.e. whether a subset of the set sits near the top of the ranking. AUC scores are used directly or binarized with per-regulon thresholds (automatic or manual) and then clustered (t-SNE, hierarchical clustering).
- Null / calibration: none for activity scores; motif enrichment uses NES > 3. Binarization thresholds are set from the AUC distributions. No P values for regulon activity differences.
- Replicates: none modelled; cells are the units. The paper reports that binary regulon activity groups cells by type across tumours and across species (human vs mouse brain), where expression-based clustering groups by tumour or species (Fig. 2c, Fig. 3; Supp. Figs 7, 11).
- Validation: mouse brain (3,005 cells): 151 regulons from 1,046 modules; clustering sensitivity 0.88, specificity 0.99, ARI > 0.80; robust to 100-cell subsamples and 1/3 reads. Human brain cross-species check; oligodendroglioma (4,043 cells, 6 tumours) and melanoma (1,252 cells, 14 lesions); NFATC2 knock-down in A375 and IHC on 25 melanomas; ChIP-seq support for MITF/STAT targets. AUCell is described as independent of expression units and normalization because it is rank-based within each cell.
- Plots: workflow schematic; regulon x cell-type heatmaps; t-SNE on the binary regulon matrix coloured by most active regulon (Fig 1d); cross-species joint clustering (Fig 2c); diffusion map on the binary matrix reconstructing a tumour differentiation trajectory (Supp. Fig. 8); IHC heatmap; ChIP aggregation plots.
- Software: R/Bioconductor GENIE3, RcisTarget, AUCell; GRNboost in Scala on Spark; links at http://scenic.aertslab.org. pySCENIC is not mentioned in this paper.
- Limitations (authors): co-expression modules include indirect targets (hence motif pruning); no repression; thresholds for binarization are partly manual. For us: motif-based regulons need a full gene universe around TSS ranks; with species-specific probe panels, regulon members missing from a panel change the AUC.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
A regulatory layer (TF regulons) scored per unit with a within-unit rank statistic, which is free of per-gene level offsets and so suits cross-species shape comparison if regulons are restricted to shared measured genes. No inference; any test must be done on per-specimen curves of the scores. Motif pruning (RcisTarget) is the part that adds information beyond co-expression.
