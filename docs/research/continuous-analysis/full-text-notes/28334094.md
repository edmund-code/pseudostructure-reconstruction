## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: three parts (Methods 2.1-2.3). (1) Gene-level statistic: smooth each gene's log-normalized, z-scored time course with penalized cubic B-splines (lambda by GCV on the top 1,000 genes), run FPCA, keep L = 3 eigenfunctions (about 86% of variance), and compute a functional F statistic (RSS of a constant fit versus the FPCA fit). (2) Overlap weights: for a gene in several sets, a concurrent functional linear model regresses its curve on the eigenfunctions of each of its sets (from a per-set FPCA), with an elastic-net penalty (recommended lambda1 = 0.4, lambda2 = 0.01, chosen by simulation AUC; Supp. S6). The gene's weight in set k is the share of its fitted signal attributed to set k; weights sum to 1 across a gene's sets. (3) Set test: a competitive, weighted Mann-Whitney U of members' F against background genes, with CAMERA's exchangeable inter-gene correlation rho inflating the variance (Barry et al. formula; Supp. S3), one-sided t with n-1 df. It reduces to CAMERA's rank test when all weights are 1.
- Null / calibration: analytic (asymptotic variance of the weighted U under exchangeable correlation, rho estimated from the data as in CAMERA; Supp. S4). Simulated type I error at 5%: 0.001-0.010 (Table 1).
- Replicates: the whole analysis is run per subject (FPCA "applied to each subject separately"), giving one P value per set per subject; per-subject P values are combined within each group by Fisher's combined probability test, then Bonferroni (Results 3.4). Subjects are therefore meta-analysed, not modelled as random effects. The authors note Fisher's method is chosen to detect subject-specific patterns and that they will look for options with better type I error protection (Discussion).
- Validation: simulation of 5,000 genes, 90 sets (18 with signal), 2,000 genes shared by 2-9 sets, 20 replicates, small/large noise; extra two-factor correlation structures (Supp. S5.5). FUNNEL beats CAMERA in power and ROC/AUC. TcGSA on the same simulation called all 90 sets significant, signal or not; on the real data 23/186 KEGG sets had size problems, 74 (40%) failed to converge, and 139 were significant (Results 3.3-3.4). Real data: influenza H3N2 challenge, 17 subjects (9 symptomatic, 8 asymptomatic), 16 time points, 186 KEGG sets. FUNNEL found 32 (symptomatic) and 22 (asymptomatic) sets; CAMERA found 4 and 7.
- Estimated mean within-set correlation on the real data (subject 1) was 0.055 (Supp. Fig. 10A).
- Plots: overlap histogram (Fig 2); ROC curves FUNNEL vs CAMERA (Fig 3); heatmap of -log10 P per subject x pathway plus the Fisher column per group (Fig 4); heatmap of estimated membership weights per subject for top overlapping genes (Fig 5A); one gene's curve against the curves of the two sets it belongs to (Fig 5B).
- Software: R package FUNNEL, https://github.com/yunzhang813/FUNNEL-GSEA-R-Package (abstract).
- Limitations (authors): designed for continuous (log, filtered) data; an NB/discrete extension is future work; Fisher combination may not protect type I error well. For us: the statistic is a self-contained "non-flat" F per gene, then a competitive set test; it needs many time points per subject (16 here), and per-subject analysis presumes each specimen has a full curve.

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
The overlap weighting is the distinctive idea and transfers to positional curves: assign a shared transporter to the set whose program its PT curve resembles. The per-specimen test plus combination mirrors our specimen-as-unit stance, but Fisher's method only says "at least one specimen" and FUNNEL needs dense curves per subject. Its independent comparison is the clearest warning in our reading list that a TcGSA mixed model can call nearly every set.
