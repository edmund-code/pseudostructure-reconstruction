## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic (PLAGE): standardize each gene to mean 0, variance 1 over samples; for each pathway take the genes-by-samples matrix Y and its SVD Y = W D C; the first metagene w (gene weights, signed) and its coefficients c_j are the pathway "activity" per sample; c_j is, up to scale, the weighted sum of the standardized member genes (Methods). Rank-1 least-squares model y_ij = alpha_i chi_j + error. The fraction of variance explained by the first metagene (lambda^2 / total) measures coordination (ribosome 0.53, oxidative phosphorylation 0.23, noise about 0.05).
- Sign: the up/down call is "somewhat arbitrar[y]"; a pathway "will typically have some genes with higher and some with lower mean expression" (Table 2 legend). Weights can be negative, so members moving in opposite directions load on the same metagene with opposite signs (Table 3, e.g. CYP2A6 -0.42 and CYP1B1 +0.38 in one pathway).
- Null / significance: two-group t statistic on activity levels (also correlation with a continuous phenotype, and one-way ANOVA); p-values from 10,000 sample-label permutations, each recording the maximum statistic over all pathways (a family-wise style correction).
- Replicates: individuals are the samples (43 in the diabetes data, 75 in the smoking data); activity computed from all samples together, ignoring class labels.
- Validation: real data only (Mootha diabetes muscle data; airway epithelium of current, former and never smokers), compared with GSEA. Notes that GSEA ranked the strongly co-expressed ribosome set first in 3 of 4 comparisons and suggests GSEA may confuse strong coordination with a consistent group difference.
- Plot types: colormaps (heatmaps) of pathway activity levels across samples and of member-gene expression (Fig 4); schematic of rank-1 approximation (Fig 2); scatter of activity versus blood glucose by group (Fig 3).
- Software: PLAGE web program (Duke URL given in the paper).
- Limitations stated: only the first metagene is used; higher metagenes may hold structure.
- Limitations for OUR design: the SVD captures whichever variation is largest across the columns included; with bins from several specimens, species or AKI status, the first metagene can be the between-specimen offset rather than position. A pathway with two independent sub-trends puts the second into metagene 2, which PLAGE discards.

## Review check
- b-continuous-pathways.md:23 (row 6) - "PLAGE takes the first right singular vector of the set's standardized matrix (the eigengene)." - VERDICT: correct (Methods, Eq 1).
- b:23 - Fit: "PLAGE gives the eigengene, which is sign-ambiguous and dominated by the highest-variance members." - VERDICT: half wrong. Sign-ambiguous: correct (Table 2 legend). "Dominated by the highest-variance members": wrong, because every gene is standardized to unit variance before the SVD; the metagene is dominated by the members that co-vary most strongly with each other. It also misses the point that matters for us: weights are signed, so rising and falling members do not cancel in PLAGE as they do in a median. Proposed replacement: "PLAGE gives the eigengene. Its sign is arbitrary. Genes are standardized first, so it is dominated by the most strongly co-varying members, not the most variable ones. Its weights are signed, so members that rise and members that fall both load on it instead of cancelling, which a median cannot do; a second, independent sub-trend is lost to the second metagene. Compute it per specimen, or after removing specimen means, or it will capture the species or specimen offset."
- b:67 (idea 2) - "or the PLAGE eigengene as a sensitivity check" - VERDICT: correct; given the signed weights it is a better check against median cancellation than the review implies.
- b:28 (row 11, WGCNA) - "Eigengene versus median: the eigengene weights genes by variance and has an arbitrary sign" - VERDICT: same error for eigengenes computed on standardized data (see the WGCNA notes).

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
A per-specimen PLAGE eigengene on standardized specimen-by-bin profiles is a cheap companion to the median program curve: signed weights keep opposing members, the explained-variance fraction is a coherence measure, and the gene weights show which members drive the curve. Orient the sign by a fixed reference (e.g. positive correlation with the median curve or with position) and never run it across pooled species.
