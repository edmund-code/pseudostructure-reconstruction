## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: for the four gap genes (hb, Kr, kni, gt), estimate at each position x along the AP axis the mean expression and the 4x4 covariance C(x) across embryos, assume Gaussian fluctuations, and apply Bayes' rule with a uniform prior to get the posterior P(x*|{g}) of implied position given a nucleus's expression levels (Results, eq. 1-3). A "decoding map" P(x*|x) plots implied vs actual position for an embryo (eq. 4). With all four genes the posterior width is about 0.01 L along nearly the whole axis (Fig. 3D); one to three genes leave ambiguities (multiple peaks) (Fig. 3A-C, S1C-E).
- Discretization: collapsing profiles into on/off domains (threshold 0.5, or per-gene optimized thresholds) makes decoding ambiguous even in WT and destroys predictions (Results; Methods; Supp. Fig. S1F-H). The authors argue graded levels act as an analog coordinate.
- Absolute levels: mutant and WT embryos were stained and imaged together so that levels are in the same absolute units; re-normalizing mutant profiles to 0-1 (keeping shape, dropping magnitude) gave much worse predictions (Methods, "The significance of absolute concentrations"; Supp. Fig. S6H-K).
- Null / calibration: no hypothesis test of information; validation is predictive. The decoder built from WT, with no free parameters, predicts pair-rule stripe positions in six maternal mutants: about 70 stripes agree within embryo-to-embryo variability (Fig. 5), with a few failures at map discontinuities; it also predicts the time shifts of Eve stripes during n.c. 14 (Fig. 6). A triple mutant gives a flat map (control).
- Replicates: the noise model is embryo-to-embryo variability at each known position: decoder from 38 WT embryos in a 40-44 min window; covariance estimated in 7 independent WT data sets (24-102 embryos each); mutants 15-40 embryos per line. Position x is measured physically (fraction of egg length), independently of expression.
- Plots: encoding distribution P(g|x) and posterior P(x|g) for one gene (Fig. 2); decoding maps P(x*|x) with 1-4 genes (Fig. 3) and in mutants (Fig. 4); predicted vs observed stripe positions (Fig. 5); time-resolved decoding maps (Fig. 6).
- Software: custom MATLAB code (Key Resources table); no package.
- Limitations (authors): Gaussian approximation; spatial averaging matters only at map discontinuities; DV variation not studied. For us: the method needs position known independently of expression, many replicates at each position to estimate the covariance, and levels in common absolute units.

## Review check
- continuous-analysis/b-continuous-pathways.md:42 (row 25) - "positional error sigma_x(x) from the local slope and noise (multi-gene with covariance)" - VERDICT: correct as a description of the framework; in this paper the error is the width of the Bayesian posterior built from position-dependent means and covariances.
- b:42 - "Single gap genes carry about 2 bits, 'twice as much as would be expected' from on/off domains" - not from this paper (Dubuis 2013, not checked here). This paper supports the direction of the claim: on/off discretization loses most decoding precision (Supp. Fig. S1F-H).
- b:42 - "Four genes jointly decode position to about 1% accuracy (an optimal Bayesian decoder)." - VERDICT: correct (Fig. 3D; sigma_x about 0.01 L).
- b:42 - Plots "sigma_x(x) curves; bits per gene or gene set; decoded versus true position maps" - VERDICT: correct for the framework; this paper's central figure is the decoding map plus predicted-vs-observed positions.
- b:42 - Repl. "Many embryos (replicates estimate the noise)" - VERDICT: correct, and it is the crux for us: 38 embryos at physically known positions.
- b:42 and idea 4 (b:85-90), synthesis continuous-analysis-literature.md:72-91 (Recommendation 2) - "Train on one specimen and test on the other; then train on mouse and test on human ... compare each pathway's bits with the 1.58-bit ceiling" - VERDICT: misses two important points (this changes Recommendation 2).
  (1) Positional information is defined relative to a position measured independently of expression. Our PT coordinate is reconstructed from the transcriptome, so a pathway's "bits" measure agreement with a consensus expression axis, not anatomical position. Held-out genes or count splitting reduce, but do not remove, this dependence.
  (2) The decoder works on absolute levels and fails when levels are re-normalized (Supp. Fig. S6H-K). Our species-specific probe panels make human and mouse levels non-comparable, so a mouse-trained decoder applied to human levels is invalid unless the features are level-free (for example within-specimen ranks or standardized curves), which changes the method.
  Proposed replacement for the recommendation's "What it is": "Decode the reconstructed coordinate from a pathway's held-out genes within a species: train on one specimen and test on the other. Report bits and sigma_x(x) relative to the consensus coordinate, not to anatomical position. Cross-species transfer needs level-free features (within-specimen standardized or ranked expression), because probe panels shift levels and the Bayesian decoder depends on absolute levels (Petkova 2019)."
- b:126 (gap: no application to adult tissue pathways) - not testable from this paper; the authors say the approach applies to any system with positional information in molecular concentrations (Discussion).

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Conceptually the right "continuous beats discrete" metric, and the paper directly shows discretizing graded profiles into on/off domains loses positional precision. But its requirements (independent position, many replicates per position, common absolute units) are exactly what our design lacks. Use it within species, against the reconstructed coordinate, with held-out genes and unit-level noise, and state that the bits are relative to that coordinate.
