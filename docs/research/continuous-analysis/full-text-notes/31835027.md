## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Argument: a human CCF must handle inter-individual variation and multiple scales; tissues lie on a spectrum from stereotyped (register to a known template) to disordered (learn location from data), and most need both ("a combination of direct registration and data-driven learning of location") (section "Organizational differences").
- Coordinate types (section "Reference coordinate systems"; Box/figure "Types of coordinate systems"): anatomical plane (Cartesian RAS, physical distances, needs normalisation across sizes); landmark-based axial (origin and orientation from conserved landmarks, e.g. Talairach AC-PC); complex nonstandard systems with local nonlinear warps (spline spine coordinates; cylindrical knee cartilage; an intratumour axis using the tumour-stroma interface).
- Named open problems: histological "conserved histological modules or repetitive geometrical structures", e.g. lung bronchiole branching and "the repeating (but non-fixed number) colonic crypts"; hierarchical multi-scale coordinate systems (section "Key developments needed").
- Template construction: global then local registration; intensity- vs landmark-based; iterative population templates (Allen CCF v3 from 1,675 mice; ICBM-152 with landmark-supervised seeding); for few, uncontrolled human samples, a Fréchet/Karcher mean template, including a fast algorithm for the Gromov-Wasserstein Fréchet mean (section "Constructing an anatomical template").
- Learned CCF: "Reconstructing an atlas from its own features" - mapping dissociated cells to space from expression with in situ references, and de novo assuming expression "smoothness"; "Such approaches extend the concept of ordering cells along a developmental continuum ('pseudotime') to ordering cells along a spatial gradient ('pseudospace')". Works best for spatially restricted stereotyped cell types and for gradients (liver, embryos).
- Uncertainty: since location may not be uniquely encoded in expression, "approaches that allow a measure of the confidence of the mapping will be extremely important"; probable locations are still useful (section "Mapping a different data modality").
- Variation as data: registration deformation fields can be analysed (e.g. PCA of shape modes in the Cardiac Atlas Project) to study inter-individual variation (section "The promise of a CCF").
- Plot types: conceptual figures only. Software: none. Replicates: n/a.

## Review check
- c-spatial-gradients.md:41 (row #16), "Perspective on a common coordinate framework (CCF). Distinguishes anatomical-plane coordinates from landmark-based axial coordinates, whose origin and orientation are anchored to conserved landmarks. Specimens are registered to templates to remove between-specimen size differences. Repeating units (crypts) are named as a hard case." - VERDICT: correct.
- c:41, What it adds, "Vocabulary to describe our pseudospace as a landmark-free, expression-anchored axial CCF for a repeated unit, a case Rood et al. name but leave unsolved." - VERDICT: misses an important point. Rood et al. already describe the expression-anchored, learned coordinate ("pseudospace", de novo under smoothness) as one of the two main CCF strategies; what they leave open is repeated histological units and multi-scale hierarchy. They also call for mapping confidence (probable locations) and for treating registration deformations as data on inter-individual variation, both directly relevant to our repeated-unit model and to the "coordinate uncertainty" gap.
  Proposed replacement: "Vocabulary and framing. Rood et al. name two routes to a coordinate: register to landmarks, or learn location from expression ('pseudospace'). They list repeated histological units (crypts) as unsolved and ask for mapping confidence and for analysing between-specimen deformations as data. Our pseudospace is the learned route applied to a repeated unit; per-unit warps or positions are the 'deformations' they suggest studying."

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
Framing only. Use it to position the coordinate as a learned (not landmark-registered) axial CCF for a repeated unit, and to justify reporting position uncertainty and between-unit variation. It offers no method or statistic to adopt; the Gromov-Wasserstein Fréchet-mean template idea is relevant to the reconstruction line rather than the gene analyses.
