## What the paper does
- **Homology graph.** Reciprocal BLAST of whole transcriptomes (tblastx) or proteomes (blastp), E < 1e-6; reciprocal
  edges only; edges below 0.25 of a gene's maximum dropped; weights normalised per gene and passed through a tanh
  (Methods, "Generate gene homology graph"). All homologs enter, so one gene can link to many.
- **Manifolds.** Each species is run through SAM (median-library normalisation, log2(x+1), k = 20, 150 PCs); the last
  SAM pass runs PCA on all genes (300 PCs).
- **Joint space.** Each species' standardised expression is projected onto its own PCs and, after translating features
  through the row-normalised homology graph (a gene's expression in the other species = weighted mean of its
  homologs), onto the partner's PCs; the two projections are concatenated (600 dimensions).
- **Cell mapping.** k = 20 cross-species neighbours by cosine; neighbourhoods coarsened with each cell's Leiden cluster
  (resolution 3); mutual nearest neighbourhoods by the geometric mean of the two directions; graphs stitched.
- **Homology refinement.** Gene-pair correlations are computed over the combined manifold (cross-species expression
  imputed from mapped neighbours, within-species expression kNN-smoothed); negative correlations set to 0; the weights
  are re-transformed; three iterations by default (Methods, "Homology graph refinement").
- **Outputs.** Cell-type alignment scores (mean fraction of mutual cross-species neighbours, edges < 0.1 dropped);
  gene pairs that drive a cell-type mapping (top 1,000 by a product score, then filtered by a within-species Wilcoxon
  DE p < 0.01 over cells, SAM weight ≥ 0.2, detection ≥ 5%); paralog substitutions.
- **Paralog substitution** (Results, Figure 3; Methods). A gene whose expression correlation with a paralog exceeds its
  correlation with its eggNOG ortholog by > 0.3 (fixed threshold). Frog–zebrafish: 8,286 orthologs and 7,093 paralogs
  used; 565 substitutions, 209 with an absent or non-specific ortholog; recent paralogs substitute more often
  (Figure 3C). A per-cell-type substitution score is normalised by the number of DE genes and of paralogs.
  **No null distribution and no p-value** for substitutions; the 0.3 threshold is the call.
- **Circularity check** (reviewer 1 asked whether substituting paralogs drive the mapping, "leading to a tautology"):
  rebuilding the manifold from 1:1 orthologs only recovered 70% of the substitutions; the rest sat near the threshold
  (Figure 3—figure supplement 1).
- **Validation.** Frog–zebrafish embryo atlases: 26 of 27 ontogeny-expected pairs linked (Figure 2B); Seurat, LIGER,
  Harmony, Scanorama and BBKNN on eggNOG 1:1 orthologs failed to align (Figure 2E). **SAMap restricted to 1:1 orthologs
  gave "similar, albeit weaker" mappings**, so "SAMap's performance is owed in large part to its robust, atlas stitching
  approach" rather than to the many-to-many homology (Results, frog–zebrafish). Flatworms; seven species from sponge to
  mouse (mouse = an embryo atlas; no mouse–human or other close-species test). Cell-type leave-out experiments.
- **Replicates.** None: cells are the units, one dataset per species, no specimen-level inference.
- **Software.** Python, `sc-samap` (README v3.0.0, Python ≥ 3.11, NCBI BLAST; BLAST step "up to around 4 hours").

## Fit to our question
SAMap's gene-level output is the paralog-substitution call, which notebook 88's P1 already tests on the PT curve, with
a matched random-gene null that SAMap lacks (88: 6 of 32 pairs against a null mean of 7.5). Its many-to-many homology
helped little even at frog–zebrafish distance (the 1:1 version gave similar mappings), and 93–94% of our PT-expressed probe-clean genes are 1:1.
Its unit is a cell in a kNN manifold, not a position on a curve, and it has no replicate structure.
