## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: impulse model, product of two sigmoids with six parameters: initial, peak and steady-state levels (h0, h1, h2), onset and offset times (t1, t2) and ONE shared slope beta (Eq 1; separate slopes "did not improve overall fit"). Least squares (Gaussian noise), conjugate gradient, 100 random restarts. Onset is defined as time-to-half-peak, chosen because it was numerically more stable than t1, half-time-to-peak or steepest-change time, and is invariant to rescaling (Methods).
- Validation: 76 yeast time courses (5-10 points), 6,209 genes. Leave-one-out imputation: impulse model better than polynomials (degree 2-5), cubic Hermite, interpolating, approximating and smoothing splines, especially with few time points (Fig 2); impulse-based KNN 20% lower error than KNN-impute and 35% lower than spline clustering (Fig 3). Robustness: onsets after adding N(0, 0.1) noise correlate 0.89 with originals; Gaussian timing jitter preserves onset (Fig 13). Coverage: up to 95% of genes fit within half an SD in some conditions, about 75-78% typically (Fig 14). "Impulse-ness" by time-shuffling null: on average 35% of genes (54% in diamide) are better fit than shuffled profiles (Fig 16).
- Timing versus function: only the onset is analysed ("We chose to focus on the onset response time"). For each GO category and condition, member onsets are compared with onsets of SIBLING categories (other children of the same parent) by a Wilcoxon test; 151 sub-categories differ at p < 1e-5 (Bonferroni) in at least one condition. Diamide: mitochondrial ribosome genes are induced early, cytosolic ribosome genes repressed later (Fig 5), with an early-repressed subgroup enriched for non-structural ribosome-related genes (10/33 vs 3/115) and paralog pairs split between early and late. Across conditions the mitochondrion responds earlier than the cytosolic ribosome in 16 of 76 conditions. A "response timeline" (median onset vs median peak per GO category, greedy selection of mutually ordered categories, Fig 6; gamma irradiation: nucleolus 2.2 min, non-membrane organelle 3.4, cytoplasmic part 5.5, membrane 8.1). Stronger repression goes with earlier onset in timed categories.
- Mechanism: TF ChIP-chip binding affinity correlates with activation onset (38 of 48 TF-condition pairs significant near the chance-level binding cutoff; e.g. MET32, MET31 under amino-acid/adenine starvation; some negative correlations, YAP1, HSF1).
- Null / significance: Wilcoxon tests with Bonferroni or BH; time-shuffling null for impulse-ness. Genes are treated as independent observations in the category tests.
- Replicates: none modelled; single time courses per condition; reliability from noise perturbation.
- Plot types: impulse parameter schematic and example fits (Fig 1); onset-time histograms per sibling GO category plus onset-versus-peak scatter (Fig 4); onset vs peak for two gene groups (Fig 5); category timeline of median onset vs median peak with SEM bars (Figs 6, 10, 11).
- Software: none released (MATLAB used for comparisons).
- Limitations stated: at most two transitions (fails for three-transition KCl responses and periodic profiles); mRNA level reflects both transcription and degradation; no mechanistic prior.
- Limitations for OUR design: shared slope forces the rise and fall widths to be equal; a time axis starting at a perturbation has a natural "resting" baseline, which a PT axis lacks; gene-level Wilcoxon tests ignore inter-gene correlation and replicates.

- b:35 - Fit (a): "The impulse model extends our single logistic edge to mid-PT peaks with TWO positions (onset and offset), giving a principled 'transient versus permanent' class." - VERDICT: correct in substance (h2 versus h0 distinguishes transient from sustained change); add that this model shares one slope between the two transitions, so the two edge widths are equal unless that constraint is relaxed (ImpulseDE2 differs).
- Missed link (b:46-61, idea 1 and b:121 gaps): this paper already runs a linear-axis "position enrichment": each GO category's onsets against the onsets of its sibling categories, Wilcoxon, Bonferroni. It is a direct precedent for idea 1 (a linear-axis analogue of PSEA, with a structured background), though its null treats genes as independent. Proposed addition to idea 1: "Chechik & Koller 2009 compared each GO category's onset times with those of its sibling categories (Wilcoxon); the sibling background is a useful alternative to all positional genes."

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Two practical borrowings: define an edge position as position-to-half-amplitude (scale-free and more stable than the fitted midpoint parameter), and draw a pathway "timeline" of median edge position versus median amplitude per pathway, with specimen-level bars. Its category-versus-siblings onset test is the closest linear-axis precedent for position-set enrichment, but needs a correlation-aware, specimen-calibrated null before use.
