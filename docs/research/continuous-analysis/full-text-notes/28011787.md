## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Input: a non-negative cells x genes matrix "comparable to log(TPM+1)" (Methods). Not counts.
- Model: f(t) = 2 mu0 / (1 + exp(-k (t - t0))), Gaussian likelihood y ~ N(f(t), sigma^2). mu0 = "average peak expression level", k = activation strength (sign = up or down), t0 = activation time. Note the shape: the curve runs from 0 to 2 mu0 (k > 0) or from 2 mu0 to 0 (k < 0); the low state is fixed at zero expression, so it has no free baseline. f(t0) = mu0.
- Fitting: maximum likelihood by L-BFGS-B. Optional zero-inflated likelihood (after Pierson & Yau 2015), fitted by EM, roughly 10x slower; it imputes dropouts (Fig. 1C) and gives a posterior predictive density (Fig. 1D).
- Test: k = 0 gives the constant model N(mu0, sigma^2); likelihood-ratio statistic referred to chi-square with 2 df. (t0 is not identified under the null, which the paper does not discuss.)
- Null / calibration: BH FDR. The authors warn: "in practice this may lead to an inflated false discovery rate due to the assumption that pseudotimes are fixed".
- Replicates: none; cells are observations.
- Validation: one application, Trapnell 2014 myoblasts, 11,253 genes, 2,336 significant at 5% FDR; NDC80 example (k = -8.71, t0 = 17.61 on a 0-77 pseudotime). No simulation or benchmark in the main text.
- Plots: one gene's points with the MLE sigmoid (Fig. 1B); zero-inflated fit with imputed zeros (Fig. 1C); posterior predictive density (Fig. 1D). No t0 x k scatter.
- Software: R/Bioconductor `switchde`.
- Width: for a logistic in t, the 10%-90% rise spans ln(81)/|k| ~ 4.39/|k| (my check of the review's arithmetic; correct for this model, since the 10% and 90% points of 0..2 mu0 are at t0 -/+ ln(9)/k).

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
A citation for the position/steepness parameterisation of an edge, nothing more. For PT the sigmoid needs a free baseline, an NB likelihood with exposure offset, specimen terms and calibration that does not rely on the chi-square LRT (whose null is non-standard because t0 vanishes under k = 0).
