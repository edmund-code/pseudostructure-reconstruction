## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Question: why mouse and human motor-neuron (MN) differentiation share the same gene-regulatory sequence but run at
  different speeds ("developmental allochrony").
- Data: embryo immunostaining (mouse E9.0-E11.5 vs human CS11-CS19, Fig. 1); in vitro ESC -> MN differentiation in both
  species with RT-qPCR (n = 3 in triplicate per species, Fig. 2G) and bulk RNA-seq time courses (mouse n = 3, human
  n = 3, Fig. 2H).
- Global scaling factor: from the matrix of pairwise Pearson correlations between mouse and human time-point
  transcriptomes, a linear fit through the high-correlation ridge gives a factor of 2.5 +/- 0.2 (median +/- sd; Fig. 2I,
  "White line shows a linear fit of the Pearson correlation with temporal scaling factor"). Preprint methods (via
  WebFetch): the factor minimises the Euclidean distance between the line and each inter-species time-point pair,
  weighted by that pair's Pearson correlation.
- Cluster and gene factors: genes clustered by dynamics (preprint, via WebFetch: DP_GP_cluster, an infinite Gaussian
  process mixture); the clusters containing Pax6, Irx3, Olig2, Nkx2.2, Isl1 and Tubb3 each fit ~2.5 (Fig. 2J); per-gene
  time factors are 2-3 (Fig. S2F,G); four cluster pairs rich in orthologues each give ~2.5 (Fig. 2L). Preprint methods
  (via WebFetch): factors minimise a distance between the human profile and the time-scaled mouse profile (Akima spline
  interpolation, Nelder-Mead), with errors by bootstrapping over inter-species pairs of replicates; genes with a TPM
  range of 50 or more were used.
- Conclusion on timing: "the relative difference in developmental tempo appears constant throughout the differentiation
  process suggesting a global temporal scaling" (Results). The warp is a pure multiplicative rescaling of real time
  from a shared start; no residual, gene-specific timing differences after the global factor are analysed or reported.
  The phrase "heterochrony on top of allochrony" does not appear.
- Mechanism (most of the paper): Shh signalling dynamics are the same (Fig. 3); a human chromosome 21 in mouse cells
  follows mouse timing (Fig. 4); mRNA half-lives are similar, proteome half-life is ~2-2.5-fold longer in human (Fig. 5);
  a GRN model reproduces the 2.5 scaling when protein decay is halved (Fig. 5E-G); NKX6.1 and OLIG2 proteins ~2-fold more
  stable in human, mKate2 reporter 17.7 h vs 32.9 h, cell cycle 10.8 h vs 28.4 h (Fig. 6).
- Statistics: two-way ANOVA with Tukey on qPCR peaks; Bayesian exponential fits with HDIs for decay; bootstrap for time
  factors. Replicate unit: independent differentiations/libraries (n = 3 per species for RNA-seq).
- Plots: cross-species time-point correlation heat map with the fitted scaling line (Fig. 2I); scaling factor per
  cluster (Fig. 2J); time factors for orthologue-rich cluster pairs (Fig. 2L); per-gene factor distributions (Fig.
  S2F,G); qPCR time courses per species.
- Software: custom; Nextflow pipeline, STAR, RSEM, DP_GP_cluster and PyDREAM appear in the reference list.
- Limitations stated: which specific proteins carry the 2-2.5-fold difference is left open; what sets global tempo is
  unknown.
- Limitations for our design: the warp is a rate on REAL time with a fixed start. On a reconstructed anatomical axis
  there is no clock: if each species' PT axis is scaled to the same range, a uniform stretch is removed by construction,
  and what can remain is a non-uniform warp (different relative S1/S2/S3 extents) or a different covered extent
  (partial overlap).

- d-comparing-trajectories.md:46 (Replicates) - "Rayon: replicate differentiations" - VERDICT: correct (n = 3 per species
  for RNA-seq; bootstrap over replicate pairs per the preprint methods).
- d-comparing-trajectories.md:46 (For us) - "Fit one global warp first, then interpret only gene-specific residual
  shifts ('heterochrony on top of allochrony'). Otherwise a uniform stretch of the human PT axis is misread as many
  genes 'shifting'." - VERDICT: misattributed and partly wrong for our axis. Rayon estimated a global factor and found
  genes and clusters agreed with it; it did not analyse residual gene-specific shifts, and the quoted phrase is not in
  the paper. A "uniform stretch" is a real-time concept; on a PT axis normalised per species it cannot cause shifts.
  Replacement: "Rayon (motor neurons, real time) found one global mouse-to-human rate factor (~2.5) that fits clusters
  and genes alike. For us the lesson is to fit and report a global human-to-mouse axis warp before calling genes
  shifted. On a normalised anatomical axis the warp to fear is non-uniform (different S1/S2/S3 proportions) or partial
  (different covered extent), not a uniform stretch. Precedents for testing genes after a global warp are Bar-Joseph
  2003 (linear warp, then per-gene test), Kanton 2019 (DTW, then a spline test on the aligned axis) and Cardoso-Moreira
  2019 (global stage map, then local deviations)."
- d-comparing-trajectories.md:13 - "timing or warp: ... Rayon's 'allochrony'" - VERDICT: correct (the paper uses
  "developmental allochrony" for a global temporal scaling).
- d-comparing-trajectories.md:101-109 (idea 4, "Global warp first, then gene-specific residual shifts, after Rayon
  2020 with cellAlign/G2G") - VERDICT: misattributed. The method idea is sound but Rayon supplies only step 1 (a

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
Useful as a cross-species precedent that a global warp can explain apparent gene-by-gene timing differences, and for
the correlation-matrix-plus-fitted-line figure. The rate model itself does not transfer: our axis is anatomical and
bounded, so check for non-uniform or partial warps (segment proportions, covered extent; both human sections are
cortex) instead of one scaling factor.
