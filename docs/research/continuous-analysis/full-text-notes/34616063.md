## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Data: MERFISH with a 258-gene panel on coronal slices of mouse primary motor cortex (MOp) and neighbours; about 300,000 cells from 2 male mice for mapping, about 190,000 cells from 2 more mice combined with retrograde CTb tracing (Results; Methods "Animals", "Statistics and reproducibility"). 95 clusters (39 glutamatergic, 42 GABAergic, 14 non-neuronal) by Louvain clustering in Scanpy, matched to companion sc/snRNA-seq clusters with a neural-net classifier.
- Coordinate: soma depth = shortest distance from the cell centroid to the cortical surface line (marked by VLMCs), divided per slice by the median depth of L6b cells, so 0 = surface and 1 = median L6b depth (Methods "Soma depth determination"; Fig. 1c legend). Slices are registered to the Allen CCF v3 by landmark-based deformable/affine transforms to delineate MOp; registration error about 2-2.5% of cortical thickness, and slices with larger errors are dropped (Methods "Registration").
- Layer boundaries on the continuous axis: set from median depths of the most superficial cluster of each layer, with an uncertainty band for the L4/L5 boundary judged from Rspo1 and Fezf2 spatial overlap (Methods "Layer boundary assessment"; Fig. 1c-e).
- Cluster positions: violin plots of each cluster's normalised depth (Fig. 1c-e) and kernel-density curves per cluster (Fig. 2b-e; Fig. 3b); robustness checked in six medial-lateral segments to rule out layer-thickness broadening; most clusters shift modestly, a few are region-specific (ED Figs. 3c, 8c-d).
- Discrete vs continuous: L5 ET, L5/6 NP, L6 CT and L6b look discrete at the subclass level, with "more continuously varying" clusters within each subclass; IT neurons form "a largely continuous gradient" across depth: clusters overlap in depth, PAGA connectivity is highest between spatially adjacent clusters (Fig. 3d), a depth-sorted gene heatmap shows "a largely gradual change ... with steeper changes at the cortical depths that approximately separate cell subclasses" (Fig. 3e), and diffusion pseudotime computed from expression alone (root in L2/3 IT 1) is "highly correlated" with cortical depth (Fig. 3f) (Results "A gradient across the IT neurons"; Discussion).
- Depth-varying genes: z-scored expression in 50 equal depth bins; a gene is called if the mean difference between any two bins exceeds 0.5 (heuristic, no P value); genes ordered by depth of maximum (rolling 10-bin window) (Methods "Connectivity and pseudotime analyses").
- Null / replicates: no statistical test of depth effects; replicate reproducibility shown as mean copy numbers per gene between the two mice (r = 0.99; ED Fig. 1c); cells pooled across mice in depth plots.
- Plot types: spatial cell-type maps; violin plots of depth per cluster with layer lines and an uncertainty band; KDE curves of depth per cluster; PAGA cluster graph; depth-sorted gene heatmap; pseudotime-vs-depth scatter (per cell and per cluster); neighbourhood-complexity histograms; projection enrichment by depth.
- Software: MERlin (Python) https://github.com/ZhuangLab/MERlin; Scanpy (Louvain, PAGA, DPT); Scrublet; data at the Brain Image Library.
- Limitations (authors): CTb labelling under-represents some projections; MOp borders depend on CCF v3. For OUR design: per-slice normalisation, pooled mice, heuristic depth genes, no uncertainty on positions.

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
Good figure templates (per-cluster depth violins/KDEs with boundary lines and an uncertainty band; depth-sorted heatmap), and the clearest published example of placing discrete layer boundaries on a continuous axis. Statistically light: no depth test, pooled mice. The pseudotime-vs-physical-depth check is the analogue of validating our pseudospace against an anatomical reference where one exists.
