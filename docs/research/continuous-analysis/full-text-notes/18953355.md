## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: a curated yeast metabolic network (1,181 reactions, 598 enzymes; 13 currency metabolites removed) is cut into wiring patterns (chains, forks, AND/OR funnels; 12 patterns of pairs and triplets plus longer chains). A "timing activity motif" (TAM) is a consistent order of response onsets on a wiring pattern: forward or backward activation/shutoff, or same-time; 64 TAM types. Onsets come from a per-gene impulse model (6 parameters; onset = time to half of peak) fitted to 76 time courses (5-11 time points each; 63 published, 13 new). An order between two genes is accepted only if robust: 30 refits after adding Gaussian noise (sd 0.1), Wilcoxon p < 0.01 between the two onset distributions; a chain is ordered if the geometric mean of its pairwise p-values is < 0.01; "same time" = onsets within 2 min.
- Null / significance: shuffle expression profiles across enzymes while keeping the network wiring fixed (10,000 randomizations; gamma fit for very small p); all-condition score = sum of log10 p over the 76 conditions. The authors caution that individual motif occurrences can arise by chance given the many wiring motifs tested.
- Results: forward activation ("just-in-time", enzymes induced in pathway order) is the most frequently enriched TAM (10 conditions; heat shock 268 observed vs 56 expected). But order is condition-dependent: forward shutoff (temperature down-shift, nitrogen depletion), BACKWARD activation (shift to non-fermentable carbon: ethanol, galactose), backward shutoff (glycerol pathway under menadione), and same-time activation at funnels and forks. 1,867 of 1,908 three-enzyme-chain TAMs lie in or next to central carbon metabolism; "In contrast to prior work involving E. coli, we did not find similar fine-grained timing motifs in the amino-acid metabolism pathways." Timed enzymes are more metabolically flexible and evolutionarily volatile. Binding activity motifs (ordered TF ChIP-chip affinities along chains) overlap forward-activation TAMs in 20 of 48 TF-condition pairs. Protein (GFP) time courses for 9 enzymes after DTT: protein onsets correlate with mRNA onsets (Spearman r = 0.87); 9 of 11 mRNA pair orders and 6 of 8 chain orders preserved.
- Replicates: none modelled; robustness from noise perturbation; conditions are independent experiments.
- Plot types: motif-count histograms against the randomized null (Fig 2a-b); per-condition motif-type bars (2c); all-condition enrichment scores (2d-e); pathway maps annotated with onset times and activation/repression colours (Fig 3); CCM localization, flexibility and volatility distributions (Fig 4); mRNA vs protein onset plots (Fig 5).
- Software: none named (GEO GSE13219 for the new data).
- Limitations stated: multiple testing over many motifs; mRNA level mixes transcription and degradation; repression may be post-transcriptional.
- Limitations for OUR design: requires a reaction graph with direction (substrate-to-product order); KEGG/Reactome membership alone is not enough. Along PT there is no "time after a stimulus"; "onset" becomes the position of a rise, and the null must shuffle positional profiles among the pathway's enzymes on the fixed graph.

## Review check
- b-continuous-pathways.md:35 (row 18) - "'Activity motifs' (Chechik 2008) and 'just-in-time' transcription (Zaslaver 2004) show that the timing of enzymes follows their order along linear metabolic pathways." - VERDICT: wrong as a generalization for Chechik 2008. Forward order was the most frequent enriched motif, but the paper also found backward activation and backward shutoff (order reversed), condition-dependence, concentration in central carbon metabolism, and no just-in-time motifs in yeast amino-acid pathways. Proposed: "Zaslaver 2004 found that E. coli amino-acid biosynthesis enzymes are activated in pathway order (abstract only). Chechik 2008 generalized this with 'activity motifs' in yeast: forward activation was the most common enriched order, but backward activation and shutoff also occurred, depending on the condition, mostly in central carbon metabolism and not in amino-acid pathways. The test shuffles expression profiles among enzymes on the fixed reaction graph."

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
A clean, portable test for "pathway topology versus position": take PT edge or peak positions for enzymes on a directed reaction graph (e.g. gluconeogenesis, fatty-acid oxidation, ammoniagenesis), count chains ordered forward or backward along PT, and compare with shuffles of positional profiles among the pathway's enzymes on the fixed graph; require the order in each specimen. Expect mixed directions, not a universal forward order.
