## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Data: mCEL-Seq2 scRNA-seq of 10,372 cells from non-diseased resection tissue of 9 donors, with fresh and cryopreserved suspensions and marker-based sorting (CLEC4G+ LSECs, EPCAM+ cells) that differs between donors (Results; ED Fig. 1d,j).
- Zonation coordinate: diffusion pseudotime (destiny, k = 100) on hepatocytes (2,534 cells) and endothelial cells (1,361 cells), "here interpreted as pseudo-space", on the reasoning that "the major axis of variability for a cell type could reflect gene expression changes associated with zonation" (Results "Zonation of human liver cell types"; Methods "Diffusion Pseudo-Time Analysis").
- Per-gene profiles: local regression of normalised counts along DPT order; z-scored profiles clustered by a 1-D self-organising map (200 nodes; neighbouring nodes merged when r > 0.85) into modules (FateID package) (Methods).
- Zonation test: DPT-ordered cells binned into three equal-size bins, one-way ANOVA, Benjamini-Hochberg; 1,384 of 3,395 hepatocyte genes (41%) and 806 of 1,198 endothelial genes (67%) at adjusted P < 0.01; "Increasing the number of bins produced similar results" (Methods; Results). Cells are the units; donors pooled.
- Pathways: hypergeometric Reactome enrichment of SOM modules (ReactomePA; ED Fig. 3h,j).
- Check against mouse: the same DPT + SOM pipeline on Halpern 2017 mouse hepatocytes; DPT profiles binned into 9 bins agree positively with Halpern's landmark-based 9-layer profiles for 1,347 of 1,684 genes (80%), with negative correlations enriched at low expression (ED Fig. 3c-d).
- Human vs mouse: 16,670 ortholog pairs (symbol match plus reciprocal BLAST). Human DPT profiles binned into 9 (hepatocytes) or 4 (endothelial) equal bins and correlated with mouse profiles: "only 68% (60%) of genes with significant zonation patterns exhibited a positive correlation" for hepatocytes (endothelial cells); "Changes were observed across all zones without enrichment of particular pathways"; known zonated genes (ALB, HP, PCK1, CYP2A1) conserved (Supp. Note 3; ED Fig. 3k-l, n = 967 and 977 genes).
- Validation: Human Protein Atlas immunostainings for selected zonated genes (Fig. 2; ED Fig. 3g,i).
- Plot types: diffusion maps coloured by DPT; SOM module heatmaps ordered by pseudo-space; per-gene profile lines with HPA stains (Fig. 2); per-gene correlation histograms (ED Fig. 3c,k,l); correlation vs expression scatter (ED Fig. 3d).
- Software: RaceID3, StemID2, FateID (R), destiny for DPT, ReactomePA/clusterProfiler; GEO GSE124395; web atlas.
- Limitations (authors): zonation validated only by antibody stainings; larger in situ validation needed (Discussion). For OUR design: cell-level ANOVA on three bins; donors pooled with donor-specific sorting; DPT orientation and bin edges differ from the mouse landmark layers; no comparison of the cross-species agreement with a within-species agreement baseline in the same analysis.

## Fit to our design (revised after reading the full text)
Moderate. It is a direct precedent for treating an expression-derived pseudotime as a spatial axis and for module discovery along it, and it is the paper reviewers will cite for "human and mouse zonation differ". Its statistics are pooled and binned, and its cross-species number lacks a within-species reference; our design can supply that reference (mouse A vs mouse B on the same axis and genes) before claiming human-specific shifts.
