## What the paper actually does (from the full text)
- Model / statistic: per gene and lineage, an NB GLM (log link) whose predictors are truncated-power (hinge) basis functions of pseudotime, chosen by the MARS/MARGE procedure: a forward stage picks knots from evenly spaced candidates by a score statistic, a backward stage prunes by a Wald information criterion. Knots near the ends of pseudotime are removed from the candidates, with minimum/maximum spacing rules. A per-cell depth offset (counts-per-10k by default, user-specifiable). Outputs: fitted values with 95% CIs, knot locations, and a slope (multiplicative effect on the log scale) per segment (Fig 1C legend). No covariate term other than the offset is described.
- Three modes: GLM (single subject; MASS glm.nb; LRT vs intercept-only, chi-square). GEE (subjects with shared dynamics; NB GEE via geeM, AR-1 working correlation within subject by default; Wald or score test). GLMM (subjects or groups with differing dynamics; per-subject GLM fits pick knots, pooled basis pruned by NB LASSO with BIC (mpath), final glmmTMB model with a random intercept per subject and per-subject random slopes on every basis function; LRT vs a null with a random intercept per subject; asymptotic chi-square). BH adjustment throughout.
- Null / calibration: asymptotic tests only; no QQ/null-calibration analysis is reported. Knots are selected on the same data that are then tested, and the LRT does not account for this selection (my observation; the paper does not discuss it). The Discussion notes double-dipping from estimating the trajectory on the same genes.
- Replicates: GEE and GLMM modes take a subject ID. Population-level (GEE) and subject-specific (GLMM) estimates. The paper gives no minimum number of subjects; the number of subjects in the simulations is not stated in the main text or Tables S2-S4.
- Validation: 72 single-subject and 715 multi-subject Scaffold simulations (250-5000 cells), scored by balanced accuracy, F-measure, sensitivity, specificity against tradeSeq and PseudotimeDE (GLM mode) and Lamian (GEE, GLMM modes) (Fig 2, S2). Case studies: pancreatic endocrinogenesis (latent time; 3355 DE genes), zebrafish two lineages, fetal-liver monocytes (38,188 cells), B-cell maturation across fetal livers (GEE mode; compared with tradeSeq and Lamian).
- Plots: fitted curves with 95% CI and dashed knot lines (Fig 1C, 3C-F, 6F; S3C), empirical distribution/histogram of knot locations across dynamic genes with per-cell-type latent-time ridgelines (Fig S3D; function getKnotDist), ratio of lineage-specific test statistics vs tradeSeq patternTest (Fig 4E), UpSet plot, coefficient-of-variation boxplots.
- Software: R/Bioconductor scLANE; GitHub jr-leary7/scLANE; web server https://sclane.rc.ufl.edu/.
- Limitations stated: runtime for large data; trajectory uncertainty and double dipping; choice between GEE and GLMM left to design.
- Limitations for OUR design: knot positions have no intervals; hinge fits on log scale give kinks, not smooth sigmoids; end-of-axis knots are excluded by design; the GEE robust variance needs many clusters, which 2 specimens per group cannot supply (general statistics, not from the paper); no specimen fixed-effect option is described.

## Review check
- a-gene-dynamics.md:22 (description) - NB GLM built from MARS hinge functions, knots per gene read as change points, a slope per segment; three modes GLM/GEE/GLMM (random intercept plus per-subject random slopes, via glmmTMB); population-level and subject-specific estimates; plots "Fitted curves with 95% CI and knot lines; distribution of knot locations"; Replicates Yes; R/Bioconductor - VERDICT: correct. The knot-distribution plot is a supplementary figure (Fig S3D).
- a-gene-dynamics.md:22 (For us) - "With only 2 mice per group, random slopes cannot be estimated, so use specimen fixed effects or the GEE mode. Knot positions are point estimates; I saw no interval reported for them." - VERDICT: misses an important point. Knot intervals: correct (none reported). But the GEE fallback is weak: GEE tests rest on a sandwich variance across subjects, which is unreliable with 2 clusters per group (a general statistical fact; the paper gives no guidance on subject numbers). The paper also describes no covariate slot for specimen fixed effects, and it reports no null-calibration check of its asymptotic tests.
  - Replacement: "With 2 mice per group, neither the GLMM's random slopes nor the GEE's robust standard errors can be trusted (the GEE sandwich variance needs many subjects). Specimen fixed effects are not a described scLANE option and would need custom code. Knot positions are point estimates with no interval. The paper reports no null-calibration check of its asymptotic tests."
- a-gene-dynamics.md:80 - scLANE as a source for the change-density track - VERDICT: correct (Fig S3D is a genome-wide histogram of knot positions, the same idea as Trendy's breakpoint density).
- a-gene-dynamics.md:113 - "Lamian and scLANE's GLMM give samples random effects but need many samples ... scLANE needs random slopes per subject" - VERDICT: correct in substance, imprecise. The paper states no minimum sample size; the problem is that the GLMM fits a random intercept and a random slope on every hinge basis per subject.
  - Replacement: "scLANE's GLMM fits a random intercept and random slopes on every hinge basis per subject. The paper states no minimum number of subjects, but these variances cannot be estimated from 2 subjects per group."
- a-gene-dynamics.md:120 - "Trendy's and scLANE's estimated breakpoints, which could be compared against the annotated boundaries" - VERDICT: correct as a proposal (note that candidate knots near the axis ends are excluded, so boundary-adjacent changes near S1 start or S3 end cannot be found).

## Fit to our design (2-4 lines, revised after reading the full text)
The closest packaged model to our NB-with-offset curves that reports "where" (knots) and "how fast" (segment slopes). Run it per specimen in GLM mode and compare knot positions across specimens, rather than relying on its GEE/GLMM modes with 2 specimens per group. Knots lack intervals and are selected on the data being tested, so its p-values need our specimen-level calibration.
