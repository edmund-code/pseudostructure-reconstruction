# Clinical hooks for the Figs. 5–6 highlights (read 2026-11-09)

These are the sources behind the drug and disease sentences in `docs/paper/fig5-6-highlights.md`.

**Provenance.** Pages were read on PMC or the publisher site. For most papers the fetch returned verbatim text through
the Results; the Discussion and Methods came back as a model summary. Re-read the PDF before quoting from those
sections.

## B0AT1 (SLC6A19) inhibition for PKU

**Sources.**
- Wobst et al. 2024, *JCI Insight* 9:e182876, PMID 39513367.
- Phase 1b: NCT05781399.
- Phase 3: NCT06971731. Recruiting; sponsor Otsuka; started 2025-10-20; record updated 2026-10-21.

**Verdict.** Supported, but for one compound only.

**Wording.** "JNT-517, an oral SLC6A19 inhibitor that raises urinary phenylalanine excretion, is in a Phase 3 trial
for PKU."

**Limits.**
- The drug also blocks intestinal SLC6A19, so the effect is not shown to be renal.
- The paper has no tubule-segment data.

## CDK4/6 inhibition in AKI

**Sources.**
- DiRocco et al. 2014, *AJP Renal* 306:F379, PMID 24338822: ischemia–reperfusion.
- Pabla et al. 2015, *PNAS* 112:5231, PMID 25848011: cisplatin.
- Zhou et al. 2024, *Sci Rep* 14:28143, PMID 39548257. A publisher correction exists and was not read.

**Verdict.** Partly supported.

**Wording.** "A single CDK4/6-inhibitor dose before injury reduced early ischemic and cisplatin AKI in male mice."

**Limits.**
- The ischemic benefit was gone by 72 h.
- In the cisplatin model the drugs also inhibit OCT2, which confounds the cell-cycle reading.
- Seven days of dosing raised senescence and fibrosis markers by day 21.
- None of the three papers localises CDK4, cyclin D or E2F to a tubule segment.

## 15-PGDH inhibition in AKI

**Sources.**
- Kim et al. 2020, *AJP Renal* 319:F1054, PMID 33135478: ischemia–reperfusion.
- Miao et al. 2020, *Front Physiol* 11:138, PMID 32231583: LPS.
- Kim et al. 2021, *Ren Fail* 43:168, PMID 33459127: contrast.

**Verdict.** Partly supported.

**Wording.** "The 15-PGDH inhibitor SW033291, given before injury, protected mice in ischemic, septic and contrast
AKI models through PGE2–EP4 signalling."

**Limits.**
- All three papers used one tool compound, and two share senior authors.
- No human trial was verified, so call it preclinical.
- Only Miao 2020 localises the enzyme: cortical and outer-medullary tubules by IHC, with no S1–S3 resolution.

## Urinary angiotensinogen as an AKI marker

**Sources.**
- Alge et al. 2013, *CJASN* 8:184, PMID 23143504: cardiac surgery, n = 97.
- Alge et al. 2013, *Crit Care* 17:R69, PMID 23587112: ICU, n = 45.

**Verdict.** The predictive claim is supported, with modest discrimination: worsening-AKI AUC 0.70–0.71 after cardiac
surgery and 0.77 in the ICU.

**Limits.** Neither paper says the urinary AGT comes from the proximal tubule. Do not attach "made by the PT" to these
citations.

## ZIP8 (SLC39A8) loss in AKI

**Source.** Cai et al. 2025, *PNAS* 122:e2426352122, PMID 40956895. A correction exists (PMC12685135); neither the
correction nor the SI was read.

**Verdict.** Supported.

**Wording.** "Loss of ZIP8 function, by the A391T knock-in or a Six2-Cre renal knockout, lowered renal zinc and
attenuated ischemic AKI at 24 h."

**Limits.**
- The paper places ZIP8 in the proximal tubule but gives no segment resolution.
- It does not show that ZIP8 rises after injury.
